# Audit of the parallel nongeometric research plan

Date: 2026-09-10. Scope: independent review of `PARALLEL_NONGEOMETRIC_20X10_PLAN_20260910.md`, checked against implementation, live result files, and the MrRoPE primary paper. No GPU jobs were launched or changed; no manuscript was edited. This is an audit of a proposed research program, not a replication of its ten experiments.

## Decision

The plan contributes useful missing controls: table-by-gain factorial comparisons, a larger paired binding evaluation, and a separation of shape from compression budget. Its central two-force explanation is a plausible organizing hypothesis, but several claimed confirmations do not follow. Most notably, the E7 result does **not** demonstrate failure of local linearization, gain does **not** restore phase arcs, and increasing a frequency does **not** generally increase periodic positional separation. Correct these before spending GPU time on the proposed separation optimizer.

The strongest current explanation of an actual success is more concrete: changing slot 28 improves both prefix formation and subsequent reading enough to move a competing-answer margin across the greedy decision boundary on one multikey case. This explanation preserves the success without claiming that its direction is already general across tasks or models. The next objective is to predict and reproduce such improvements on unseen examples and frozen cross-model transfers.

## Corrections that change research decisions

### 1. E7 separates numerical realization from local theory

`selection/E7_precision_breakdown.json`, generated by `experiments/nongeometric_screen/local_precision.py`, gives the same frozen-state/local-support output NMSE:

| Calculation | NMSE |
|---|---:|
| Linear prediction | 7.9366844e-8 |
| Exact ideal relative-phase change | 7.9364028e-8 |
| FP32 absolute-coordinate rotations | 7.9325473e-8 |
| BF16 absolute-coordinate realization | 1.2807392e-5 |

The approximately 161-fold squared-error discrepancy occurs in the BF16 realization, while ideal finite change agrees with the linear prediction. It is not evidence that first-order theory underestimated nonlinearity by 160 times. Also, an NMSE ratio is a squared-norm ratio, not a 160-fold amplitude ratio.

Retain three separate questions: (i) whether the local derivative predicts the corresponding ideal intervention; (ii) whether the deployed arithmetic realizes that intervention; (iii) whether the local criterion predicts full-model generation. Here (i) succeeds, (ii) differs materially, and downstream success in (iii) fails. This does not prove numerical error caused the downstream failure. The matched-magnitude BM direction control remains useful; abandoning all gradient/sensitivity tools is unsupported.

### 2. Gain is an independent amplitude variable

The implementation multiplies both rotated Q and K by `gain`, so at fixed states the attention logits are multiplied by `gain²`. Reducing `c` in `gain = 1 + c log S` increases softmax temperature relative to the larger gain; it changes neither the frequency nor the angular span. BM-with-gain results cannot be counted as independent confirmation of an arc-restoration mechanism.

Keep the missing **MrPro × gain 0.074** arm first: together with existing MrPro/BM at gain 0.1 and BM at gain 0.074, it completes the relevant 2×2 table-by-gain factorial on identical inputs. Evaluate gain effects within the same table before reporting their magnitude. Gain 1 is a useful additional endpoint if the factorial leaves a specific question; a full three-gain/three-length grid is not needed to establish the interaction.

### 3. Periodic separation has no universal compression direction

For a content-independent positional feature map with fixed nonnegative slot weights, the squared chord distance is

`D²(Δ; ν) = 4 Σ_j w_j sin²(Δ ν_j / 2)`.

This expression depends on relative displacement Δ, not absolute position p. Thus the proposed extra minimization over `p ≈ 90–128K` has no effect for a static frequency table unless the weights or another part of the statistic explicitly depend on content/position. A slot-to-slot phase spread is also not the same quantity as distinguishability of two token positions.

Compression is not monotone in this metric: a component with `Δν = 2π` has zero separation, while compressing its frequency by one half gives maximal separation. This is not merely a hypothetical issue. CPU calculation from the actual candidate contracts gives:

| Changed slot | Δ=1K | 2K | 4K | 8K | 16K |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| s28_less minus MrPro: change in squared chord distance | +0.1623 | −0.3651 | +0.5320 | −1.5277 | −0.4050 |
| s29_more minus MrPro | −0.1988 | −0.0022 | +0.0087 | +0.0340 | +0.1255 |

Only one slot changes in each table, so these are also the total-distance changes for equal unit weights. In particular, the successful s28 change reduces this separation at three of the plan's five proposed test distances. It may improve a *relevant content-conditioned decision*, but the reported task wins do not identify a global separation increase.

For one fixed attention state the actual score is

`z(q,k;ν) = gain² / sqrt(d) × Σ_j [A_j cos(Δν_j) + B_j sin(Δν_j)]`,

where `A_j` and `B_j` are signed functions of the raw Q/K coordinates. Neither a norm-only weight nor an unweighted positional distance determines whether the correct key beats its distractor. Full-model changes also alter those coordinates through earlier layers and prefix formation. A corrected B5 should predict signed correct-versus-distractor margins on held-out states/examples, with the existing geometry metric as a diagnostic. Fitting a metric to five known outcomes is retrospective calibration, not independent validation or preregistration of those outcomes. Failure of that particular metric would close that formulation, not every possible geometric design.

At 32K, slot 28 already spans about 67.83 radians under MrPro and slot 29 about 51.77 radians; these slots are not in a single small-angle regime. A learned-window argument must distinguish observed phase *distributions* and learned content associations from an interval bound alone. Within the native training length, greater compression is not automatically restoration of an otherwise out-of-range phase.

### 4. Binary task scores can manufacture apparent interaction

The new retrospective prefill/read-table crossovers have order `(Mr/Mr, Mr/E1, E1/Mr, E1/E1)`:

| Case | Scores | Interpretation within this intervention |
|---|---|---|
| s28 multikey 128K row 2 | (0, 0, 0, 1) | Both changes are needed to obtain the observed correct answer, but this alone does not prove strong nonadditive representation interaction |
| s28 multiquery 128K row 3 | (0.75, 1, 0.75, 1) | Changing reading is sufficient to recover the score gain under either prefix |
| s29 VT 128K row 2 | (0.8, 1, 1, 1) | Either intervention is sufficient to recover the score gain |
| s29 QA 32K row 1 | (0, 0, 0, 0) on cached paths | Unusable for attribution of the original gain: the same-table cached E1 path already loses the full-prefill success |

For multikey, the first differing answer digit is directly inspectable at the same generated prefix `[220]`: correct token 21 (`6`) versus wrong token 24 (`9`). The correct-minus-wrong logit margins are **(−2.125, −1.125, −1.000, +0.250)**. Relative to Mr/Mr, the prefix contribution is +1.125, reading contributes +1.000, and the factorial interaction is only +0.250. These nearly additive improvements cross the argmax threshold together. The record therefore supports an explicit answer-margin mechanism more strongly than a qualitative claim of hidden-state synergy. The residual interaction is not exactly zero and is measured using BF16 logits.

For multiquery and VT, cached and full outputs are not always token-identical, although the scores relevant to their gains are preserved. Restrict conclusions accordingly. This crossover changes the prefix state bundle and reading table; it does not uniquely localize an effect to a layer, head, or attention link.

### 5. Two structural proposals need precise definitions

**Row-wise compression:** specify whether a phase is attached to a token, `φ(t)`, or a query rescales every key position using `G_q`. These are different kernels and cache contracts. Under the straightforward `φ(t)=νt/G_t`, `G_t=ceil((t+1)/W)` produces a backward phase-coordinate jump at W (approximately W → W/2) and repeated mapped coordinates. It is not automatically a smooth/native-local mapping. A query-conditioned remapping of every key cannot generally use one fixed pre-rotated K cache with ordinary SDPA. The claimed native-prefix identity also requires native gain and identical execution semantics. In an exactly causal model with identical prefix operators, later tokens cannot change earlier prefix states; a prefix discrepancy is not evidence of a future-state formation effect.

**E9/E10:** the existing E9 relative clock is continuous at its distance boundary; a slope kink is different from a discontinuity or a gap in the frequency ladder. E2's failure does not demonstrate E9 must misbind. Likewise, a poor BM endpoint does not prove that every mixed kernel using BM is poor: two endpoint logits can each favor different wrong keys while their average favors the correct key. This is a mathematical possibility, not a prediction that E10 will win. Keep the actual operator comparison; native as a second clock is an alternative hypothesis, not a consequence already established by BM's aggregate loss.

### 6. Avoid closing families beyond the experiment

- Fifty paired MK rows are useful additional evidence, but “high power” requires a target effect and observed paired-discordance rate. Two development wins are not a reliable effect-size estimate after selection. Keep new-seed and task diversity; MK-only expansion cannot validate s29's QA gain or a general long-context claim.
- Failure of 300–500 searched points cannot upper-bound the entire static table family at +5 pp. Search outcomes would describe the tested domain, objective, algorithm and budget. A separate untouched test set is needed after task-driven search.
- Full slow-band zero frequency retains the content channels. Matching MrPro would indicate no detected positional contribution under that intervention, not that all those dimensions are dead weight; collapse would show dependence on the removed rotations, not uniquely prove arc restoration.
- Good additivity of NLL costs plus a poor task score does not refute per-slot additivity. The two observables differ; even additive answer-logit effects can yield nonadditive accuracy through thresholding, as the multikey case illustrates.
- A few losing global shapes cannot close the entire support-constrained table family. The 2×2 shape/budget experiment can attribute those four specified constructions. Define the finite-grid budget and endpoint constraints exactly; the continuum expressions `(N−1)/3` and `(N−1)/2` require checking against the actual sampled arrays.
- The success percentages and runtime estimates in the plan are planning guesses, not calibrated probabilities or measured timings. They should not decide promotion.

## Minimal useful continuation

1. **Finish the already prepared independent evaluations of s28/s29 and frozen cross-model transfer.** Report each task and length, paired row differences, and which inputs were used in selection. Preserve the two successful old rows as mechanisms to explain. A model-relative slot rule is a generalization hypothesis until target results support it.
2. **Add the one missing MrPro/gain-0.074 arm on the same panel** to resolve whether BM's length tradeoff is driven by gain, table, or their interaction. Reuse existing arms. Add 64K on these fixed candidates if the resulting pattern requires resolving a length transition; do not call the missing length itself a mechanism.
3. **Analyze continuous answer margins in the successful crossovers**, as already possible for multikey. Test the specific prediction that held-out gains occur where the candidate raises the correct answer relative to actual competitors without compensating regressions elsewhere. Keep raw answer generation as the outcome; use margins to explain, not replace it.
4. **Retain shape × budget as the strongest distinct construction experiment**, after defining its exact finite-slot constraints. A slot-28/29 combination is also a small targeted interaction test if their independent evidence survives; it is not guaranteed to combine gains because the current short gain comes from one fragile QA example.
5. **Rewrite B5 and the row-wise operator before implementation.** B5 needs a score tied to signed content-dependent competition and a prediction on unseen data. The dynamic proposal needs an explicit phase map, cache semantics, and continuous/step mapping counterexample check. Broad search can wait until these results tell us what structure or uncertainty it would resolve.

The useful unifying framework is therefore currently **phase-dependent, content-conditioned score changes + altered prefix states + amplitude/numerical effects + a task decision boundary**. These are computationally defined components with separating interventions. A simpler two-force rule may emerge from them, but has not yet been demonstrated.

## Prior-art boundary

The plan's claim that “training-free + static + nongeometric frequencies” is unoccupied is too broad. MrRoPE-Pro itself uses a progressive per-dimension radix schedule, whose cumulative middle-band exponent is nonlinear and whose frequencies are not one global geometric progression; YaRN also applies a piecewise schedule. MrRoPE explicitly formulates independent per-dimension scaling. A useful novelty claim must specify the new allocation rule, content/checkpoint dependence, permitted nonmonotonicity, or predictive theory beyond these existing constructions. The primary evidence is [MrRoPE, Sections 3.1–3.2, equations 10 and 14–16](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22181v1#S3), checked directly for this audit. This is a correction of the stated novelty boundary, not a complete new literature survey.
