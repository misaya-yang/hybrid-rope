\section{The Allocation Coordinate}
\label{sec:exponents}
\label{sec:identification}

Let $K=\drot/2\ge2$ be the number of rotary pairs. Standard RoPE assigns
\begin{equation}
\omega_k=b^{-\phi_k},\qquad \phi_k=\frac{k}{K},\qquad k=0,\ldots,K-1,
\label{eq:exponent-rope}
\end{equation}
with base $b>1$. To separate range from placement, write any strictly ordered positive table $\omega_0>\cdots>\omega_{K-1}>0$ as
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,\qquad
z_k=\frac{x_k-x_0}{x_{K-1}-x_0},\qquad z_0=0,\ z_{K-1}=1.
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Here $(a,R)$ fixes the sampled log-frequency interval, and the $K-2$ interior values of $z$ allocate the pairs within it. Every geometric table has $z_k=k/(K-1)$, irrespective of base. A base change therefore moves the sampled range while retaining uniform normalized spacing. Changing $z$ at fixed $(a,R,K)$ redistributes the interior frequencies.

This ordered table is installed in labeled Q/K coordinate blocks. A permutation $\pi$ of the same frequency multiset changes its \emph{slot assignment}; learned projections determine how each assigned frequency contributes to attention. We use these distinctions to design separate interventions on allocation, runtime range, and assignment.

Continuous densities give a convenient way to construct allocations through quantiles. The installed tensor also depends on the finite grid: midpoint nodes, inclusive endpoints, and affine endpoint anchoring yield different tables. Our identification experiments anchor quantiles to the baseline's actual sampled endpoints. Model applications state their grid convention alongside the comparison. This makes the mathematical coordinate in~\eqref{eq:table-decomposition} directly usable as an experimental control.
