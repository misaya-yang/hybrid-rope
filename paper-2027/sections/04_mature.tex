\section{Adjusting Exponents in Frozen Models}
\label{sec:mature-adjustments}
\label{sec:exp-frozen-support}
\label{sec:exp-zero-training-system}
\label{sec:coordinate}

The crossings in \S\ref{sec:coadaptation-finding} show that trained weights are co-adapted to their exponent basis, motivating adjustments defined relative to native exponents.

\subsection{A common coordinate for exponent adjustments}

For native frequencies $\omega_k^N$ and installed frequencies $\omega'_k$, define
\begin{equation}
d_k=\log\frac{\omega_k^N}{\omega'_k},\qquad
\omega'_k=\omega_k^N e^{-d_k},\qquad
\phi'_k-\phi_k^N=\frac{d_k}{\log b}.
\label{eq:movement-allocation}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
d_k=
\begin{cases}
-\log(1-w_k+w_k/s), & \text{frequency blend},\\
m_k\log s, & \text{log-frequency shift},\\
\sum_{j<k}\log\lambda_j, & \text{mixed-radix conversion}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:adjustment-families}
\end{equation}
Static tables are installed before prefill and retained through decoding, with a single amplitude $g$ scaling both the cosine and sine tables, so it enters the attention logits as $g^2$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:adjustment-details}). Throughout, $g$ takes the form $1+c\ln s$; the two coefficients that appear, $c=0.1$ (YaRN's default) and $c=0.074$ (the low-gain arm), are settings of one parameter rather than two conventions, and no result folds a temperature into the frequency table.

\subsection{Fixed-range and single-table comparisons}

\paragraph{Frozen fixed-support control.}
On frozen OLMo-$2$-$1$B and Qwen-$2.5$-$1.5$B we hold endpoints, $64$ pairs, attention amplitude, inputs, and decoder fixed. Both structured profiles produce large gains over uniform exponents (OLMo $16$K RULER: $0.56\%$ to $61.04\%$), with coarse controls capturing the bulk of the effect (Table~\ref{tab:frozen-exponent-main}; Appendix~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support}).



\paragraph{One static configuration across lengths.}
A normalized-index profile transfers a reference displacement curve onto the target grid ($\omega'_k=\omega_k^N s^{-m_k}$). On Qwen-$2.5$-$0.5$B, the same factor-two table is evaluated at $32$K and $64$K across full RULER-13 ($20$ rows per task). At the reported fixed amplitudes it improves $64$K macro over YaRN by $6.09$ points ($95\%$ bootstrap interval $[2.76, 9.58]$), while $32$K scores are near-equal (Table~\ref{tab:static-index-main}); the gain is attributed to the complete static configuration (table $\times$ amplitude; Appendix~\ref{sec:index-adjustment}).



\paragraph{Finite-grid placement.}
Comparing normalized-index interpolation against direct-gap evaluation on Gemma-$1.1$-$2$B ($128$ pairs; \citealt{gemmateam2024gemma}) yields $79.00\%$ versus $72.81\%$ at $16$K ($+6.19$ points, $[2.81, 9.63]$), making placement an important implementation choice (Appendix~\ref{sec:coordinate-confirmation}).

\subsection{Redistributing the intermediate-band shift}
\label{sec:bm-main}

MrRoPE-Pro progressively distributes the cumulative exponent shift across an intermediate band $k\in[l,h]$ with $N=h-l$ and $q=\operatorname{clip}(k-l,0,N)$:
\begin{equation}
m_q^{\rm Pro}=\frac{q(q+1)}{N(N+1)},
\qquad
m_q^{\rm BM}=\frac{q(q+1)(3N+2-2q)}{N(N+1)(N+2)}.
\label{eq:bm-formula}
\end{equation}
Boundary matching (BM) minimizes quadratic difference energy, smoothing radix transitions toward unmodified outer bands (Appendix~\ref{sec:bm-construction}).



In the OLMo-$1$B confirmation, BM reaches $51.32\%$ at $16$K against $2.78\%$ for MrRoPE-Pro (Table~\ref{tab:bm-models}), and across $631$ untruncated LongBench prompts exceeding $4$K tokens over five tasks it raises task-equal complete-output token F1 from $21.62\%$ to $25.44\%$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:bm-natural-qa}; $95\%$ bootstrap interval $[1.32, 6.29]$, all task differences positive).



However, preferences differ across models (Table~\ref{tab:bm-models}): while OLMo favors BM, the two Qwen arms favor MrRoPE-Pro at $128$K. Those two comparisons are exploratory: $24$ and $12$ long-prompt rows, and an archived $36$-row tally of $6$ wins to $4$ losses at a paired standard error near $13$ points that does not separate the profiles. We read the contrast as direction only. With endpoints and total scale fixed, intermediate profile preferences are checkpoint- and length-dependent (Appendix~\ref{sec:bm-model-comparison}).
