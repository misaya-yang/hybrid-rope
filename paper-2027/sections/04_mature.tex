\subsection{TailSpline: allocating extension around a native table}
\label{sec:mature-adjustments}\label{sec:exp-zero-training-system}\label{sec:coordinate}\label{sec:bm-main}
In Eq.~\eqref{eq:movement-allocation}, $m_k=0$ preserves native-distance
responses, whereas $m_k=1$ restores them at $s$ times the distance
(Fig.~\ref{fig:intro-allocation}). Longer inputs can contain both unchanged
local dependencies and stretched query--evidence separations, motivating
different amounts of scaling across channels \citep{wu2026datashapes}.

We retain the high-frequency band and divide the low-frequency tail by $s$,
then design the intervening allocation. Following MrRoPE's $32/1$-turn
boundaries \citep{tian2026mrrope}, let $l$ be the last pair above $32$ native
turns and $h$ the first below one. Put $n=h-l\ge1$ and
$q=\operatorname{clip}(k-l,0,n)$. The transition accumulates
$\epsilon_q=m_q-m_{q-1}$, with $m_0=0$, $m_n=1$.

The increments have a direct spectral meaning. For a geometric native grid with gap $c=\log b/K$,
\begin{equation}
x'_{l+q}-x'_{l+q-1}=c+\epsilon_q\log s.
\label{eq:increment-log-gap}
\end{equation}
Their unit sum distributes exactly $\log s$ of extra span across the band.
At the tail, constant displacement $m=1$ preserves native adjacent gaps,
so the extra gap returns to zero.

MrRoPE-Pro uses increasing increments, $m_q=q(q+1)/[n(n+1)]$.
We formulate a discrete tail-connection problem by penalizing variation
in the extra log gaps and their mismatch with the fully interpolated tail:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\epsilon:\,\sum_{q=1}^n\epsilon_q=1}
\sum_{q=1}^{n-1}(\epsilon_{q+1}-\epsilon_q)^2+\epsilon_n^2.
\label{eq:tailspline-objective}
\end{equation}
This one-sided minimum-bending objective smooths the interior log gaps and
their connection to the stretched-distance reference. The high-frequency
band stays fixed, with its entry gap free to accommodate the required span.
\begin{theorem}[Finite-grid TailSpline]\label{thm:tailspline}
The unique minimizer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline-objective} has positive increments and cumulative profile
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_q=\frac{3(n+q)(n-q+1)}{n(n+1)(2n+1)},\qquad
m_q^{\rm TS}=\frac{q(3n^2+3n+1-q^2)}{n(n+1)(2n+1)}.
\label{eq:tailspline}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The solution assigns more extension throughout the transition:
$m_q^{\rm TS}\ge m_q^{\rm Pro}$, with a larger entry gap and a smaller
terminal gap (Fig.~\ref{fig:construction-contrast}). The gap reduction has
a direct phase consequence. At the last interior pair, for $n\ge2$, $s>1$
and any nonzero separation, residual phases relative to $\omega^N/s$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\frac{\delta_{\rm TS}}{\delta_{\rm Pro}}
=\frac{s^{\epsilon_n^{\rm TS}}-1}{s^{\epsilon_n^{\rm Pro}}-1}
\le\frac{3}{2n+1}.
\label{eq:tail-phase-ratio}
\end{equation}
Here $\delta_M=|\Delta|\,|\omega_{h-1}^{M}-\omega_{h-1}^N/s|$.
The Llama $s=4$ ratio is $0.080$. More generally, Appendix~\ref{sec:allocation-reference-intervals}
relates phase mismatch to dependency stretch and gives a finite content-response
bound. These properties explain the distance preference encoded by the construction.

Strict convexity gives the closed form (Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details}).
Penalizing both boundaries instead,
by adding $\epsilon_1^2$, yields the symmetric construction BM with increments
proportional to $q(n-q+1)$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:bm-construction}).

\paragraph{What the comparison isolates.}
TailSpline and MrPro retain the same outer bands and endpoints, so their
comparison tests complete internal allocations. The control $C$ additionally
matches total log-frequency displacement to test the remaining shape difference
(Appendices~\ref{sec:tailspline-dose-control} and~\ref{sec:clean-shape-new}).

\paragraph{Installation.}
(1) Read the public native grid, length and scale; (2) locate the $32/1$-turn band; (3) compute $m_k$, $\omega'_k=\omega_k^Ns^{-m_k}$ and shared gain $g=1+0.1\ln s$; (4) install before prefill across all layers and lengths. Construction costs $O(K)$, retains standard rotary computation and uses no checkpoint observations.
