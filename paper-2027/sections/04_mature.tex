\subsection{TailSpline: allocating extension around a native table}
\label{sec:mature-adjustments}\label{sec:exp-zero-training-system}\label{sec:coordinate}\label{sec:bm-main}
A frozen model already uses its native frequencies. We retain its high-frequency band and divide its low-frequency tail by $s$, while designing the intervening allocation. Following the $32/1$-turn boundaries used by MrRoPE \citep{tian2026mrrope}, let $l$ be the last pair making more than $32$ turns over the native window and $h$ the first making less than one. For a nonempty transition, put $n=h-l\ge1$ and $q=\operatorname{clip}(k-l,0,n)$. The transition accumulates increments $\epsilon_q=m_q-m_{q-1}$, with $m_0=0$, $m_n=1$.

The increments have a direct spectral meaning. For a geometric native grid with gap $c=\log b/K$,
\begin{equation}
x'_{l+q}-x'_{l+q-1}=c+\epsilon_q\log s.
\label{eq:increment-log-gap}
\end{equation}
Their unit sum distributes exactly $\log s$ of extra span across the band.
The outer bands preserve two distance references: high frequencies retain
$R_{\omega^N}(d)$, while the low-frequency tail satisfies
$R_{\omega^N/s}(sd)=R_{\omega^N}(d)$. At the tail, constant displacement
$m=1$ preserves native adjacent gaps, so the extra gap returns to zero.

MrRoPE-Pro uses increasing increments, $m_q=q(q+1)/[n(n+1)]$.
We formulate a discrete tail-connection problem by penalizing variation
in the extra log gaps and their mismatch with the fully interpolated tail:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\epsilon:\,\sum_{q=1}^n\epsilon_q=1}
\sum_{q=1}^{n-1}(\epsilon_{q+1}-\epsilon_q)^2+\epsilon_n^2.
\label{eq:tailspline-objective}
\end{equation}
The first term smooths neighboring extra gaps; the second closes the transition at the zero-extra-gap boundary supplied by the fully interpolated tail. The high-frequency outer band is fixed by construction. This boundary prior prioritizes a gradual handoff to the fully interpolated distance reference, while allowing a larger entry gap.
\begin{theorem}[Finite-grid TailSpline]\label{thm:tailspline}
The unique minimizer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline-objective} has positive increments and cumulative profile
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_q=\frac{3(n+q)(n-q+1)}{n(n+1)(2n+1)},\qquad
m_q^{\rm TS}=\frac{q(3n^2+3n+1-q^2)}{n(n+1)(2n+1)}.
\label{eq:tailspline}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The solution shifts more extension into the transition: $m_q^{\rm TS}\ge
m_q^{\rm Pro}$ at every interior pair. Relative to MrPro, it accepts a larger
entry jump and reduces the terminal jump by the exact factor $3/(2n+1)$:
$0.086$ for Llama's $n=17$ and $0.081$ for OLMo's $n=18$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:construction-contrast}). On the Llama $s=4$ grid, the largest
TailSpline/MrPro wavelength ratio is $1.766$.
Appendix~\ref{sec:allocation-scale-response} derives this ordering and
compares the response to scale with YaRN's frequency blend.

Strict convexity and the tridiagonal stationarity system give the closed form
(Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details}). Penalizing both boundaries instead,
by adding $\epsilon_1^2$, yields the symmetric construction BM with increments
proportional to $q(n-q+1)$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:bm-construction}).

\paragraph{What the comparison isolates.}
TailSpline and MrPro retain the same outer bands and endpoints, so their
comparison tests complete internal allocations. The control $C$ additionally
matches total log-frequency displacement to test the remaining shape difference
(Appendices~\ref{sec:tailspline-dose-control} and~\ref{sec:clean-shape-new}).

\paragraph{Installation.}
(1) Read the public native grid, length and scale; (2) locate the $32/1$-turn band; (3) compute $m_k$, $\omega'_k=\omega_k^Ns^{-m_k}$ and shared gain $g=1+0.1\ln s$; (4) install before prefill across all layers and lengths. Construction costs $O(K)$, retains standard rotary computation and uses no checkpoint observations.
