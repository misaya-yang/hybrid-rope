\subsection{TailSpline: allocating extension around a native table}
\label{sec:mature-adjustments}\label{sec:exp-zero-training-system}\label{sec:coordinate}\label{sec:bm-main}
A frozen model already uses its native frequencies. We retain its high-frequency band and divide its low-frequency tail by $s$, while designing the intervening allocation. Following the $32/1$-turn boundaries used by MrRoPE \citep{tian2026mrrope}, let $l$ be the last pair making more than $32$ turns over the native window and $h$ the first making less than one. For a nonempty transition, put $n=h-l\ge1$ and $q=\operatorname{clip}(k-l,0,n)$. The transition accumulates increments $\epsilon_q=m_q-m_{q-1}$, with $m_0=0$, $m_n=1$.

The increments have a direct spectral meaning. For a geometric native grid with gap $c=\log b/K$,
\begin{equation}
x'_{l+q}-x'_{l+q-1}=c+\epsilon_q\log s.
\label{eq:increment-log-gap}
\end{equation}
Their unit sum allocates exactly $\log s$ of extra span across the band. Beyond it, constant displacement $m=1$ preserves native adjacent gaps: the extra gap returns to zero. The remaining choice is how to reach this junction.

MrRoPE-Pro uses increasing increments, $m_q=q(q+1)/[n(n+1)]$. We instead smooth the transition into the fully interpolated tail, whose subsequent increments are zero:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\epsilon:\,\sum_{q=1}^n\epsilon_q=1}
\sum_{q=1}^{n-1}(\epsilon_{q+1}-\epsilon_q)^2+\epsilon_n^2.
\label{eq:tailspline-objective}
\end{equation}
The first term smooths neighboring extra gaps; the second closes the transition at the zero-extra-gap boundary supplied by the fully interpolated tail. The high-frequency outer band is fixed by construction. Leaving the entry unpenalized allows a larger first extra gap while prioritizing the smooth tail connection.
\begin{theorem}[Finite-grid TailSpline]\label{thm:tailspline}
The unique minimizer of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline-objective} has positive increments and cumulative profile
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_q=\frac{3(n+q)(n-q+1)}{n(n+1)(2n+1)},\qquad
m_q^{\rm TS}=\frac{q(3n^2+3n+1-q^2)}{n(n+1)(2n+1)}.
\label{eq:tailspline}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
Strict convexity and the tridiagonal stationarity system give Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline} (Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details}). Adding the entry penalty $\epsilon_1^2$ yields the symmetric construction BM, with increments proportional to $q(n-q+1)$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:bm-construction}). Relative to MrPro, TailSpline accepts a larger entry jump and reduces the terminal jump by the exact factor $3/(2n+1)$: $0.086$ for Llama's $n=17$ and $0.081$ for OLMo's $n=18$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:construction-contrast}a). These guarantees solve the declared objectives; task value is tested below.

\paragraph{What the comparison isolates.}
TailSpline and MrPro retain the same outer bands and endpoints while changing
both total displacement and transition shape. An additional control $C$ matches
TailSpline's total displacement to test the remaining shape difference
(Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-dose-control}). The completed classic diagnostic
gives T--C $-0.41$ points in Full-13 AUC, with interval $[-2.63,1.82]$;
T uses batch $1$ and C batch $2$, leaving this finer ordering unresolved
(Appendix~\ref{sec:current-controls}).

\paragraph{Installation.}
(1) Read the public native grid, reference length and scale $s$; (2) compute the $32/1$-turn band; (3) evaluate $m_k$, $\omega'_k=\omega_k^Ns^{-m_k}$ and $g=1+0.1\ln s$; (4) install before prefill, retaining one table across layers and input lengths. The baseline shares $g$. Construction costs $O(K)$ and leaves the rotary computation unchanged. Neither constructor reads weights, activations or calibration outputs.
