\section{Identifying the Allocation Axis}
\label{sec:identification}

\subsection{Fixed-support training intervention}
\label{sec:exp-identify}

\paragraph{Q1: Does $z$ change trained behaviour at fixed $(a,R)$?}
Within each of three $151.9$M training seeds, both arms share architecture,
initialisation, token order, optimiser, LR schedule, global batch, a
$499{,}974{,}144$-token budget, and $32$ evaluation anchors. The baseline is
FMRoPE at fixed training support, implementing \citet[\S6.1]{oka2026fmrope}
with $\theta{=}L_{\mathrm{train}}{=}256$ and geometric exponents. Anchored
\evq{} ($\tau{=}4$) normalises its quantiles to the same extrema and log-span,
so only the $K{-}2=30$ interior frequencies move while retaining the deployed
Cosh table's $z$.
Every training seed favours anchored \evq{} at every OOD length. The anchored
\evq{} minus FMRoPE NLL difference is $+0.026$ at the $256$-token training
length and $-0.281/-0.176/-0.146$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K
(Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral-budget-overview}c). No geometric base can reproduce this
intervention once the sampled support is pinned: \emph{fixed support $+$
different $z$ $\Rightarrow$ different trained-model behaviour.}

\paragraph{Q2: Does this beat target-aware FMRoPE?}
No. Under target-matched support, anchored \evq{} trails FMRoPE by
$+0.060/+0.227/+0.460$ NLL at $2\times$/$4\times$/$8\times$, with FMRoPE
favoured in all three seeds---$0/3$ for anchored \evq{}
(App.~Fig.~\ref{fig:exact-range-control}). The
fixed-support result therefore identifies allocation $z$; it does not establish
an additive gain over target-aware support selection.

\paragraph{Q3: Does support selection exhaust the design?}
No. In a separate frozen-checkpoint protocol, support, attention amplitude,
checkpoint, data, and decoder are fixed while only interior $z$ changes;
same-support geometric, derived, and coarse-ramp allocations score
$0.56\%/60.47\%/61.04\%$ on released-model nine-task $16$K RULER (20 rows/task;
App.~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support}). This is
neither the $151.9$M training protocol nor a test of the Cosh table.

\subsection{Cross-configuration and shape breadth}

lengths, three head dimensions, three paired seeds, scaled Cosh schedules, and
a deformation-matched exponential; it supports the allocation axis without
identifying a unique shape or strength (App.~Table~\ref{tab:m4}).
