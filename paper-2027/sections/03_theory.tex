\section{Positional Geometry of Exponent Distributions}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Full-pair geometry and effective rank identity}
\label{sec:subspaces}
\label{sec:budget}

For a fixed content pair, a rotary channel contributes $C\cos(\omega\Delta)+D\sin(\omega\Delta)$ to an attention logit, where $\Delta$ is relative token separation. Its positional object is therefore the two-dimensional function space
\begin{equation}
V_\omega=\operatorname{span}\{\cos(\omega\Delta),\sin(\omega\Delta)\}.
\label{eq:subspace}
\end{equation}
Let $x_\omega(\Delta)=[\cos(\omega\Delta),\sin(\omega\Delta)]$, and define self- and cross-Gram matrices $S_\omega=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\omega]$ and $H_{\omega\nu}=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\nu]$ under a declared separation measure. For nonsingular $S_\omega,S_\nu$, the singular values of whitened cross-Gram $Q_{\omega\nu}=S_\omega^{-1/2}H_{\omega\nu}S_\nu^{-1/2}$ are the canonical correlations of the two spaces. Their average squared value,
\begin{equation}
c_{\omega\nu}=\tfrac12\|Q_{\omega\nu}\|_F^2\in[0,1],
\label{eq:collision}
\end{equation}
is invariant under invertible basis changes within either pair, measuring how much of the positional basis the frequencies share. Stacking all block-whitened pairs into $\Gamma$ (with identity diagonal blocks), if $\bar c$ is the mean pairwise squared canonical overlap, its R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank satisfies
\begin{equation}
r_2(\Gamma)=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Gamma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)}=\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\label{eq:budget-identity}
\end{equation}
This is an exact identity for the specified positional design matrix, not a downstream model accuracy bound.

\subsection{Shared slow-frequency limit}
\label{sec:slow-limit}

\begin{proposition}[Shared slow-frequency subspace]
\label{prop:collapse}
Under uniform measure on $[0,L]$, let $x=\omega L$. As $x\to0$, $\cos(xt)\to 1$ and $\sin(xt)/x\to t$, so $V_\omega$ converges to $\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$. For frequencies with $\epsilon=\max\{|\omega L|,|\nu L|\}\to0$, squared chordal separation satisfies $2-\|Q_{\omega\nu}\|_F^2=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^4)$.
\end{proposition}

In standard grids ($b=500{,}000, K=64, L=4096$), the $23$ pairs with $\omega L\le1$ supply $46$ coordinate dimensions but a block-whitened rank $r_2=2.00013$. In raw unwhitened coordinates, the weak eigenvalue scales as $\lambda_-(S_\omega)\approx (\omega L)^2/12$. This motivates studying how tables distribute positional directions, though static rank does not guarantee downstream extrapolation.

\subsection{What a schedule-only summary can see}
\label{sec:non-identifiability}



The separation between basis geometry and downstream performance is formal:

\begin{theorem}[Static surrogates cannot rank allocations]
\label{thm:non-identifiability}
Let $\Phi(\Omega)$ depend only on the frequency schedule and let $\mathcal{R}$ be measured downstream capability.
\emph{(i) Permutation-invariant $\Phi$ cannot separate the two most behaviourally distant tables we can construct.} For any non-trivial permutation $\pi$, invariance gives $\Phi(\pi\Omega)=\Phi(\Omega)$, while by Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} $\Omega$ and $\pi\Omega$ admit \emph{no} position-independent invertible reparameterization. On the evaluated checkpoints the measured risk gap is large: $1\times$ tail NLL rises from $3.10423$ to $6.86493$ and $64$K macro accuracy falls from $0.7000$ to $0.0000$. So no such $\Phi$ is a standalone predictor of $\mathcal{R}$.
\emph{(ii) The remaining schedule-only surrogates fail on named witnesses.} A discrete scale-orbit count shifts by $\Delta q=58$ under $1$-ULP jitter with $\Delta\mathrm{NLL}=+10^{-5}$; and curvature ill-conditioning ($\kappa(F_N)\gg10^4$) concentrates sensitivity at knee slot $19$, so an $\mathrm{MAE}=0.0012$ coordinate error fails pre-registered retention gates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{remark}
Part (i) is structural, not anecdotal, and this is the point of coupling it to Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction}: the schedule's only position-independent equivalence is permutation, and permutation is exactly the transformation the trained weights do not survive. A summary $\Phi$ must therefore be invariant to something; the only invariance the schedule offers is the fatal one. We do \emph{not} claim non-identifiability for every schedule-dependent $\Phi$---$\Phi(\Omega)=\Omega$ is injective---which is precisely why the useful question is not ``which metric is best'' but ``what can a schedule-only summary see at all''.
\end{remark}

\subsection{An analytic allocation construction}
\label{sec:construction}

To allocate samples over normalized exponent space $[0,1]$, let $S_\rho(t)=\int_t^1\rho(\phi)\,d\phi$, and consider the convex design criterion
\begin{equation}
\Capp[\rho]=\frac\alpha2\int_0^1\rho(\phi)^2\,d\phi+\frac\beta2\int_0^1S_\rho(t)^2\,dt,
\qquad \text{s.t. } \int_0^1\rho=1,\ \rho\ge0.
\label{eq:Capp}
\end{equation}
The first term penalizes concentration; the second penalizes cumulative mass beyond slow-end thresholds. These are explicit design preferences; we do not identify this functional with complete trained-model loss.

\begin{theorem}[Cosh allocation]
\label{thm:ode}
For $\alpha>0$ and $\beta\ge0$, the unique minimizer of \eqref{eq:Capp} over positive $\rho\in C^2([0,1])$ with unit integral is
\begin{equation}
\rho_\tau(\phi)=\frac{\tau\cosh[\tau(1-\phi)]}{\sinh\tau},
\qquad \tau=\sqrt{\beta/\alpha},
\label{eq:rho-tau}
\end{equation}
recovering uniform density as $\tau\to0$. Inverting the CDF yields the explicit quantile map
\begin{equation}
\phi_k(\tau)=1-\frac{1}{\tau}\operatorname{arcsinh}\!\left((1-u_k)\sinh\tau\right),
\qquad u_k=\frac{k+1/2}{K}.
\label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
We call this construction \evq{}. It is an analytic allocation family, not a claim of universal optimality. Once $\tau$, grid, and endpoint anchoring are specified, installation evaluates the table once without changing the rotary operator or trainable parameter count.
