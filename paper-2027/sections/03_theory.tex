\section{Positional Structure and Content Use}
\label{sec:theory}

The controlled gains raise a structural question: what changes when the
frequency range stays fixed? Complete rotary pairs supply positional directions
whose finite-window overlap connects frequency spacing to redundancy. The
model learns how to combine these directions with content.

\subsection{Complete rotary pairs and positional overlap}
\label{sec:subspaces}
\label{sec:budget}

For a fixed content pair, one rotary channel contributes $C\cos(\omega\Delta)+D\sin(\omega\Delta)$ to an attention logit, where $\Delta$ is relative token separation. Both coefficients depend on content; the positional object is
\begin{equation}
V_\omega=\operatorname{span}\{\cos(\omega\Delta),\sin(\omega\Delta)\}.
\label{eq:subspace}
\end{equation}
To avoid privileging a content phase, let $x_\omega(\Delta)=[\cos(\omega\Delta),\sin(\omega\Delta)]$ and define $S_\omega=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\omega]$, $H_{\omega\nu}=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\nu]$ under a declared separation measure. For nonsingular self-Grams, the singular values of $Q_{\omega\nu}=S_\omega^{-1/2}H_{\omega\nu}S_\nu^{-1/2}$ are the canonical correlations, or cosines of principal angles, of the two spaces \citep{bjorck1973angles}. Their mean square,
\begin{equation}
c_{\omega\nu}=\tfrac12\|Q_{\omega\nu}\|_F^2\in[0,1],
\label{eq:collision}
\end{equation}
measures shared positional directions and is invariant to invertible basis changes within either pair. Let $\Gamma$ be the Gram matrix of the concatenated block-whitened pairs, with diagonal blocks $I_2$. The average pairwise overlap $\bar c$ gives the exact R\'enyi-$2$ effective-rank identity
\begin{equation}
r_2(\Gamma)=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Gamma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)}
=\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\label{eq:budget-identity}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)$ is the frame potential of the normalized positional functions \citep{benedetto2003frames}. The identity specializes this classical measure to full rotary pairs: increasing pairwise overlap concentrates the same $2K$ coordinates into fewer effective positional directions. These are normalized directions in the supplied frequency basis; content-dependent coefficients determine their contribution to attention logits.

\subsection{Finite-window concentration of slow directions}
\label{sec:slow-limit}

\begin{proposition}[Shared slow-frequency subspace]
\label{prop:collapse}
Under uniform measure on $[0,L]$, with $t=\Delta/L$, as $x=\omega L\to0$, $\cos(xt)\to1$ and $\sin(xt)/x\to t$, so $V_\omega$ converges to $\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$. For $\epsilon=\max\{|\omega L|,|\nu L|\}\to0$, squared chordal separation satisfies $2-\|Q_{\omega\nu}\|_F^2=\mathcal O(\epsilon^4)$.
\end{proposition}

Distinct slow frequencies therefore approach a shared two-dimensional
positional space. On the identification grid $b=256$, $K=32$, $L=256$,
the eight slowest pairs span $\omega L\in[1.19,4]$ and have effective rank
$r_2=2.11$. Their overlap is substantial even beyond the small-phase limit
(Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry-main}; Appendix~\ref{sec:finite-window-rank}).

\paragraph{Why use complete rotary pairs?}
A cosine-only overlap proxy can reverse the allocation ordering: explicit
four-pair tables with identical endpoints give $C_{\cos}(A)=0<C_{\cos}(B)$,
yet $r_2(A)=6.29<8.00\approx r_2(B)$ because sine--cosine cross terms also
carry positional overlap (Appendix~\ref{sec:static-counterexamples}).
The complete-pair measure captures this structure independently of phase.

\subsection{Shared positional dependence and content use}
Each rotary block remains invertible even when its positional functions
overlap. Shared dependence on distance can therefore coexist with distinct
content comparisons. A slow-block construction makes this distinction
explicit (Appendix~\ref{sec:content-coordinate-retention}), complementing
prior observations of positional and semantic frequency use
\citep{barbero2025round}.

The complete frozen Llama tables provide a concrete example. At $16/32$K,
TailSpline has full-pair effective ranks $8.28/10.08$, compared with
MrPro's $8.74/10.21$ under uniform separations, while improving clean
RULER by $3.39/11.72$ points (Table~\ref{tab:clean-length-main}).
These gains establish the value of controlling distance dependence through
allocation even when normalized positional diversity decreases. The model's
use of the supplied frequencies determines their practical value
(Appendix~\ref{sec:allocation-rank-quality}).
Concretely, changing a frequency changes the distance response multiplying
its content coefficients. A useful response improves target evidence relative
to competing keys: a larger target logit alone does not determine its attention
share. Appendix~\ref{sec:content-competition} makes this comparison exact for
fixed pre-RoPE states, linking positional structure to content competition.

\paragraph{Frequency changes and coordinate assignment.}
Changing frequencies also differs from reassigning a fixed spectrum.
In the no-alias interval $(0,\pi)$, position-independent invertible Q/K
maps preserve the full integer-position rotary kernel exactly if and only
if the frequency multisets agree (Appendix~\ref{sec:discrete-kernel-proof}).
This criterion complements the coordinate interventions and STRING's
structural characterization \citep{schenck2025string}.
These distinctions motivate treating the native table as a design reference
for changing the distance responses of learned content channels.
