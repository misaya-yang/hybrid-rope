\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

The interventions establish that interior allocation changes model behaviour;
theory explains how such a within-support change can matter. Exact geometry of
the full sin/cos pairs shows how $z$ redistributes a fixed spectral budget and
why geometric slow bands can collapse onto nearly the same positional basis.
Weight--table co-adaptation and the transplant obstruction explain why the
installation stage matters. A stated convex surrogate then yields \evq{} as an
analytic construction on the same coordinate.

\subsection{A finite table is a set of two-dimensional subspaces}
\label{sec:subspaces}

Let $\phi \in [0,1]$ parameterise log-frequency, $\omega(\phi) = b^{-\phi}$, let
$\drot$ be the number of rotated dimensions and $K = \drot/2$ the number of
rotary pairs ($\drot{=}\dhd$ for standard MHA, smaller for compressed-RoPE
architectures such as MLA).

We reserve $z$ for the support-normalised coordinate in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:table-decomposition}. Standard implementations use raw geometric
exponents $u_k=k/K$, while deployed \evq{} uses midpoint quantiles
$u_k=(k+1/2)/K$; endpoint-inclusive grids used for static diagnostics are
labelled explicitly. Normalising the sampled geometric endpoints gives
$z_k=k/(K-1)$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:table-decomposition} is a coordinate identity for any
ordered positive table with nonzero span. In the geometric family
$z_k=k/(K-1)$, so fixing $(a,R)$ fixes every channel. A second table with the
same support and different interior $z$ therefore cannot be represented by a
different scalar base. Quantities such as median wavelength or the number of
channels completing a cycle are summaries of $z$, not omitted scalar controls.

The frozen-checkpoint protocols instantiate this identity without the Cosh
family; \S\ref{sec:exp-frozen-support} defines their fixed-support displacement
map, and App.~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support} specifies its controls.

A rotary pair contributes $f_\omega(\Delta) = C\cos(\omega\Delta) +
D\sin(\omega\Delta)$ to the relative-position attention logit, with $C,D$ set by
content. The object attached to $\omega$ is therefore the two-dimensional
subspace
\begin{equation}
V_\omega=\operatorname{span}\{\cos(\omega\Delta),\ \sin(\omega\Delta)\},
\label{eq:subspace}
\end{equation}
not a cosine feature: an analysis that fixes one content phase sees a slice of
$V_\omega$ and is not invariant to rotation inside the pair. Two channels are
redundant to the extent that their subspaces are. With
$x_\omega(\Delta)=[\cos(\omega\Delta)\ \ \sin(\omega\Delta)]$, Gram matrices
$S_\omega=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\omega]$ and
$H_{\omega\nu}=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\nu]$ over a separation prior, and the
whitened cross-Gram $Q_{\omega\nu}=S_\omega^{-1/2}H_{\omega\nu}S_\nu^{-1/2}$
whenever $S_\omega,S_\nu\succ0$,
the singular values of $Q_{\omega\nu}$ are the canonical correlations between
$V_\omega$ and $V_\nu$, and
\begin{equation}
c_{\omega\nu}=\tfrac12\lVert Q_{\omega\nu}\rVert_F^2\in[0,1]
\label{eq:collision}
\end{equation}
is invariant to phase rotation inside a pair and to any change of basis of
either subspace. For $\Delta\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,L]$, $H_{\omega\nu}$ has a closed
form in $\sin t/t$ and $(1{-}\cos t)/t$ at $t=(\omega{\mp}\nu)L$
(App.~\ref{sec:proofs}).

\subsection{The spectral budget is an effective-dimension account}
\label{sec:budget}

Block-whitening every pair gives a global correlation Gram $\Gamma$ with diagonal
blocks $I_2$. Write $\bar c$ for the mean of the $c_{ij}$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral budget identity]
\label{thm:budget}
$\operatorname{tr}\Gamma = 2K$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)=2K[1+(K{-}1)\bar c]$, so the
Rényi-$2$ effective rank of the positional basis is exactly
\begin{equation}
r_2(\Gamma)=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Gamma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)}
      =\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\label{eq:budget-identity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The denominator turns the spectral budget into an exact R\'enyi-$2$
effective-dimension identity: larger mean pairwise redundancy lowers $r_2$.
Because $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma=2K$ is fixed, allocation cannot create nominal
rotary dimension; it changes how evenly that fixed budget is distributed across
positional directions. This is the precise sense in which a finite RoPE table
has a spectral budget.
Fix $K$, sampled support $(a,R)$, and separation measure $\mu$. Substituting
$\omega_k(z)=\exp[-(a+Rz_k)]$ into the full-pair block-whitened positional Gram
$\Gamma_\mu(z)$ makes its spectrum, and hence the effective sin/cos basis realised by
the nominal rotary budget, a function of interior allocation. Geometry diagnoses
that basis; the fixed-support intervention of \S\ref{sec:exp-identify}
establishes its behavioural consequence. Block whitening removes per-channel
energy and conditioning from the diagnosis.

The deflation is concentrated at the slow end.

\begin{proposition}[Low-frequency collapse]
\label{prop:collapse}
As $\omega L\to0$, $V_\omega\to\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$. For slow
channels $x{=}\omega L$, $y{=}\nu L$, and
$\epsilon{=}\max\{|x|,|y|\}\to0$,
$2-\lVert Q_{x,y}\rVert_F^2=\tfrac{19}{12600}(x^2{-}y^2)^2+O(\epsilon^6)$: slow
subspaces approach a common two-dimensional limit at fourth order. Under the
softmax metric for a fixed $p$ with nondegenerate support on at least three
distances, $F=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top$ annihilates the constant direction,
and the centred limit is
$\operatorname{span}\{\Delta-\mathbb E_p\Delta,\ \Delta^2-\mathbb E_p\Delta^2\}$.
\end{proposition}

Concretely, at $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, $K{=}64$ the $23$ pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions but have block-whitened
$r_2 = 2.00$. Thus more than one third of the rotary pairs spend $46$ nominal
dimensions on approximately two effective positional dimensions. The channels
may still carry content; the geometric redundancy tax is that many of them
describe almost the same positional directions. Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}
characterises the severe slow-band limit. The fixed-support identification also
covers settings outside that asymptotic regime: in its $b{=}256$ control no
sampled pair lies deep in the limit, and Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} measures
overlap over the full table. Additional allocation and redundancy views appear
in App.~Fig.~\ref{fig:frequency-geometry}.

\subsection{The table is a training-time coordinate system}
\label{sec:coordinate}

Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} measures the geometry available to a table; trained
behaviour additionally depends on the coefficients learned in that basis. To
separate the two, we cross two trained $50$M weight sets with two runtime
tables at $L{=}512$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and
$\tau_{\mathrm{EVQ}}{=}2.83$. All parameters remain frozen throughout this
diagnostic
(Table~\ref{tab:coadapt}). Substituting \evq{} for the geometric table at
inference \emph{raises} the block-whitened $r_2$ from $4.57$ to $12.54$
while perplexity degrades from $7.14$ to $76.20$.

Two consequences discipline the rest of the paper. First, collision, effective
rank, and log-determinant describe the table without its learned coefficients;
the task claims in \S\ref{sec:experiments} are therefore established on trained
models. Second, the two \emph{self-consistent} systems are essentially
tied in-window ($7.14$ vs.\ $7.16$) while both mismatched cells collapse: on LM
loss the table and weight main effects are $+0.599$ and $-0.597$, and their
interaction contrast is $-3.537$ (configuration-level bootstrap
CI $[-5.17,-3.04]$). The table behaves as a coordinate system fixed before
training, with the weights learning coefficients in it.

\begin{theorem}[Post-hoc transplant obstruction]
\label{thm:obstruction}
Let $\mathcal R_\Omega(\Delta)$ be the block-rotation operator of a frequency multiset
$\Omega$. If position-independent invertible maps $A,B$ satisfy
$A^\top \mathcal R_{\Omega'}(\Delta)B = \mathcal R_\Omega(\Delta)$ for all $\Delta$ in an open
interval containing zero, then $\Omega'$ and $\Omega$ have the same frequency
multiset, up to sign and permutation. Repeated frequencies may mix within their
full equal-frequency invariant subspace.
\end{theorem}

The proof is short: $\Delta{=}0$ gives $A^\top B = I$, so the relation becomes a
similarity $A^\top \mathcal R_{\Omega'}A^{-\top}=\mathcal R_\Omega$; differentiating at zero makes
the generators similar, and similarity preserves the spectrum $\{\pm i\omega_k\}$
(App.~\ref{sec:proofs}). The theorem therefore characterises exact function
preservation by this broad class of fixed linear Q/K compensation. The
finite-distribution protocols of \S\ref{sec:exp-capability} measure the complementary
case in which Q/K or broader low-rank paths adapt to the new table.

\subsection{An analytic non-uniform allocation family}
\label{sec:construction}

A positive density $\rho\in C^2([0,1])$ with $\int_0^1\rho=1$ induces a discrete
allocation by inverse-CDF quantisation, $\phi_k=F_\rho^{-1}(u_k)$; geometric RoPE
is $\rho\equiv1$. The exact canonical-correlation objective is oscillatory. To
obtain a closed-form construction, we use the convex modelling surrogate
\begin{equation}
\Capp[\rho]=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_0^1\!\rho^2\,d\phi
 +\frac{\beta}{2}\iint_{[0,1]^2}\!\!\rho(\phi)\rho(\psi)\min(\phi,\psi)\,d\phi\,d\psi,
\label{eq:Capp}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha>0,\beta\geq0$. The diagonal term penalises channel-load concentration;
$\min(\phi,\psi)$ is the Green kernel of $-\partial_\phi^2$ and encodes a
penalty for pairs of channels that are both slow, matching the direction of
Prop.~\ref{prop:collapse}. The surrogate supplies the analytic allocation;
phase-invariant geometry measures the resulting basis. Under the closed-form
cosine-feature kernel, the derived table lowers collision by $24$--$92\%$ and
raises its cosine-Gram Shannon rank in all $12$ tested configurations
(App.~\ref{sec:surrogate-validation}), while trained interventions establish
the behavioural effect.

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form optimum under the surrogate]
\label{thm:ode}
Under \eqref{eq:Capp} with $\alpha{>}0,\beta{\geq}0$ and constraints
$\rho\in C^2([0,1])$, $\rho{>}0$, $\int_0^1\rho{=}1$, the unique constrained
minimiser is
\begin{equation}
\rho_\tau(\phi)=\frac{\tau\cosh(\tau(1{-}\phi))}{\sinh\tau},
\qquad \tau=\sqrt{\beta/\alpha};
\label{eq:rho-tau}
\end{equation}
at $\beta{=}0$ it is the $\tau{\to}0$ limit $\rho_0\equiv1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Closed-form quantiles]
\label{cor:quantiles}
$F_\tau(\phi)=1-\sinh(\tau(1{-}\phi))/\sinh\tau$, so
\begin{equation}
\phi_k(\tau)=1-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\operatorname{arcsinh}\!\left((1-u_k)\sinh\tau\right),
\qquad u_k=\tfrac{k+1/2}{K},
\label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
which tends to $u_k$ as $\tau\to0$, recovering the uniform-log midpoint grid.
\end{corollary}

The theorem selects the Cosh family exactly for \eqref{eq:Capp}; midpoint
quantisation turns that density into a finite RoPE table. For $\tau>0$,
$\rho_\tau$ decreases across log-frequency: relative to the uniform grid it
places more samples on the fast side and thins the redundant slow side, while
$\tau\to0$ recovers the geometric allocation. The inverse CDF therefore
discretises the continuous solution without search or learned positional
parameters. Its uniqueness is conditional on the stated surrogate. The
pre-specified operating rule, finite multiplier evidence, and grid conventions
are in
App.~\ref{sec:tau-scaling}. The exact-range endpoint embedding is specified in
App.~\ref{sec:identification-details}.

Viewed together, the theory turns finite RoPE design into a basis-allocation
problem. The coordinate $z$ selects the basis available to training; the
collapse law identifies where geometric sampling spends the finite budget
redundantly; the crossing and obstruction show that learned coefficients bind
to the installed basis; and the variational solution supplies a table before
those coefficients are learned. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests the same
coordinate with weights fully frozen, under matched low-rank adaptation, and
through from-training co-adaptation.
