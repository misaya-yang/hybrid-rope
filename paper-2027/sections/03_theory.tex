\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

Finite RoPE design is a constrained basis-allocation problem. With pair count
and sampled support fixed, a table chooses where to place phase resolution. We
first account for that finite budget exactly, then locate the slow-end
redundancy of geometric sampling and derive \evq{} as a closed-form
reallocation. Finally, we connect the designed basis to the stage at which
weights exploit it; capability is established by the controlled interventions,
not inferred from static geometry.

\subsection{A finite spectral budget is a design problem}
\label{sec:subspaces}
\label{sec:budget}

Let $\phi \in [0,1]$ parameterise log-frequency, $\omega(\phi) = b^{-\phi}$,
let $\drot$ be the number of rotated dimensions and $K = \drot/2$ the number of
rotary pairs ($\drot{=}\dhd$ for standard MHA, smaller for compressed-RoPE
architectures such as MLA).

We reserve $z$ for the support-normalised coordinate in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:table-decomposition}. Normalising a sampled geometric table gives
$z_k=k/(K-1)$, so fixing $(a,R)$ fixes the geometric family and a non-uniform
interior allocation is a distinct fixed-support design. Standard
implementations use raw exponents $u_k=k/K$, deployed \evq{} uses midpoint
quantiles $u_k=(k+1/2)/K$, and endpoint-inclusive diagnostic grids are labelled
explicitly.

A rotary pair contributes $f_\omega(\Delta) = C\cos(\omega\Delta) +
D\sin(\omega\Delta)$ to the relative-position attention logit, with $C,D$ set by
content. The object attached to $\omega$ is therefore the two-dimensional
subspace
\begin{equation}
V_\omega=\operatorname{span}\{\cos(\omega\Delta),\ \sin(\omega\Delta)\},
\label{eq:subspace}
\end{equation}
not a cosine feature: an analysis that fixes one content phase sees a slice of
$V_\omega$ and is not invariant to rotation inside the pair. Two channels are
redundant to the extent that their subspaces are. With
$x_\omega(\Delta)=[\cos(\omega\Delta)\ \ \sin(\omega\Delta)]$, Gram matrices
$S_\omega=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\omega]$ and
$H_{\omega\nu}=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\nu]$ over a separation prior, and the
whitened cross-Gram $Q_{\omega\nu}=S_\omega^{-1/2}H_{\omega\nu}S_\nu^{-1/2}$
whenever $S_\omega,S_\nu\succ0$,
the singular values of $Q_{\omega\nu}$ are the canonical correlations between
$V_\omega$ and $V_\nu$, and
\begin{equation}
c_{\omega\nu}=\tfrac12\lVert Q_{\omega\nu}\rVert_F^2\in[0,1]
\label{eq:collision}
\end{equation}
is invariant to phase rotation inside a pair and to any change of basis of
either subspace. For $\Delta\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,L]$, $H_{\omega\nu}$ has a closed
form in $\sin t/t$ and $(1{-}\cos t)/t$ at $t=(\omega{\mp}\nu)L$
(App.~\ref{sec:proofs}).

Block-whitening every pair gives a global correlation Gram $\Gamma$ with diagonal
blocks $I_2$. Write
$\bar c=[K(K-1)]^{-1}\sum_{i\ne j}c_{ij}$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral budget identity]
\label{thm:budget}
$\operatorname{tr}\Gamma = 2K$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)=2K[1+(K{-}1)\bar c]$, so the
Rényi-$2$ effective rank of the positional basis is exactly
\begin{equation}
r_2(\Gamma)=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Gamma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)}
      =\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\label{eq:budget-identity}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Practically, this identity exposes the design budget. For fixed $K$, sampled
support $(a,R)$, and separation measure $\mu$, substituting
$\omega_k(z)=\exp[-(a+Rz_k)]$ into the block-whitened Gram $\Gamma_\mu(z)$
changes how the same trace $2K$ is distributed across positional directions.
Block whitening removes per-channel energy and conditioning: the theorem
characterises the basis's R\'enyi-$2$ effective dimension, while trained-model
protocols establish what
that basis enables.

\subsection{Slow-end redundancy gives a design direction}

\begin{proposition}[Low-frequency collapse]
\label{prop:collapse}
As $\omega L\to0$, $V_\omega\to\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$. For slow
channels $x{=}\omega L$, $y{=}\nu L$, and
$\epsilon{=}\max\{|x|,|y|\}\to0$,
$2-\lVert Q_{x,y}\rVert_F^2=\tfrac{19}{12600}(x^2{-}y^2)^2+O(\epsilon^6)$: slow
subspaces approach a common two-dimensional limit at fourth order. Under the
softmax metric for a fixed $p$ with nondegenerate support on at least three
distances, $F=\operatorname{diag}(p)-pp^\top$ annihilates the constant direction,
and the centred limit is
$\operatorname{span}\{\Delta-\mathbb E_p\Delta,\ \Delta^2-\mathbb E_p\Delta^2\}$.
\end{proposition}

Practically, geometric slow-end sampling provides diminishing new positional
directions. This motivates thinning that region while preserving support and
multiscale coverage; the channels may still carry content, and the static rank
is not a behavioural selector. As a representative severe-regime instance, at
$L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and $K{=}64$, the $23$ standard-grid pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions but have block-whitened
$r_2{=}2.00$ under the uniform separation prior. The full-table identity also
applies outside this asymptotic regime; additional views appear in
App.~Fig.~\ref{fig:frequency-geometry}.

\subsection{A closed-form reallocation}
\label{sec:construction}

A positive density $\rho\in C^2([0,1])$ with $\int_0^1\rho=1$ induces a finite
allocation by inverse-CDF quantisation, $\phi_k=F_\rho^{-1}(u_k)$; geometric RoPE
is $\rho\equiv1$. The exact canonical-correlation objective is oscillatory, so
we use the convex modelling surrogate
\begin{equation}
\Capp[\rho]=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_0^1\!\rho^2\,d\phi
 +\frac{\beta}{2}\iint_{[0,1]^2}\!\!\rho(\phi)\rho(\psi)\min(\phi,\psi)\,d\phi\,d\psi,
\label{eq:Capp}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha>0,\beta\geq0$. The first term limits channel-load concentration;
the Green kernel $\min(\phi,\psi)$ penalises pairs that are both slow, following
the direction of Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}.

\begin{theorem}[Closed-form optimum under the surrogate]
\label{thm:ode}
Under \eqref{eq:Capp} with $\alpha{>}0,\beta{\geq}0$ and constraints
$\rho\in C^2([0,1])$, $\rho{>}0$, $\int_0^1\rho{=}1$, the unique constrained
minimiser is
\begin{equation}
\rho_\tau(\phi)=\frac{\tau\cosh(\tau(1{-}\phi))}{\sinh\tau},
\qquad \tau=\sqrt{\beta/\alpha};
\label{eq:rho-tau}
\end{equation}
at $\beta{=}0$ it is the $\tau{\to}0$ limit $\rho_0\equiv1$.
\end{theorem}

Practically, the theorem turns the slow-end design direction into an analytic
allocation rather than a search problem. The following inverse CDF instantiates
that continuous solution as a finite table.

\begin{corollary}[Closed-form quantiles]
\label{cor:quantiles}
$F_\tau(\phi)=1-\sinh(\tau(1{-}\phi))/\sinh\tau$, so
\begin{equation}
\phi_k(\tau)=1-\tfrac{1}{\tau}\operatorname{arcsinh}\!\left((1-u_k)\sinh\tau\right),
\qquad u_k=\tfrac{k+1/2}{K},
\label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
which tends to $u_k$ as $\tau\to0$, recovering the uniform-log midpoint grid.
\end{corollary}

For $\tau>0$, $\rho_\tau$ decreases across log-frequency and crosses the uniform
density once (Lemma~\ref{lem:budget-crossing}); the quantiles therefore
place more samples on the fast side and thin the redundant slow side. This gives
\evq{} explicitly, without learned positional parameters or numerical search.
Its uniqueness is conditional on \eqref{eq:Capp}; the operating rule, finite
multiplier evidence, grid conventions, and exact-range endpoint embedding are
in Apps.~\ref{sec:tau-scaling} and~\ref{sec:identification-details}. The
cosine-feature diagnostic is reported separately in
App.~\ref{sec:surrogate-validation}; trained interventions establish behaviour.

\subsection{A designed basis must be evaluated at its installation stage}
\label{sec:coordinate}

A designed table fixes the positional basis; model capability depends on the
coefficients that exploit it. Controlled fixed-support protocols show that
designed reallocations can strongly improve capability in the tested protocols. The training
intervention in \S\ref{sec:exp-identify} evaluates anchored \evq{}, while the
fully frozen study in \S\ref{sec:exp-frozen-support} evaluates separate derived
and coarse profiles; they supply likelihood and capability evidence,
respectively.

The installation stage is visible in a frozen $50$M weights-by-table crossing
(Table~\ref{tab:coadapt}). The self-consistent Geo and \evq{} systems have PPL
$7.14$ and $7.16$, while both mismatched cells degrade. In particular, installing
\evq{} under Geo-trained weights raises block-whitened $r_2$ from $4.57$ to
$12.54$ but worsens PPL from $7.14$ to $76.20$; the loss interaction is $-3.537$
(bootstrap CI $[-5.17,-3.04]$).

Thus static geometry diagnoses the designed basis but does not rank trained
systems: weights learn coefficients in the basis installed during training or
adaptation.

\begin{theorem}[Post-hoc transplant obstruction]
\label{thm:obstruction}
Let $\mathcal R_\Omega(\Delta)$ be the block-rotation operator of a frequency multiset
$\Omega$. If position-independent invertible maps $A,B$ satisfy
$A^\top \mathcal R_{\Omega'}(\Delta)B = \mathcal R_\Omega(\Delta)$ for all $\Delta$ in an open
interval containing zero, then $\Omega'$ and $\Omega$ have the same frequency
multiset, up to sign and permutation. Repeated frequencies may mix within their
full equal-frequency invariant subspace.
\end{theorem}

Practically, a nontrivial new table cannot be made exactly function-equivalent by
fixed, position-independent invertible Q/K maps: the design must be evaluated
with the weights, adapter, or operator that will use it. At $\Delta{=}0$ the
hypothesis gives $A^\top B=I$; differentiating the resulting similarity at zero
preserves the generator spectrum $\{\pm i\omega_k\}$ (App.~\ref{sec:proofs}). The
theorem does not exclude approximate finite-data adaptation or broader paths,
which are tested in \S\ref{sec:exp-capability}.

Together, the budget identity defines what can be reallocated, the collapse law
locates redundant slow-end sampling, and the variational theorem supplies a
closed-form table before its coefficients are learned. The fixed-support and
lifecycle experiments test designed allocations with weights fully frozen,
under matched adaptation, and through from-training co-adaptation
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
