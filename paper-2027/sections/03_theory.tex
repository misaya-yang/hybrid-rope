\section{Theory and Method}
\label{sec:theory}

The minimiser of $\Capp$ is exact conditional on the stated convex surrogate;
the pure-tether branch then gives a closed-form modelling choice. A separate
post-softmax calculation supplies the $\deff/\sqrt{\Ltr}$ scaling structure,
while its finite prefactor is selected empirically.

\subsection{RoPE as frequency allocation}

Let $\phi \in [0,1]$ parameterise log-frequency, $\omega(\phi) = b^{-\phi}$.
Let $\drot$ be the number of RoPE-rotated dimensions and $K = \drot/2$ the
number of rotary pairs. For standard MHA $\drot = \dhd$; for compressed-RoPE
architectures such as MLA (\S\ref{sec:exp-mla}) $\drot$ may be smaller, in
which case the inverse-CDF grid uses $K = \drot/2$. The concrete MLA study uses
its pre-specified $\tau$ and is not treated as a test of the general
operating-point rule. A positive density $\rho \in C^2([0,1])$ with
$\int_0^1\rho = 1$ induces a discrete allocation by inverse-CDF quantisation,
$\phi_k = F_\rho^{-1}(u_k)$, $F_\rho(\phi) = \int_0^\phi\rho$. Geometric RoPE
is $\rho \equiv 1$.

\subsection{A surrogate collision functional}

For a bounded-support separation prior $D$, the exact phase-collision kernel
$K(\phi_1,\phi_2) = \int D(\Delta)\cos(\omega(\phi_1)\Delta)\cos(\omega(\phi_2)\Delta)\,d\Delta$
is oscillatory and is not pointwise approximated by a smooth two-term model. We
therefore analyse the surrogate quadratic form
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Capp[\rho]
={}& \frac{\alpha}{2}\int_0^1 \rho(\phi)^2\,d\phi\\
 &+ \frac{\beta}{2}\iint_{[0,1]^2}\!\! \rho(\phi)\rho(\psi)\min(\phi,\psi)\,d\phi\,d\psi,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Capp}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha,\beta>0$ fitted to the exact kernel on a finite channel grid
(App.~\ref{sec:tau-scaling}), giving $\alpha \approx 1/\drot$. The diagonal term penalises channel-load
concentration; $\min(\phi,\psi)$ is the Green kernel of $-\partial_\phi^2$ on
$[0,1]$ and penalises pairs of channels that are \emph{both} slow, whose phase
features are strongly correlated over a finite separation window.  This is a
spectral-packing penalty; it does not assume that slow channels carry no
content or positional function.
$\Capp$ is a discrete-grid surrogate, not the continuum stationary-phase
asymptotic of $K$ (App.~\ref{sec:why-constant-alpha}).

\subsection{Constrained stationary allocation}
\label{sec:ode}

Minimising \eqref{eq:Capp} subject to $\int_0^1\rho = 1$ with multiplier $\nu$,
the first variation gives $\alpha\rho(\phi) + \beta g(\phi) + \nu = 0$ with
$g(\phi) = \int_0^1\rho(\psi)\min(\phi,\psi)\,d\psi$. Since $g'' = -\rho$,
every interior stationary density obeys $\rho'' - \tau^2\rho = 0$ with
$\tau = \sqrt{\beta/\alpha}$; pre-differentiated stationarity and mass
normalisation give $\rho'(0) = -\tau^2$, $\rho'(1) = 0$
(App.~\ref{sec:proofs}).

\begin{theorem}[Exact stationary allocation under the surrogate]
\label{thm:ode}
Under \eqref{eq:Capp} with $\alpha{>}0,\beta{\geq}0$ (so $\Capp$ is convex) and
constraints $\rho\in C^2([0,1])$, $\rho{>}0$, $\int_0^1\rho{=}1$, the unique
constrained minimiser is
\begin{equation}
\rho_\tau(\phi) = \frac{\tau\,\cosh(\tau(1{-}\phi))}{\sinh\tau},
\qquad \tau = \sqrt{\beta/\alpha},
\end{equation}
strictly positive on $[0,1]$ for $\beta{>}0$; at $\beta{=}0$ the minimiser is
the $\tau{\to}0$ limit $\rho_0\equiv 1$ (uniform/geometric).
\end{theorem}

Here $\tau{=}\sqrt{\beta/\alpha}$ indexes the exact surrogate family. The
deployed value is \emph{not} obtained by equating it to the fitted coefficient
ratio; it is selected by the separate operating-point model below.

The forced extension and the choice of the closed-form homogeneous branch are
derived in App.~\ref{subsec:fisher-ext}.

\subsection{Closed form, and the geometric limit}

\begin{corollary}[Closed-form pure-tether quantiles]
For $\tau>0$ the density of Theorem~\ref{thm:ode} has CDF
$F_\tau(\phi) = 1 - \sinh(\tau(1{-}\phi))/\sinh\tau$, so inverse-CDF
quantisation gives
\begin{equation}
\phi_k(\tau) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{\tau}\operatorname{arcsinh}\!\left((1-u_k)\sinh\tau\right),
\qquad u_k = \tfrac{k+1/2}{K}.
\label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
As $\tau\to0$, $\phi_k(\tau)\to u_k$, recovering the uniform-log midpoint
grid within the pure-tether sub-family.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{The complete recipe.} For effective head dimension $\deff$ and
training length $\Ltr$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:evq-practical}
\begin{gathered}
\tau = \deff/\sqrt{\Ltr},
\qquad
K=\drot/2,\\[2pt]
u_k = (k+\tfrac12)/K,
\qquad
\omega_k^{\mathrm{EVQ}} = b^{-\phi_k(\tau)}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Set $\tau$, compute midpoint quantiles, evaluate \eqref{eq:warp}, replace the
inverse frequencies. No auxiliary loss, no extra parameters, no optimiser
dependence, and no declared deployment length. The endpoint grid $u_k{=}k/K$ is
an analytic anchored variant ($\phi_0{=}0$ preserves $\omega_0{=}1$); the
deployed implementation uses midpoint quantisation, and experiments using the
anchored variant are labeled separately.

\subsection{What the schedule concretely does}

At $\tau{=}4$, $b{=}5\times10^5$, the warp moves budget toward short
inverse-frequency scales: the continuous quantile $u{=}0.5$ moves from
$1/\omega{=}707$ to $9.69$, and $u{=}0.9$ from $134{,}609$ to $1{,}720$.
The deployed midpoint grid therefore changes sampled extrema and span at finite
$K$; the exact-range experiments anchor those quantities explicitly. At
$\tau{=}2$, $\rho_\tau(0.1){=}1.71$ and $\rho_\tau(1){=}0.55$, while the band
$1/\omega{=}2048$ has relative density $0.76$. Thus thinning concentrates on
channels with $1/\omega\gg\Ltr$ while preserving more of the training-scale
band. This motivates a directional prediction: channels with $1/\omega\ll\Ltr$
complete many periods during training, so positions beyond $\Ltr$ land in
already-trained phase regions, while channels with $1/\omega\gg\Ltr$ never
complete one and extrapolate into unseen phase. Moving budget toward the former
should favour extrapolation, while any in-window cost must be measured after
training. The surrogate does not assign the much larger mismatch observed when
a pretrained model is retrofitted with a new table.

\subsection{Where the finite budget moves}
\label{sec:budget-shift}

The allocation is easiest to read in wavelength,
$\lambda(\phi)=2\pi/\omega(\phi)=2\pi b^\phi$.  For a training span $L$ and
an evaluation ceiling $M>L$, the exponent axis has three geometric regions:
$\lambda\le L$ completes at least one cycle during training;
$L<\lambda\le M$ varies across the target window without completing a cycle
during training; and $\lambda>M$ completes no cycle even at the target ceiling.
These regions describe phase geometry, not a claim that any channel is unused.

\begin{lemma}[Single-crossing budget shift]
\label{lem:budget-crossing}
For every $\tau>0$, $\rho_\tau$ crosses the uniform density exactly once, at
\begin{equation}
\phi_c(\tau)
=1-\frac{1}{\tau}\operatorname{arcosh}\!\left(\frac{\sinh\tau}{\tau}\right)
\le 1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\approx0.4226.
\label{eq:budget-crossing}
\end{equation}
Thus \evq{} assigns more density than the geometric grid for
$\phi<\phi_c$ and less for $\phi>\phi_c$.
\end{lemma}

For OLMo-2, $b=5\times10^5$, $L=4096$, and $M=32768$ place the two boundaries
at $\phi_L=0.494$ and $\phi_M=0.652$, both to the right of the universal
crossing bound.  At $\tau=2$, the continuous mass in the three regions changes
from $0.494/0.158/0.348$ under a uniform grid to $0.671/0.122/0.208$.
The corresponding $64$-pair midpoint tables contain $32/10/22$ versus
$43/8/13$ channels.  The warp therefore buys more completed-cycle channels by
spending density in both longer-wavelength regions.  This is an exact budget
statement, not a trained-loss theorem; it predicts a measurable reach--resolution
trade that the mature-model experiments test.  Proof and a complementary
divergence inequality are in App.~\ref{sec:waterbed-proof}.

\subsection{The operating-point rule, and its status}
\label{sec:tau-star}

Shape and scale come from different layers. The cosh shape is the pre-softmax
stationary solution under $\Capp$; the scale is selected \emph{post}-softmax.

\begin{samepage}
\begin{proposition}[Semi-analytic operating-point scaling]
\label{prop:softmax-transport}
On the pure-tether family, assuming $Q_1(L,b)>0$ and a diffuse post-softmax baseline
$p_0{=}1/L$, the Pearson-$\chi^2$ channel-load stiffness and post-softmax
transport utility admit small-$\tau$ expansions
$S_{\chi^2}(\tau) = c_S\tau^4/\dhd + O(\tau^6)$ with $c_S{=}\tfrac{1}{45}$, and
$U(\tau,L) = (\dhd/L)[Q_0 + \tau^2 Q_1(L,b) + O(\tau^4)]$, with
$Q_1(L,b) = \int_0^1 \eta(\phi)q(Lb^{-\phi})d\phi$,
$\eta(\phi) = (1{-}\phi)^2/2 - 1/6$, and
$q(x) = 1/2 + \sin(2x)/(4x) - (\sin x/x)^2$
(App.~\ref{sec:stiffness-derivation}). The leading-order stationary point of
$\mathcal{F}(\tau) = \tfrac12 S_{\chi^2}(\tau) - \lambda U(\tau,L)$ satisfies
\[
\tau_*^2 = 45\lambda Q_1(L,b)\cdot\frac{\dhd^2}{L},
\qquad \tau_* \propto \frac{\dhd}{\sqrt{L}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}

Within this model, the explicit $\dhd/\sqrt{L}$ factor is modulated by
$Q_1(L,b)$; this is a scaling structure, not a pure power law. The prefactor
$c(\Pi){=}\sqrt{45\lambda Q_1(L,b)}$ remains convention- and
protocol-dependent. At practical $\tau$ the exact cosh warp is evaluated
directly---the small-$\tau$ series motivates the scaling and does not certify a
finite operating point. For non-diffuse attention, a softmax-Jacobian Rayleigh
quotient provides a curvature diagnostic, but connecting that diagnostic to
the utility above is an additional modelling assumption
(App.~\ref{sec:leff-derivation}).

We therefore deploy the zero-search convention $c{=}1$ and test it as an
operating default. In one direct selection sweep, selected $\tau{=}5$ and the
rule value $5.657$ differ by $0.0119$ selection NLL. Under the stricter
exact-range factorial the rule is the best pre-specified Cosh multiplier in $4/12$
structural configurations ($0.75\times/1.25\times$ win $2/12$ and $6/12$).
These results support a useful basin initialisation, not point optimality;
\S\ref{sec:exp-tau} reports the full controlled comparison.
