\section{The Positional Structure Changed by \texorpdfstring{$z$}{z}}
\label{sec:theory}

We next characterize what changing $z$ changes in the supplied positional basis. The objects are complete rotary-pair subspaces under a declared separation measure; their overlap describes positional directions before model-specific content coefficients enter.

\subsection{Complete rotary pairs and positional overlap}
\label{sec:subspaces}
\label{sec:budget}

For a fixed content pair, one rotary channel contributes $C\cos(\omega\Delta)+D\sin(\omega\Delta)$ to an attention logit, where $\Delta$ is relative token separation. Both coefficients depend on content; its positional object is the full function space
\begin{equation}
V_\omega=\operatorname{span}\{\cos(\omega\Delta),\sin(\omega\Delta)\}.
\label{eq:subspace}
\end{equation}
Comparing only cosine curves would privilege one content phase. Instead, let $x_\omega(\Delta)=[\cos(\omega\Delta),\sin(\omega\Delta)]$ and define $S_\omega=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\omega]$, $H_{\omega\nu}=\mathbb E[x_\omega^\top x_\nu]$ under a declared separation measure. For nonsingular self-Grams, the singular values of $Q_{\omega\nu}=S_\omega^{-1/2}H_{\omega\nu}S_\nu^{-1/2}$ are the canonical correlations, or cosines of principal angles, of the two spaces \citep{bjorck1973angles}. Their mean square,
\begin{equation}
c_{\omega\nu}=\tfrac12\|Q_{\omega\nu}\|_F^2\in[0,1],
\label{eq:collision}
\end{equation}
measures shared positional directions and is invariant to invertible basis changes within either pair. Let $\Gamma$ be the Gram matrix of the concatenated block-whitened pairs, with diagonal blocks $I_2$. The average pairwise overlap $\bar c$ gives the exact R\'enyi-$2$ effective-rank identity
\begin{equation}
r_2(\Gamma)=\frac{(\operatorname{tr}\Gamma)^2}{\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)}
=\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\label{eq:budget-identity}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)$ is the frame potential of the normalized positional functions \citep{benedetto2003frames}. The identity specializes this classical measure to full rotary pairs: increasing pairwise overlap concentrates the same $2K$ coordinates into fewer effective positional directions.

\subsection{Finite-window concentration of slow directions}
\label{sec:slow-limit}

\begin{proposition}[Shared slow-frequency subspace]
\label{prop:collapse}
Under uniform measure on $[0,L]$, with $t=\Delta/L$, as $x=\omega L\to0$, $\cos(xt)\to1$ and $\sin(xt)/x\to t$, so $V_\omega$ converges to $\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$. For $\epsilon=\max\{|\omega L|,|\nu L|\}\to0$, squared chordal separation satisfies $2-\|Q_{\omega\nu}\|_F^2=\mathcal O(\epsilon^4)$.
\end{proposition}

Distinct slow frequencies therefore approach a shared two-dimensional positional space. On the standard grid $b=500{,}000$, $K=64$, $L=4096$, the $23$ pairs with $\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ coordinates but have block-whitened effective rank $r_2=2.00013$. At $b=256$, $K=32$, $L=256$, the eight slowest pairs span $\omega L\in[1.19,4]$ and have effective rank $r_2=2.11$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:allocation-basis}). Their overlap is substantial even though every frequency has $\omega L>1$.

\paragraph{Design implication.}
A fixed set of rotary pairs can supply strongly overlapping positional directions. Allocation controls this concentration, while learning determines how the model combines these directions with content. This motivates two uses of frequency-spacing design: distribute the basis during learning, or redistribute extension relative to a frozen model's native table. Appendix~\ref{sec:compatibility} examines the learned frequency--coordinate associations through crossed-table and slot interventions.
