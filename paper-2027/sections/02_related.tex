\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Positional encoding can determine whether a transformer generalises beyond its
training length \citep{kazemnejad2023impact}. RoPE extensions rescale positions,
change a scalar base, transport frequency bands, or remap remote phases
\citep{chen2024position,peng2024yarn,ding2024longrope,shang2025longrope2,
tian2026mrrope,tang2026jetlong}. FMRoPE provides a target-aware support/base
construction for a chosen length \citep{oka2026fmrope}. Its paper-faithful arm
serves here as the geometric causal control, not a competing method: pinning its
endpoints and log-span lets us change only the interiors. Frequency-dependent
transport can likewise realise a non-uniform $z$ while moving support or phase.
Base analyses bound the geometric family's attainable context
\citep{xu2024base,liu2024scaling}, while learned use of a supplied grid adds a
data-dependent view \citep{wu2026datashapes}.

Other work measures unequal use of RoPE bands, removes or clips selected
rotations, learns input-dependent angles or frequency tables, or replaces the
fixed table with a larger positional function
\citep{barbero2025round,oka2026frequencyentropy,chiang2025rotary,
chen2025hope,li2026copeclipped,wertheimer2026frayed,zhang2026grape,
movahedi2026selectiverope,wang2024resonance,zheng2024dape,li2024fire,
hua2025fope,li2026repo}. LeRoPE and AdaRoPE make allocation explicit through
learned non-geometric frequencies \citep{karypis2026lerope,wang2026adarope};
LeRoPE's in-window improvements provide convergent evidence that allocation
matters beyond extrapolation. Its learned intervention complements our matched
fixed-support controls, which retain all $K$ canonical rotary planes, identify
$z$ causally, and derive a closed-form table without frequency search or learned
positional parameters.
