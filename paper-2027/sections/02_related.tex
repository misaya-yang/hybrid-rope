\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Range and frequency allocation.}
RoPE's rotation operator \citep{su2024roformer} admits many frequency choices. Scaling-law analyses study how geometric range affects length generalization \citep{liu2024scaling,xu2024base}. FMRoPE relates the learned band to base and training length \citep{oka2026fmrope}. In that family normalized exponents remain equally spaced; our fixed-range experiments isolate interior allocation. Position interpolation scales positions uniformly \citep{chen2024position}, while YaRN blends frequencies across bands \citep{peng2024yarn}. LongRoPE searches nonuniform interpolation factors and uses progressive extension \citep{ding2024longrope}; LongRoPE2 uses needle-driven perplexity for search and mixed-window training to preserve short-context performance \citep{shang2025longrope2}. Our paired training isolates allocation at matched endpoints, and our constructions specify analytic tables. MrRoPE parameterizes intermediate-band changes through mixed-radix conversion \citep{tian2026mrrope}. Equation~\eqref{eq:adjustment-families} casts these modifications as exponent displacements; our work separates their interior profile from sampled range.

\paragraph{Learning and using frequency bands.}
LeRoPE learns shared per-channel scales and tests training with a separately learned fixed table, while AdaRoPE studies head-specific frequencies and attention scaling \citep{karypis2026lerope,wang2026adarope}. These studies establish the value of flexible frequencies. Our exact-endpoint interventions identify the contribution of normalized interior placement, and crossed evaluation connects that placement to weights learned under each table. Frequency-use studies connect rotary coordinates to positional versus symbolic attention, retrieval, and data-dependent scales \citep{barbero2025round,urrutia2026decoupling,chiang2025rotary,oka2026frequencyentropy,wu2026datashapes}. \citet{urrutia2026decoupling} study symbolic and positional attention through model-dependent measurements. Our shared slow-frequency subspace describes supplied positional functions; learned usage is a distinct model-dependent object. Appendix~\ref{sec:pe-dominant} reports our historical unconstrained learned-frequency comparator. DoPE uses truncated matrix entropy to selectively modify positional encodings \citep{xiong2025dope}. Our canonical correlations instead characterize supplied sine--cosine subspaces as exponents vary.

M\"obius RoPE uses an anti-periodic frequency ladder and tests frozen table swaps \citep{bae2026mobius}. Its matched boundary-condition controls and our fixed-endpoint allocation controls address complementary aspects of frequency design.

\paragraph{Changing the positional operator.}
FoPE introduces Fourier components \citep{hua2025fope}, while HoPE and clipped encodings alter slow rotations \citep{chen2025hope,li2026copeclipped}. GRAPE extends the group action \citep{zhang2026grape}; Selective RoPE makes rotations input-dependent \citep{movahedi2026selectiverope}. Other variants learn contextual token positions \citep{li2026repo}, and compression preservation \citep{huang2026ppe}. Long-context token confusion analyses further motivate studying frequency choices and learned representations \citep{gu2026deconstructing,du2026distinguishes}. Our constructions retain the standard rotary map, while the controlled studies examine how its finite frequency table and learned coordinate associations affect behavior.
