\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Range and frequency allocation.}
RoPE's rotation operator \citep{su2024roformer} admits many frequency choices. Scaling-law analyses study how geometric range affects length generalization \citep{liu2024scaling,xu2024base}. FMRoPE relates the learned band to base and training length \citep{oka2026fmrope}. In that family normalized exponents remain equally spaced; our fixed-range experiments isolate interior allocation. Position interpolation scales positions uniformly \citep{chen2024position}, while YaRN blends frequencies across bands \citep{peng2024yarn}. LongRoPE and LongRoPE2 select per-model extension profiles through needle-guided evolutionary search over mixed-window training \citep{ding2024longrope,shang2025longrope2}; our aim is complementary rather than competing---we identify a design coordinate and give zero-search analytic constructions, and we do not claim to replace search-based per-model optimization. MrRoPE parameterizes intermediate-band changes through mixed-radix conversion \citep{tian2026mrrope}. Equation~\eqref{eq:adjustment-families} casts these modifications as exponent displacements; our work separates their interior profile from sampled range.

\paragraph{Learning and using frequency bands.}
That frequencies admit optimization, that geometric spacing need not be optimal, and that different channels may warrant different scales are established positions, not our contribution. LeRoPE learns shared per-channel scales, while AdaRoPE studies head-specific frequencies and attention scaling \citep{karypis2026lerope,wang2026adarope}; both search within a training loop. Our delta is narrower: causal identification of the interior allocation coordinate at strictly fixed support, parameter-free shared-table constructions, and the separation of a training allocation from the compatibility of mature weights with it. Frequency-use studies connect rotary coordinates to positional versus symbolic attention, retrieval, and data-dependent scales \citep{barbero2025round,urrutia2026decoupling,chiang2025rotary,oka2026frequencyentropy,wu2026datashapes}. \citet{urrutia2026decoupling} show that slow frequencies serve semantic representations rather than fine positional discrimination, consistent with our geometric collapse $\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$. DoPE uses truncated matrix entropy to selectively modify positional encodings \citep{xiong2025dope}. Our canonical correlations instead characterize supplied sine--cosine subspaces as exponents vary.

\paragraph{Changing the positional operator.}
FoPE introduces Fourier components \citep{hua2025fope}, while HoPE and clipped encodings alter slow rotations \citep{chen2025hope,li2026copeclipped}. Other variants explore group actions \citep{zhang2026grape}, input-dependent rotations \citep{movahedi2026selectiverope}, contextual token positions \citep{li2026repo}, and compression preservation \citep{huang2026ppe}. Long-context token confusion analyses further motivate studying frequency choices and learned representations \citep{gu2026deconstructing,du2026distinguishes}. While these methods alter the positional operator, our constructions retain the standard rotary map and isolate what is achievable within that space.
