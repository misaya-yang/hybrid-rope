\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
For a model trained to length $L$, $sL$ specifies a target window; quality
within that window determines its usefulness. RoPE's base sets a frequency
range, while internal placement determines the positional basis. We study
how this allocation affects model quality.

Frequency design includes frozen scaling policies such as YaRN and MrRoPE
and learned frequencies such as LeRoPE
\citep{peng2024yarn,tian2026mrrope,karypis2026lerope}.
Our paired interventions show that interior placement improves performance
at fixed frequency endpoints. Further controls isolate shape effects at equal
total displacement and distinguish positional structure from learned coordinate use.

We turn this design freedom into explicit constructions. TailSpline connects
the transition to the extended low-frequency tail, improving long-context tasks
across model families and on natural question answering. Native Contrastive
Proximal allocation (NCP) improves task performance within the original window.
Cosh supplies complementary evidence through paired learning and extrapolation.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Range and allocation have distinct, interacting effects.}
At fixed endpoints, internal placement improves performance; changing the
range can reverse the preferred allocation.
\item \textbf{Positional structure and learned use are distinct.}
Complete rotary pairs characterize positional overlap; coordinate interventions
and rank--quality comparisons distinguish it from learned frequency use.
\item \textbf{Explicit allocations improve extension and in-window quality.}
TailSpline provides a closed-form extension with cross-model and natural-QA
gains; NCP improves native-window tasks at unchanged endpoints.
\end{itemize}
