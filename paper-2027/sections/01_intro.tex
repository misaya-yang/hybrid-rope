\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A finite RoPE table has a spectral budget: its exactly $K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs
must allocate phase resolution across scales. Our question is not whether RoPE
frequencies can be changed, but what independent degrees of freedom a finite
frequency table contains. At the implementation level, changing a scalar base,
moving a sampled range, and relocating interior frequencies all change the
table. At the scientific level, they intervene on different coordinates. We
make those coordinates explicit by writing
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes the fixed $K$ rotary pairs, $a$ sets support location, $R$
its span, and $z$ the normalised interior allocation; geometric RoPE fixes
$z_k=k/(K-1)$. Thus support $(a,R)$ and interior allocation $z$ are distinct
coordinates: $z$ decides which phase scales the fixed samples occupy and where
resolution is placed.

To identify allocation, three paired training seeds hold $K$, sampled support,
the RoPE operator, sampled extrema, and log-span fixed while only the $30$
interiors move; every OOD length favours the reallocation in every seed. In a
separate condition, $L_{\mathrm{target}}$ retargets support and reverses the
tested ordering. The reversal identifies support as a distinct, interacting
coordinate of the same finite table (\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}).

Exact phase-invariant geometry explains why interior placement can matter. In
one representative table, $23$ slow pairs occupy $46$ nominal dimensions yet
have block-whitened R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. These channels can still
carry content, but as positional functions they converge toward the same
two-dimensional subspace: geometric spacing spends more than one third of its
pairs on near-duplicate slow positional directions.

Optimising a convex surrogate over $z$ yields \emph{Exact Variational
Quantization with a Cosh density} (\evq{}), a closed-form table with no learned
positional parameters, unique within that surrogate. It is one construction on
the support--allocation design space, not the identity of the paper.

We test behavioural consequences through three distinct intervention routes.
A fully frozen route installs model-relative derived or coarse allocations with
no parameter updates: on a billion-scale OLMo checkpoint, the derived allocation
raises nine-task $16$K RULER from $0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$, while official
Transformers YaRN scores $7.94\%$ on the same rows, and a coarse task-label-free
allocation reproduces the recovery. Matched low-rank adaptation tests whether
pretrained representations can exploit an \evq{} basis, yielding task-family
length transfer at $1.485$B and causal remote-source use at $8$B. From training,
weights co-adapt to the installed table: in a $750$M continuation, \evq{} lowers
$16$K PPL from $45.1$ to $24.4$ and raises strict $8$K autoregressive exact
retrieval from $0\%$ to $77.5\%$. These routes intervene on the same scientific
coordinate without forcing their methods or estimands to coincide.

Figure~\ref{fig:spectral-budget-overview} follows this scientific order: it
defines the two coordinates, identifies them through fixed-support and
support-retargeted interventions, and then shows a mature zero-training
behavioural consequence. The remaining experiments retain separate fully
frozen, matched-adaptation, and from-training estimands across full and scarce
rotary budgets and a bidirectional $3$D video DiT (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We decompose a finite RoPE table into support $(a,R)$ and interior
allocation $z$, then causally identify $z$ with a paired three-seed
fixed-support intervention; target-aware support retargeting reverses the tested
ordering and establishes that the two coordinates interact.
(ii) We give an exact phase-invariant account of the full sin/cos basis: a
spectral-budget identity, a slow-frequency collapse law, the weight--table
co-adaptation boundary, and a frozen-transplant obstruction.
(iii) We solve a convex allocation surrogate in closed form to obtain \evq{}
with no learned positional parameters, unique within the stated surrogate.
(iv) We establish behavioural consequences across fully frozen,
matched-adaptation, and from-training interventions, including long-context
likelihood, downstream, retrieval, causal source-use, and in-window results,
while keeping their methods and estimands separate.
