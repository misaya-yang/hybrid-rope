\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RoPE specifies how a frequency rotates a query or key; it does not specify how
a finite attention head should allocate its frequencies. Each head receives
exactly $K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs. The familiar scalar base determines the whole
table only after one additionally assumes geometric spacing, thereby coupling
the spectral interval to a particular allocation rule. We separate these two
choices by writing
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $(a,R)$ is sampled support and $z$ is the normalised interior allocation.
Standard geometric RoPE fixes $z_k=k/(K-1)$. Support says which frequencies are
available; allocation says how a finite head spends them. We ask whether the
second choice changes trained behaviour when the first is held fixed, then turn
the answer into a closed-form construction.

This distinction matters because geometric spacing is highly redundant at the
slow end. For $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and $K{=}64$, the $23$ pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions yet have block-whitened
R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. These channels can still carry content, but
as positional functions they converge toward the same two-dimensional
subspace. A geometric table therefore spends more than one third of its rotary
pairs on near-duplicate slow positional directions. This is the spectral-budget
problem studied here.

Long-context methods can change realised phases, sampled support, or interior
placement, often more than one at once. Section~\ref{sec:related} classifies
range transport, scalar-base grids, learned tables, and broader positional
operators under this decomposition. The control below instead pins both
endpoints and the log-span before moving the interior frequencies.

It can. In a paired three-seed control, anchored \evq{} moves only $30$
interior frequencies relative to a geometric allocation at the fixed training
support. Every seed favours the non-uniform allocation at every evaluated OOD
length (\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}). The arms share endpoints, initialisation,
token
order, optimiser, and training budget, so no scalar base can reproduce the
intervention. Interior allocation $z$ is therefore a consequential training
variable, not a disguised base change.

A mature-checkpoint control makes the same variable visible after pretraining.
On a released $1.485$B OLMo checkpoint at $16$K, with support,
attention amplitude, data, decoder, and weights fixed, replacing a same-support
geometric allocation by the tested derived allocation raises nine-task RULER
macro (20 rows/task) from $0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$; the paired evaluation-row
difference is $59.92$ points with interval $[54.88,64.80]$
(App.~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support}). The common support already extends the
Native slow endpoint by a factor of four, so the contrast is how its interior
is spent. A coarse projected ramp reaches $61.04\%$ and matches the derived
profile within evaluation-row uncertainty. Two distinct reallocations recover
the checkpoint, exposing the allocation axis directly; neither is the Cosh
table used in the training experiments.

\evq{} turns the identified degree of freedom into a deployable table. The
unique minimizer of a stated convex surrogate has a Cosh density, and its
inverse CDF gives every frequency in closed form. The construction adds no
learned positional parameters and leaves the RoPE operator, architecture, and
optimiser unchanged.

From-scratch MHA and MLA, full-parameter continuation, low-rank adaptation,
frozen checkpoints, and a bidirectional $3$D video DiT then track this design
variable across the model-building lifecycle.

The geometry makes the finite-basis constraint exact; trained-model ordering
still comes from controlled comparisons. Each rotary frequency contributes a
two-dimensional sin/cos subspace, and Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} converts their
mean phase-invariant redundancy into effective dimension while
Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse} characterises slow-frequency collapse.
Table~\ref{tab:coadapt} then shows that higher static rank can accompany a
catastrophic frozen mismatch, and Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} gives the exact
limit of position-independent invertible Q/K compensation.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We identify interior allocation as a training variable with a paired
three-seed intervention that fixes the sampled support and every scalar-base
degree of freedom.
(ii) We give a phase-invariant spectral-budget geometry for the full sin/cos
basis: an exact effective-dimension identity, a slow-frequency collapse law,
and a frozen-transplant obstruction.
(iii) We derive \evq{} as the closed-form solution of a stated convex surrogate
and show the consequences of its zero-parameter allocation across architectures,
training stages, scales, and pretrained checkpoints.
