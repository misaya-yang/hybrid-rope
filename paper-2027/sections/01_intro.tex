\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Most RoPE optimisation is described through a scalar base, a spectral range,
or a transform of the phases realised at longer positions. That framing leaves
a separate finite-table choice implicit: where a head places phase resolution
among its exactly $K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs. A scalar base determines the table
only after geometric spacing has fixed this allocation. We separate the choices
by writing
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes the fixed $K$ rotary pairs, $a$ sets support location, $R$
its span, and $z$ the normalised interior allocation; geometric RoPE fixes
$z_k=k/(K-1)$. The third coordinate $z$
decides which phase scales the fixed $K$ samples occupy and where resolution is
placed. We test it with support, $K$, and the RoPE operator fixed, then derive a
closed-form construction.

This distinction matters because geometric spacing is highly redundant at the
slow end. For $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and $K{=}64$, the $23$ pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions yet have block-whitened
R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. These channels can still carry content, but
as positional functions they converge toward the same two-dimensional
subspace. A geometric table therefore spends more than one third of its rotary
pairs on near-duplicate slow positional directions. This is the spectral-budget
problem studied here.

Long-context methods can change realised phases, sampled support, or interior
placement, often more than one at once. Learned-frequency methods also show
that the geometric exponent sequence need not remain fixed. Section~\ref{sec:related}
classifies these interventions under the decomposition above. We isolate the
missing variable by pinning both endpoints and the log-span, then moving only
the interior allocation.

The strongest practical consequence appears after pretraining. At matched
support, amplitude, checkpoint, rows, and decoder, changing only $z$ raises
OLMo nine-task $16$K RULER from $0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$, without parameter updates
(\S\ref{sec:exp-frozen-support}). A separate fixed-support training control
answers the causal question at model construction: across three paired
$151.9$M seeds, moving only the $30$ interiors improves every tested OOD length
in every seed (\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}). Together these interventions show
that $z$ is neither a disguised base change nor a coordinate that disappears
once weights have been trained.

Optimising a convex surrogate over $z$ yields \emph{Exact Variational
Quantization with a Cosh density} (\evq{}), a closed-form table whose
inverse-CDF quantiles require no learned positional parameters. The solution is
unique within that surrogate.

We test the same coordinate in three complementary regimes: fully frozen
checkpoints, pretrained models under matched low-rank adaptation, and
from-initialisation or continued full-parameter training. They ask whether $z$
acts without weight updates, can be exploited after limited adaptation, and is
learned through weight--table co-adaptation, respectively. Full and scarce
rotary budgets and a bidirectional $3$D video DiT extend these tests across
architectures and beyond autoregressive text (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We expose $z$ as a third finite-RoPE coordinate---phase-resolution placement
within fixed support and $K$---and causally identify its effect in a paired
three-seed training intervention; an independent frozen-checkpoint pure-$z$
intervention shows that the same coordinate remains active after pretraining.
(ii) We give an exact phase-invariant account of the full sin/cos basis: a
spectral-budget identity, a slow-frequency collapse law, the weight--table
co-adaptation boundary, and a frozen-transplant obstruction.
(iii) We solve a convex allocation surrogate in closed form to obtain \evq{}
with no learned positional parameters, and demonstrate allocation control in
fully frozen, matched-adaptation, and from-training interventions.
