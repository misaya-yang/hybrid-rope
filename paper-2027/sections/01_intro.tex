\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RoPE has a spectral budget. Each attention head receives exactly
$K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs, but the familiar scalar base specifies only the
spectral interval they occupy. Formally, for an ordered table we write
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $a=x_0$, $R=x_{K-1}-x_0$, and $z$ is the normalised allocation inside the
sampled log-frequency span. Standard geometric RoPE fixes $z_k=k/(K-1)$, so
the base determines the entire table only within that geometric family.

\paragraph{Our view: support says which frequencies are available; allocation
says how a finite head spends them.}
Support fixes what frequencies are available; $z$ determines how the finite
channels use them. The distinction is most consequential at finite $K$, the
regime in which every attention head operates.

The same parameterisation clarifies where existing methods act. Position
interpolation, YaRN, and LongRoPE transport a trained spectrum toward a target
range \citep{chen2024position,peng2024yarn,ding2024longrope,shang2025longrope2};
FMRoPE changes scalar support while retaining a geometric exponent grid
\citep{oka2026fmrope}; LeRoPE and AdaRoPE optimize broader frequency tables
\citep{karypis2026lerope,wang2026adarope}. These interventions may combine
coordinates. In contrast, our controlled question fixes $a$ and $R$:
\emph{does moving only the $K-2$ interior entries of $z$ change what a model
learns?}

It does. Across three paired $151.9$M training seeds, moving only $30$
frequencies changes mean OOD NLL by $-0.281/-0.176/-0.146$ at
$2\times/4\times/8\times$, at a $+0.026$ in-window cost
(\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}). Every seed agrees at every OOD length. The
intervention is an affine support normalisation of the deployed Cosh quantiles,
which preserves their normalised $z$ while matching the uniform baseline's
sampled endpoints. Moreover, a $50.9$M factorial spanning $12$ structural
configurations finds the same non-uniform direction for both $1.25\times$ Cosh
($10/12$) and a deformation-matched exponential ($9/12$). Allocation is thus
identifiable beyond one formula or one training seed.

The controlled result establishes that the axis exists; Figure~\ref{fig:evidence-overview}b
shows why it matters. In a $432$M MLA transformer with only $K{=}16$, Geo and
\evq{} are nearly tied at the $8$K training length, whereas all three seeds
favour \evq{} at $16$K. The crossover also survives later training stages and
larger models. A $750$M full-parameter continuation from a shared $2$K Geo
checkpoint changes $16$K PPL from $45.1$ to $24.4$, and a $1.485$B
from-initialisation trajectory crosses at $8$K and favours \evq{} on $126/128$
held-out documents at $16$K. Finally, the Q/K adaptation in
Figure~\ref{fig:evidence-overview}c transfers multi-hop QA beyond its physical
training window, while the $8$B intervention shows that the remote source is
causally used. These larger protocols test persistence and capability; the
fixed-support control identifies $z$.

Why can rearranging a fixed support change a trained model? Each rotary
frequency contributes a two-dimensional sin/cos subspace
(\S\ref{sec:theory}). Canonical correlations measure redundancy without
choosing a content phase, and block whitening yields the finite-basis identity
\[
r_2(R)=\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\]
In a standard $4$K table, $23$ slow pairs occupy $46$ nominal dimensions but
have Rényi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. This is a static redundancy statement,
not a task-loss predictor. The missing link is co-adaptation: a weight--table
crossing leaves the two self-consistent systems usable but breaks both post-hoc
swaps. Consistently, unequal frequency multisets cannot be absorbed by fixed,
position-independent invertible Q/K maps. The table is a coordinate system
whose coefficients are learned with it.

A convex modelling surrogate then supplies a constructive point on this axis:
the Cosh density and its inverse-CDF table are closed form, with geometric RoPE
as the $\tau\to0$ limit. \evq{} is reproducible and adds no learned positional
parameters.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A support/allocation parameterisation and a three-seed fixed-support
intervention identify interior allocation as a training variable.
(ii) Phase-invariant full-RoPE geometry yields an exact effective-rank identity;
an exact frozen-transplant obstruction and a weight--table crossing connect
that basis to training co-adaptation.
(iii) A closed-form analytic allocation produces the predicted
effective-context shift in a three-seed scarce-budget test, with training
evidence through $1.485$B and capability evidence at $1.485$B and $8$B.
