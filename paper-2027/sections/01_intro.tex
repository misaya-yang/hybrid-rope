\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A finite RoPE table has a spectral budget: its exactly $K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs
must allocate phase resolution across scales. On a frozen billion-scale OLMo
checkpoint, a model-relative derived
allocation raises nine-task $16$K RULER from $0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$, while
official Transformers YaRN scores $7.94\%$ on the same rows; a coarse
task-label-free allocation reproduces the recovery without training or parameter
updates. These model-relative frozen tables are separate from the \evq{}
construction used in training and adaptation.

Most RoPE optimisation is instead described through a scalar base, a spectral
range, or a transform of the phases realised at longer positions. That framing
leaves a separate finite-table choice implicit: where the fixed pairs place
phase resolution inside their sampled range. We separate the choices by writing
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes the fixed $K$ rotary pairs, $a$ sets support location, $R$
its span, and $z$ the normalised interior allocation; geometric RoPE fixes
$z_k=k/(K-1)$. Thus support $(a,R)$ and interior allocation $z$ are distinct
coordinates: $z$ decides which phase scales the fixed samples occupy and where
resolution is placed.

We study structured allocation through three distinct intervention routes. A
fully frozen route installs model-relative derived or coarse allocations with no
parameter updates. Matched low-rank adaptation tests whether pretrained
representations can exploit an \evq{} basis, yielding task-family length transfer
at $1.485$B and causal remote-source use at $8$B. From training, weights co-adapt
to the installed table: in a $750$M continuation, \evq{} lowers $16$K PPL from
$45.1$ to $24.4$ and raises strict $8$K autoregressive exact retrieval from
$0\%$ to $77.5\%$. These routes share a design principle; their estimands remain
separate.

The causal foundation separates allocation from support selection. Across three
paired $151.9$M seeds, anchored \evq{} and paper-faithful FMRoPE share sampled
extrema and log-span while only the $30$ interiors move; every OOD length favours
the reallocation in every seed. In a separate target-aware condition, the
declared $L_{\mathrm{target}}$ may retarget support and the ordering reverses.
Together the two conditions identify support and structured allocation as
distinct but interacting controls of the same finite table
(\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}).

This distinction matters because geometric spacing is highly redundant at the
slow end. For $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and $K{=}64$, the $23$ pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions yet have block-whitened
R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. These channels can still carry content, but
as positional functions they converge toward the same two-dimensional
subspace. A geometric table therefore spends more than one third of its rotary
pairs on near-duplicate slow positional directions. This is the spectral-budget
problem studied here.

Optimising a convex surrogate over $z$ yields \emph{Exact Variational
Quantization with a Cosh density} (\evq{}), a closed-form table whose
inverse-CDF quantiles require no learned positional parameters. The solution is
unique within that surrogate.

Figure~\ref{fig:spectral-budget-overview} defines the design object, separates
the support and allocation interventions, and shows the mature zero-training
consequence. The experiments then follow the practical order above---fully
frozen, matched adaptation, and from training---across full and scarce rotary
budgets and a bidirectional $3$D video DiT (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We demonstrate structured allocation across three model-building stages,
led by a zero-training mature-checkpoint intervention and supported by strong
long-context likelihood, downstream, retrieval, and causal source-use results.
(ii) We separate support $(a,R)$ from interior allocation $z$: a paired
three-seed fixed-support intervention identifies allocation, while target-aware
support retargeting reverses the tested ordering and establishes their
interaction.
(iii) We give an exact phase-invariant account of the full sin/cos basis: a
spectral-budget identity, a slow-frequency collapse law, the weight--table
co-adaptation boundary, and a frozen-transplant obstruction.
(iv) We solve a convex allocation surrogate in closed form to obtain \evq{}
with no learned positional parameters, and test that construction across
training and matched-adaptation regimes while keeping the separate frozen route
explicit.
