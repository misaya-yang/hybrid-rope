\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The frequency range locates a RoPE spectrum; allocation distributes its finite rotary budget. A base specifies the range of a standard geometric table \citep{su2024roformer,liu2024scaling,xu2024base}, while the placement of its rotary pairs determines which positional functions the model receives. Interior allocation remains a design choice when the fastest and slowest frequencies are held exactly fixed.

Recent work studies distinct parts of this design problem: frequency-band and entropy measurements characterize learned usage \citep{oka2026fmrope,oka2026frequencyentropy}; LeRoPE and AdaRoPE learn shared or head-specific frequencies \citep{karypis2026lerope,wang2026adarope}; context-extension methods transform a native spectrum for a target length \citep{peng2024yarn,tian2026mrrope}; and GRAPE and Selective RoPE extend the positional map \citep{zhang2026grape,movahedi2026selectiverope}. We isolate how a finite table populates a fixed range, asking \emph{how interior allocation changes the supplied basis, its learned use, and model behavior}.

We combine controlled interventions, full-pair geometry, and concrete designs. Figure~\ref{fig:spectral-budget-overview} starts from paired $151.9$M training: changing only $30$ interior exponents improves extended-length loss in every seed. Runtime range retargeting reverses the ordering, and crossed evaluations reveal strong weight--table compatibility. The geometric analysis characterizes the positional directions being allocated; training and frozen-model experiments show how that freedom becomes useful.

Each rotary pair supplies a complete sine--cosine space. Canonical overlap and an exact effective-rank identity show how distinct frequencies can concentrate into shared positional directions over a finite window. Learned projections associate these directions with coordinate slots. This connects the supplied basis to its use by a model and motivates two practical settings: constructing a table that the model can learn, and adjusting the native table of an existing checkpoint. Analytic allocations, complete-answer generation, low-dimensional profile transport, and natural QA provide complementary evidence of this design freedom's value.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identifying allocation effects.} We separate sampled support from interior exponent placement and isolate its effect through paired training, multiple allocation families, and fixed-support frozen interventions.
\item \textbf{Connecting positional geometry and learning.} We characterize full sine--cosine overlap, its effective-rank identity and slow-frequency limit, and use range retargeting, crossed evaluation, and slot interventions to establish how learned weights use an allocation.
\item \textbf{Constructing useful allocations.} We develop analytic learning-time and native-relative deployment constructions, with gains in language modeling, complete generation, and natural QA across their respective matched protocols.
\end{itemize}
