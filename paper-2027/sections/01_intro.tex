\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RoPE has a spectral budget. Each attention head receives only
$K=\drot/2$ rotary frequency channels, and those channels must be placed before
the model sees a token. Standard geometric RoPE \citep{su2024roformer} makes a
specific allocation: it spaces the channels uniformly in log-frequency,
$\omega_i=b^{-2i/\drot}$. Because this table is inherited across model families
--- from LLaMA-3 and Qwen2.5 to DeepSeek-V3 and video DiTs
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3,qwen2024qwen25,deepseekv3,videorope2025} --- its
allocation is easily mistaken for part of the rotary operator itself.

Recent work makes the distinction consequential. Positional interpolation,
YaRN, and LongRoPE-style search transport a realised spectrum toward a target
context \citep{chen2024position,peng2024yarn,ding2024longrope,
shang2025longrope2}; FMRoPE retargets the geometric band through its base
\citep{oka2026fmrope}; LeRoPE instead learns the individual frequencies and
finds stable non-geometric tables \citep{karypis2026lerope}. Range, transport,
and learning all show that the default table is not sacrosanct. They leave a
sharper question: \emph{at a fixed spectral range, is the allocation of the
finite interior channels an independent training-time variable, and can it be
chosen in closed form?}

We answer this in three steps. First we make the budget exact
(\S\ref{sec:theory}). A rotary pair is a two-dimensional subspace, not a cosine
feature, so the right measure of redundancy between two channels is the
canonical correlation between their subspaces; block-whitening all $K$ pairs
gives the identity $r_2 = 2K/(1+(K{-}1)\bar c)$, in which the nominal budget
$2K$ is deflated by the mean redundancy $\bar c$ alone. Geometric RoPE spends a
great deal on redundancy: at $L{=}4096$ with $b{=}5{\times}10^5$ and $K{=}64$,
the $24$ slowest pairs occupy $48$ nominal dimensions but only $2.00$ effective
ones. Because $\bar c$ is the single quantity allocation can move once $K$ and
the endpoints are pinned, this turns \emph{interior allocation} into a
well-posed variable rather than a heuristic.

Second, we test whether that variable is real for a trained model. In an
exact-range identification experiment (\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}) two arms share
the sampled highest and lowest frequencies, log-span, initialisation, token
order, optimiser, training budget, and evaluation anchors; only the $K{-}2=30$
interior frequencies move. After training, endpoint-normalised cosh minus the
uniform-in-log grid changes OOD NLL by $-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K
in the seed-$42$ matched pair, and a separate $12$-configuration, three-seed
factorial replicates the direction in $10/12$ and $9/12$ configurations for two
structurally different non-uniform schedules.

Third, we bound what the geometry explains. Static identifiability is necessary
but not sufficient: in a frozen $50$M diagnostic, substituting the \evq{} table
at inference raises stable rank from $4.57$ to $12.54$ while perplexity degrades
from $7.14$ to $76.20$, and the table$\times$weights interaction on LM loss is
$5.9\times$ the size of either main effect. We prove that this is structural ---
no fixed, position-independent invertible Q/K map can absorb a nontrivial table
change exactly --- so a table is a coordinate system fixed before training, and
the weights learn coefficients in it.

Against this backdrop \evq{} is a construction, not the theory's unique answer.
Representing a table by a density over log-frequency and minimising a convex
collision surrogate gives a cosh density whose inverse CDF quantises to a
closed-form warp of the RoPE inverse-frequency initialisation
(\S\ref{sec:construction}). It adds no learned parameters, auxiliary loss, or
search, and recovers geometric RoPE as $\tau\to0$; we use it as a reproducible,
zero-parameter intervention on the allocation axis.

The effect survives beyond mechanism-scale controls (\S\ref{sec:exp-mature}). On
OLMo-2 ($1.485$B), Native and \evq{} receive the same $300$-step Q/K-only
continuation, data, module budget, and $4$K/$8$K/$16$K phase exposure; the active
method variable is the frequency table. At $8$K, Native/\evq{} scores are
$2.02/31.63\%$ on RULER and $0/17.5\%$ exact on held-out 2WikiMultiHopQA.
Matching long-phase exposure therefore does not erase the frequency-substrate
effect. On LLaMA-3-8B, a separate matched physical-$8$K continuation moves $16$K
RULER from $0.295\%$ to $14.03\%$.
