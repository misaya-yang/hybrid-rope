\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RoPE specifies how frequencies rotate queries and keys, not where a finite head
places phase resolution among its exactly $K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs. A scalar
base determines the table only after assuming geometric spacing, coupling the
spectral interval to one allocation rule. We separate the choices by writing
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $a$ sets support location, $R$ its span, and $z$ the normalised interior
allocation; geometric RoPE fixes $z_k=k/(K-1)$. The third coordinate $z$
decides which phase scales the fixed $K$ samples occupy and where resolution is
placed. We test it with support, $K$, and the RoPE operator fixed, then derive a
closed-form construction.

This distinction matters because geometric spacing is highly redundant at the
slow end. For $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and $K{=}64$, the $23$ pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions yet have block-whitened
R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. These channels can still carry content, but
as positional functions they converge toward the same two-dimensional
subspace. A geometric table therefore spends more than one third of its rotary
pairs on near-duplicate slow positional directions. This is the spectral-budget
problem studied here.

Long-context methods can change realised phases, sampled support, or interior
placement, often more than one at once. Section~\ref{sec:related} classifies
range transport, scalar-base grids, learned tables, and broader positional
operators under this decomposition. The control below instead pins both
endpoints and the log-span before moving the interior frequencies.

The strongest practical consequence appears after pretraining. At matched
support, amplitude, checkpoint, rows, and decoder, changing only $z$ raises
OLMo nine-task $16$K RULER from $0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$, with no optimisation
(\S\ref{sec:exp-frozen-support}). A separate fixed-support training control
answers the causal question at model construction: across three paired
$151.9$M seeds, moving only the $30$ interiors improves every tested OOD length
in every seed (\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}). Together these interventions show
that $z$ is neither a disguised base change nor a coordinate that disappears
once weights have been trained.

\evq{} is the bounded constructive witness: inverse-CDF quantiles of a stated
convex surrogate give a closed-form table with no learned positional parameters,
unique for that surrogate.

Three protocol-separated routes establish actionability after pretraining with
zero optimisation, persistence through matched low-rank adaptation, and
co-adaptation from training; a bidirectional $3$D video DiT adds modality breadth
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We expose $z$ as a third finite-RoPE coordinate---phase-resolution placement
within fixed support and $K$---and causally identify its behavioural effect with
a paired three-seed intervention fixing every scalar-base degree of freedom.
(ii) We give an exact phase-invariant account of the full sin/cos basis: a
spectral-budget identity, a slow-frequency collapse law, the weight--table
co-adaptation boundary, and a frozen-transplant obstruction.
(iii) We derive \evq{} as a closed-form, zero-learned-parameter witness for a
stated convex surrogate and show across zero-training, matched-adaptation, and
from-training routes that $z$ remains a controllable determinant of
long-context behaviour in the tested protocols.
