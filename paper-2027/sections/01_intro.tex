\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A RoPE table distributes a finite set of rotary frequencies across a chosen range. How should those frequencies be placed to improve extrapolation? Existing extensions change their placement through bandwise scaling, mixed-radix conversion or learned frequencies \citep{peng2024yarn,tian2026mrrope,karypis2026lerope}. We identify the independent value of internal allocation $z$ and derive explicit constructions that improve learning and zero-training deployment.

With initialization, data, optimization and actual frequency endpoints fixed, moving only $30$ interior frequencies improves $2\times$, $4\times$ and $8\times$ extrapolation loss in all three seeds. Frozen-model controls also show gains at fixed support and differences at equal total displacement. Thus internal shape carries useful information beyond the range and amount of frequency change (Fig.~\ref{fig:method-overview}).

Changing $z$ changes the positional basis: distinct frequencies can supply overlapping directions over a finite window. We characterize this structure and design frequency spacing in two settings: Cosh specifies a training-time table through a density objective; TailSpline smooths the transition into a frozen model's fully interpolated low-frequency tail. Both retain the rotary operator and construct their tables without fitted model statistics.

The resulting benefits reach beyond a small controlled experiment. Cosh improves multi-seed MLA extrapolation and $750$M continued training. TailSpline improves frozen Llama-$3$-$8$B and OLMo on RULER-13 and NIAH, while the symmetric boundary-matched construction (BM) raises frozen OLMo's five-task natural-QA F1 from $21.62$ to $25.44$. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Identification.} Fixed-support training establishes the extrapolation value of $z$; frozen controls distinguish internal shape from range and total displacement.
\item \textbf{Characterization and construction.} Full-pair geometry describes the positional structure changed by $z$; density and boundary objectives yield explicit Cosh and TailSpline allocations.
\item \textbf{Learning and zero-training validation.} Matched evaluations establish language-modeling, retrieval and natural-QA gains, with training, adaptation and frozen protocols distinguished.
\end{itemize}
