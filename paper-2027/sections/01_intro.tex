\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RoPE has a spectral budget. Each attention head receives only
$K=\drot/2$ rotary frequency pairs, fixed before training, and standard RoPE
spends them on the geometric table $\omega_i=b^{-2i/\drot}$
\citep{su2024roformer}. That choice is reused across model families---including
LLaMA-3, Qwen2.5, DeepSeek-V3, and video DiTs
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3,qwen2024qwen25,deepseekv3,videorope2025}---so the
table is easily mistaken for part of the rotary operator rather than a design
decision.

Long-context methods expose several distinct decisions. Position
interpolation, YaRN, and LongRoPE transport a trained spectrum toward a target
range \citep{chen2024position,peng2024yarn,ding2024longrope,shang2025longrope2};
FMRoPE changes the scalar base while retaining a geometric exponent grid
\citep{oka2026fmrope}; LeRoPE learns individual frequencies and obtains stable
non-geometric tables \citep{karypis2026lerope}. We isolate the variable between
these cases: \emph{with the sampled endpoints and log-frequency span fixed,
does the placement of the finite interior channels still change what a model
learns?}

It does. Our central claim is that interior allocation is an independent
training-time variable even at fixed spectral range. We first make the finite
budget precise (\S\ref{sec:theory}). A rotary frequency contributes a
two-dimensional sin/cos subspace, not one cosine feature. Canonical
correlations between these subspaces give a phase-invariant redundancy
$c_{ij}$, and block-whitening all pairs yields
\[
r_2(R)=\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\]
This is an exact identity for the static positional basis. It also reveals a
sharp failure of geometric allocation: at $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and
$K{=}64$, the $24$ slowest pairs span $48$ nominal dimensions but have stable
rank $2.00$. They are mutually redundant, not necessarily unused.

We then identify allocation in trained models
(\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}). In a $151.9$M matched pair, the sampled highest and
lowest frequencies, log-span, initialisation, token order, optimiser, budget,
and evaluation anchors are identical; only $K{-}2=30$ interior frequencies
move. An endpoint-normalised Cosh diagnostic---not the deployed midpoint
\evq{} grid---minus the uniform-in-log grid changes OOD NLL by
$-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K. A separate $50.9$M factorial over
two bases, two training lengths, three head dimensions, and three seeds finds
the non-uniform direction in $10/12$ configurations for the pre-specified
$1.25\times$ Cosh and $9/12$ for a deformation-matched exponential. The two
analytic shapes are not empirically
separated; the result identifies the allocation axis, not a unique curve.

Static geometry alone cannot select a table for a trained model. Crossing two
$50$M weight sets with the geometric and \evq{} runtime tables raises the
static stable rank from $4.57$ to $12.54$ in the worst mismatch while PPL
degrades from $7.14$ to $76.20$. The two self-consistent systems remain at
$7.14$ and $7.16$, whereas both post-hoc swaps fail. We prove the exact
counterpart: unequal frequency multisets cannot be absorbed by fixed,
position-independent invertible Q/K maps. The table is therefore a coordinate
system to which weights co-adapt during training, not a plug-in statistic.

Within this theory, \evq{} has a constructive role. A convex
surrogate for allocating density in log-frequency has a unique Cosh minimiser;
inverse-CDF quantisation gives a closed-form table with geometric RoPE as the
$\tau\to0$ limit. This supplies a reproducible, zero-learned-parameter
construction and controlled intervention. It does not claim full-RoPE,
language-model, or universal optimality.

Finally, the table-conditioned effect persists at mature scale
(\S\ref{sec:exp-mature}). OLMo-2 Native and \evq{} systems receive the same
$300$-step Q/K-only continuation and the same $4$K/$8$K/$16$K phase exposure;
their $8$K RULER scores are $2.02/31.63\%$, and held-out 2Wiki exact is
$0/17.5\%$. A separate matched LLaMA-3-8B physical-$8$K continuation reaches
$0.295/14.03\%$ RULER at $16$K. Together, the theory, exact-range controls,
co-adaptation diagnostic, and mature-model comparisons establish one chain:
finite interior allocation changes both the positional basis and the trained
system that uses it.

The exact-range pair owns causal identification, the three-seed factorial owns
cross-configuration replication, and the mature-model runs test scale rather
than estimate the primary effect.
