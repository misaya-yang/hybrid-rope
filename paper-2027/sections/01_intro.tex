\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
For a model trained to length $L$, $sL$ specifies a target window; quality
within that window determines its usefulness. RoPE's base sets a frequency
range, while internal placement determines the positional basis. We study
how this allocation affects model quality.

Frequency design includes frozen scaling policies such as YaRN and MrRoPE
and learned frequencies such as LeRoPE
\citep{peng2024yarn,tian2026mrrope,karypis2026lerope}.
Paired experiments hold the
frequency endpoints fixed and show that interior placement improves both learned
extrapolation and frozen-model performance. Additional controls distinguish
its effects from total frequency displacement and learned coordinate assignment.

Full sine--cosine geometry reveals how distinct frequencies can supply
overlapping positional directions. For frozen extension, a model already uses
its native frequencies; we introduce TailSpline to allocate extension through
a smooth connection to the low-frequency tail. A single static table improves
clean RULER over MrRoPE-Pro at both $2L$ and $4L$, by $3.39$ and $11.72$
percentage points.
Cosh provides a complementary frequency transport for extrapolation, used in
paired training to establish the wider value of changing $z$.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Range and allocation have distinct, interacting effects.}
At fixed endpoints, internal placement improves model performance;
changing the evaluation range can reverse the preferred allocation.
\item \textbf{Positional structure and learned use are distinct.}
Complete rotary pairs expose finite-window direction overlap. Task gains
with lower positional effective rank, together with coordinate and table
interventions, distinguish this structure from its learned use.
\item \textbf{Analytic allocation improves task quality.}
TailSpline gives a closed-form static extension with clean RULER gains at
both $2L$ and $4L$. Cosh provides a supporting extrapolation transport.
\end{itemize}
