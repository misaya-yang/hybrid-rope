\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

RoPE specifies how a frequency rotates a query or key; it does not specify how
a finite attention head should allocate its frequencies. Each head receives
exactly $K=\drot/2$ rotary pairs. The familiar scalar base determines the whole
table only after one additionally assumes geometric spacing, thereby coupling
the spectral interval to a particular allocation rule. We separate these two
choices by writing
\begin{equation}
x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k,
\qquad z_0=0,\quad z_{K-1}=1,
\label{eq:table-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $(a,R)$ is sampled support and $z$ is the normalised interior allocation.
Standard geometric RoPE fixes $z_k=k/(K-1)$. Support says which frequencies are
available; allocation says how a finite head spends them.

This distinction matters because geometric spacing is highly redundant at the
slow end. For $L{=}4096$, $b{=}5{\times}10^5$, and $K{=}64$, the $23$ pairs with
$\omega L\le1$ occupy $46$ nominal dimensions yet have block-whitened
R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. These channels can still carry content, but
as positional functions they converge toward the same two-dimensional
subspace. A geometric table therefore spends more than one third of its rotary
pairs on near-duplicate slow positional directions. This is the spectral-budget
problem studied here.

Long-context methods already produce many non-geometric frequency tables, but
they generally change support and interior placement together. Position
interpolation rescales the entire spectrum; YaRN and LongRoPE transport
channels toward a target range
\citep{chen2024position,peng2024yarn,ding2024longrope,shang2025longrope2};
FMRoPE changes scalar support while retaining a geometric exponent grid
\citep{oka2026fmrope}. Learned methods such as LeRoPE and AdaRoPE optimize a
broader table jointly with model weights
\citep{karypis2026lerope,wang2026adarope}. Because support and interior
placement change together, these comparisons do not isolate the effect of $z$
at fixed support: if both sampled endpoints and the log-frequency span are
fixed, can moving only the interior frequencies change what a model learns?

It can. In a paired three-seed control, anchored \evq{} moves
only $30$ interior frequencies relative to FMRoPE. Every seed favours
the non-uniform allocation at every evaluated OOD length
(\S\ref{sec:exp-identify}). Because the endpoints are identical, no scalar base
can reproduce the intervention. Interior allocation $z$ is therefore a genuine
training variable, not a disguised base change.

We make the identified degree of freedom constructive with \evq{}. A convex
variational surrogate of the collision geometry has a unique Cosh minimizer
whose inverse CDF gives the frequency table in closed form. Evaluated under the
closed-form cosine-feature kernel, the resulting allocations reduce collision
and increase the kernel's Shannon effective rank in every tested configuration
(App.~\ref{sec:surrogate-validation}). The solution concentrates samples toward
phase-resolved fast frequencies: at $K{=}64$ and $\tau{=}4$, $55$ of the $64$
midpoint quantiles lie in the fast half of the log-frequency span. The RoPE
operator, architecture, and optimiser remain unchanged, and the construction
adds no learned positional parameters.

The controlled experiment identifies $z$; the remaining studies test the
resulting table under realistic model-building paths. Under scarce-channel $432$M
MLA, all three seeds favour \evq{} at $16$K. In a separate $454$M three-seed
MHA study, the same fixed \rs{} operator reaches $100\%$ teacher-forced
retrieval on an \evq{} substrate versus $61\%$ on Geo, showing that range
transport inherits the table learned by the weights. Separately, the
in-window-to-long-range crossover seen in MLA reappears after $750$M
full-parameter continuation from a shared checkpoint and in a
same-initialisation $1.485$B from-initialisation comparison. A frozen
fixed-support intervention on mature OLMo and Qwen checkpoints then changes
only $z$, showing that the same coordinate remains consequential after
pretraining. Finally, matched adaptation carries multi-hop QA beyond the
$1.485$B arm's physical training window, while an $8$B decode-attention
intervention shows that the distant source is causally used with prompt tokens
and rotary indices fixed. Together, these studies carry the fixed-support
finding from full-parameter training through $1.485$B to mature-model
adaptation at $8$B.

The theory explains why this pattern is coherent. Each rotary frequency is a
two-dimensional sin/cos subspace. Canonical correlations expose overlap between
these subspaces, and block whitening yields the exact finite-basis identity
\[
r_2(R)=\frac{2K}{1+(K-1)\bar c}.
\]
Slow frequencies collapse toward a common subspace, whereas a frozen
weight--table crossing shows that improving static geometry after training can
still destroy perplexity. The missing link is co-adaptation: the weights learn
coefficients in the frequency basis they see during training. Consistently,
unequal frequency multisets cannot be absorbed by fixed,
position-independent invertible Q/K maps.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We expose support and interior allocation as distinct coordinates and
identify allocation with a three-seed fixed-support intervention.
(ii) We characterize the resulting full-RoPE basis through a phase-invariant
effective-rank identity, low-frequency collapse, and an exact frozen-transplant
obstruction.
(iii) We derive the unique closed-form optimum of the allocation surrogate,
verify its direction under the exact cosine-feature kernel, and show the
construction's consequences across scarce-channel training, range composition,
full-parameter continuation, training through $1.485$B, and mature-checkpoint
interventions and adaptation.
