\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Standard RoPE ties frequency placement to a geometric grid: changing the base changes the range but leaves normalized spacing uniform. Internal exponent allocation $z$ opens this spacing to design. Existing extensions alter it through bandwise scaling, mixed-radix conversion or learned frequencies \citep{peng2024yarn,tian2026mrrope,karypis2026lerope}. We isolate the value of allocation and derive explicit constructions that improve language modeling, retrieval and natural QA during learning and zero-training deployment.

Controlled experiments identify this value. With initialization, data, optimization and actual frequency endpoints fixed, moving $30$ interior frequencies improves $2\times$, $4\times$ and $8\times$ extrapolation loss in all three seeds. Frozen-model controls show gains at fixed support and differences at equal total displacement. Internal shape therefore matters beyond the range and amount of frequency change (Fig.~\ref{fig:method-overview}).

Changing $z$ changes the positional basis: distinct frequencies can supply overlapping directions over a finite window. We characterize this structure and design frequency spacing in two settings: Cosh specifies a training-time table through a density objective; TailSpline smooths the transition into a frozen model's fully interpolated low-frequency tail. Both retain the rotary operator and construct their tables without fitted model statistics.

Cosh improves multi-seed MLA and $750$M continued training. TailSpline improves frozen Llama-$3$-$8$B and OLMo on RULER-13 and NIAH; the symmetric construction BM raises OLMo's five-task natural-QA F1 from $21.62$ to $25.44$. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Allocation as a design variable.} Matched interventions demonstrate the value of internal frequency placement beyond range and total displacement.
\item \textbf{Structure and analytic designs.} Full-pair geometry characterizes finite-window overlap; density and boundary objectives yield closed-form Cosh and TailSpline tables.
\item \textbf{Learning and zero-training gains.} The constructions improve language modeling, retrieval and natural QA across training, continuation and frozen deployment.
\end{itemize}
