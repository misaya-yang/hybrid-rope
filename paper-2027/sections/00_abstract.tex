A finite rotary position embedding (RoPE) table chooses both a frequency range and an allocation of rotary pairs within that range. We study interior exponent allocation as a design freedom that organizes the positional basis, shapes what a model learns, and improves length generalization. Paired training at identical sampled endpoints isolates its effect: changing only 30 interior exponents improves extrapolation loss at $2\times$, $4\times$, and $8\times$ the training length in all three seeds. Full sine--cosine subspace analysis reveals how finite-window overlap concentrates positional directions, while crossed evaluations show that weights co-adapt to their allocation. Retargeting the runtime range reverses the ordering, connecting allocation to the scales at which a learned basis is used. We turn this perspective into analytic constructions and evaluate them across training, continuation, adaptation, and frozen deployment. EVQ-Cosh reduces $16$K perplexity from $138.8$ to $95.6$ in three-seed MLA training; intermediate-band redistribution improves five-task natural-QA F1 from $21.62$ to $25.44$ on a frozen OLMo checkpoint. Together, the controlled interventions, geometric analysis, and model results establish interior allocation as a consequential and usable dimension of RoPE design.
