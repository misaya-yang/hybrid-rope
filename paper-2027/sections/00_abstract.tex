Structured reallocation of RoPE's finite rotary budget produces large
long-context gains. On a frozen billion-scale OLMo checkpoint, a model-relative
derived allocation, separate from our training-time \evq{} construction,
raises nine-task $16$K RULER from $0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$
(official Transformers YaRN: $7.94\%$ on the same rows); a coarse task-label-free
allocation reproduces the recovery, with no training or parameter updates.
Under continued $750$M training, \evq{} lowers $16$K PPL from $45.1$ to $24.4$
and raises strict $8$K
autoregressive exact retrieval from $0\%$ to $77.5\%$; matched $1.485$B and $8$B
adaptations further yield task-family length transfer and causal remote-source
use. Writing $x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates sampled support $(a,R)$ from
interior allocation $z$. A paired three-seed fixed-support intervention isolates
the allocation effect; when a target-aware support policy may use
$L_{\mathrm{target}}$, the FMRoPE control reverses the tested ordering, identifying
support and allocation as distinct but interacting levers. Exact
phase-invariant sin/cos geometry explains how a fixed nominal
budget can collapse onto a much smaller positional basis, and a convex surrogate
yields closed-form zero-learned-parameter \evq{}, unique within that surrogate.
Across fully frozen, matched-adaptation, and from-training routes, structured
allocation turns a finite spectral budget into usable context.
