RoPE tables with the same frequency range can allocate their interior frequencies differently. We study the value of normalized exponent allocation $z$ through controlled interventions, positional geometry, and explicit constructions for learning and zero-training deployment. Against a matched geometric control, changing only $30$ interior frequencies at fixed support lowers $2\times$, $4\times$, and $8\times$ extrapolation loss in all three paired training seeds; separate frozen-model controls show task differences at matched support and equal total displacement. Full sine--cosine geometry reveals finite-window redundancy through an overlap--effective-rank identity and a shared slow-frequency limit. Cosh provides an analytic training-time allocation, reducing $16$K perplexity from $138.8$ to $95.6$ in three-seed MLA evaluation. For frozen deployment, we introduce TailSpline, the exact finite-grid solution of a one-sided boundary-smoothing objective. Without updating weights or fitting activations, it improves log-length-averaged depth-balanced RULER-13 scores over MrRoPE-Pro by $3.20$ points on Llama-$3$-$8$B and $49.23$ points on OLMo. Allocation design thus improves language modeling and task performance while retaining the rotary operator.
