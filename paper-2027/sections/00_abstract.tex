Rotary position embeddings specify both a frequency range and an allocation of a finite number of rotary pairs within that range. We study the second choice separately from range selection and from the weights that learn to use the resulting basis. In paired $151.9$M training runs with identical frequency endpoints, changing only the $30$ interior exponents lowers extrapolation NLL at every tested extended length in all three seeds. The ordering reverses under a specified runtime range-retargeting policy, and weights-by-table crossings show strong dependence on the training allocation. We characterize the supplied positional basis through full sine--cosine subspaces, establish their shared slow-frequency limit, and derive a Cosh allocation from an explicit convex design criterion. Matched training and adaptation evaluate this construction beyond the controlled setting. For frozen deployment, we study native-relative exponent adjustments: a boundary-matched profile raises task-equal F1 from $21.62\%$ to $25.44\%$ on five OLMo long-context QA tasks, while a static Qwen table--amplitude configuration improves $64$K RULER-13 by $6.09$ points over the tested YaRN configuration. Together, these results identify interior allocation as a consequential design choice whose benefits depend on sampled range and learned compatibility.
