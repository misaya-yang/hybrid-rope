Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) typically tie their frequency placement to a single base. We study frequency allocation as a design dimension for quality within and beyond the native context window. Controlled interventions show that internal placement matters even when the frequency range and total log-frequency displacement are fixed. Full sine--cosine geometry characterizes positional overlap, while coordinate interventions distinguish this structure from the model's learned use of frequencies. We introduce TailSpline by formulating and solving a discrete allocation problem for the transition into the extended low-frequency tail. Its closed-form rule retains standard rotary computation without weight updates or calibration. Matched comparisons with established extension rules show improvements in retrieval and language modeling across model families, together with gains on natural question answering. A complementary native-window construction improves task performance at unchanged frequency endpoints using only public RoPE parameters. Cosh transport supplies supporting learning and extrapolation evidence. Together, these results establish internal frequency placement as a practical design dimension for context extension and in-window quality.
