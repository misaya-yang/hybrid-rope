A scalar RoPE base fixes a log-frequency span; it does not determine how a
finite budget of $K$ rotary pairs is spent inside that span. Writing
$x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates spectral support $(a,R)$ from the
normalised interior allocation $z$. We show that $z$ is an independent
training-time design variable. In a $151.9$M exact-range control, fixing
$a$, $R$, initialisation, data order, and optimisation while moving only the
$30$ interior frequencies changes OOD NLL by
$-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K. A separate $12$-configuration,
three-seed factorial finds the same non-uniform direction for two different
analytic shapes in $10/12$ and $9/12$ configurations. Full sin/cos subspace
geometry gives a phase-invariant account of finite-table redundancy, while a
$50$M $2\times2$ crossing shows why geometry alone is insufficient:
self-consistent weight--table pairs have PPL $7.14/7.16$, but post-hoc swaps
reach $76.20/23.05$. We prove the corresponding exact obstruction for fixed
invertible Q/K compensation. \evq{} is one closed-form,
zero-learned-parameter allocation on this axis. The same effective-context
shift persists at scale: a $1.485$B from-initialisation trajectory crosses in
favour of \evq{} at $8$K/$16$K; matched $300$-step adaptation at $8$B reduces
PPL from $108.96$ to $24.07$ at $16$K; and matched Q/K-only adaptation at
$1.485$B preserves $4$K 2Wiki exact match ($22.0/21.5\%$) while changing the
$8$K result from $0$ to $17.5\%$.
