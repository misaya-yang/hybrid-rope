A scalar RoPE base fixes log-frequency support, but not how a finite budget of
$K$ rotary pairs is allocated inside it. Writing
$x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates support $(a,R)$ from normalised interior
allocation $z$. We show that $z$ is an independent training-time design
variable. In a $151.9$M exact-range control, initialisation, data order,
optimisation, and both sampled endpoints are fixed; moving only the $30$
interior frequencies reduces OOD NLL by $0.478$ at $2\times$ the training
length. A separate $12$-configuration, three-seed factorial repeats the
non-uniform direction for both Cosh and deformation-matched exponential
allocations. Full sin/cos subspace geometry then gives an exact,
phase-invariant accounting of finite-table redundancy. The table must be
learned with the weights: self-consistent weight--table pairs have PPL
$7.14/7.16$, whereas frozen swaps reach $76.20/23.05$. Separately, we prove
that unequal frequency multisets cannot be absorbed exactly by fixed
invertible Q/K maps. \evq{} is one closed-form, zero-learned-parameter
construction on this allocation axis. The resulting effective-context shift
persists at scale: matched $8$B adaptation reduces $16$K PPL from $108.96$ to
$24.07$, while matched $1.485$B Q/K-only adaptation preserves $4$K 2Wiki exact
match and changes the $8$K result from $0$ to $17.5\%$.
