RoPE is usually specified by a scalar base, yet a finite attention head must
also decide how its $K$ rotary pairs are distributed within that support. We
write $x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$, separating sampled support $(a,R)$ from
normalised interior allocation $z$. In a three-seed fixed-support control,
moving only $30$ interior frequencies consistently shifts modelling quality
from the training window toward longer contexts, making $z$ a separately
identifiable training-time variable. Full sin/cos subspace geometry gives an
exact, phase-invariant account of finite-table redundancy; an exact transplant
obstruction and a weight--table crossing then show why model weights co-adapt
to the table. We instantiate this axis with \evq{}, a closed-form schedule
with no learned positional parameters. In a three-seed $432$M MLA stress test
with only $16$ rotary frequencies, \evq{} leaves in-window PPL essentially
unchanged and lowers $16$K PPL by $31.1\%$. The crossover recurs under
full-parameter training through $1.485$B. At $1.485$B, low-rank adaptation
carries multi-hop QA beyond the physical training window; at $8$B, adaptation
produces causal use of remote source content. Interior allocation is thus a
practical part of long-context training, alongside support selection and range
transport.
