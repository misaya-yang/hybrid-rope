Rotary position embeddings specify both a frequency range and an allocation of a finite number of rotary pairs within that range. Writing a log-frequency table as endpoints $(a,R)$ together with normalized interior exponents $z$, we study $z$ separately from the sampled range $R$ and from the weights $M$ that learn to use the resulting basis. In paired $151.9$M training runs with identical frequency endpoints, changing only the $30$ interior exponents lowers extrapolation NLL at every tested extended length in all three seeds. The ordering reverses under runtime range retargeting, and weights-by-table crossings show strong dependence on the training allocation. We characterize the supplied basis through full sine--cosine subspaces, and show by counterexample that three classes of static surrogate fail as standalone predictors, so $z$ cannot be ranked without $R$ and $M$. Two constructions follow as consequences rather than optimality claims: an analytic Cosh allocation from a convex design criterion, and a native-relative profile for frozen checkpoints. On five OLMo long-context QA tasks the latter raises task-equal F1 from $21.62\%$ to $25.44\%$ against MrRoPE-Pro, while a static Qwen table--amplitude configuration improves $64$K RULER-13 by $6.09$ points over the tested YaRN configuration. Together, these results show that a finite RoPE system is not described by its range alone: support, interior allocation and learned slot compatibility are three coordinates that cannot be merged, and an allocation carries no ranking apart from the other two.
