RoPE specifies rotations but not how a finite head should spend its frequency
channels. We show that geometric spacing spends many slow pairs on nearly
duplicate positional subspaces: in a representative $4$K setting, $46$
nominal dimensions have R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. Writing
$x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates sampled support $(a,R)$ from interior
allocation $z$. A three-seed fixed-support intervention moves only $30$
interior frequencies and improves every evaluated OOD length in every seed,
establishing $z$ as an independent training variable. Canonical correlations
make the full sin/cos redundancy phase invariant. A tractable convex surrogate
then yields \evq{}, its unique closed-form Cosh minimiser; the predicted
reallocation reduces collision under the exact cosine-feature RoPE kernel in
every tested configuration. A weight--table crossing and exact transplant
obstruction explain co-adaptation. With only $16$ frequencies, three-seed
$432$M MLA lowers $16$K PPL by $31.1\%$. Long-range gains persist under $454$M
range composition, $750$M full-parameter continuation, and a $1.485$B
from-initialisation comparison. Frozen fixed-support interventions on OLMo and
Qwen show that allocation remains actionable after pretraining, while a
separate $8$B position-preserving attention intervention establishes causal use
of distant source content. Frequency allocation is therefore a first-class
long-context design decision.
