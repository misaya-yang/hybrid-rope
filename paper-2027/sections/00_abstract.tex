RoPE optimisation is usually framed through its base, spectral range, or phase
transport, while geometric spacing silently fixes how a finite set of rotary
pairs is allocated inside that range. Writing
$x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ exposes interior allocation $z$ as a third
finite-table design coordinate. Exact, phase-invariant sin/cos geometry shows
that redundancy can collapse many nominal dimensions onto nearly the same
two-dimensional positional basis. On a frozen billion-scale OLMo checkpoint,
changing only $z$ at matched support raises nine-task $16$K RULER from
$0.56\%$ to $60.47\%$; a coarse label-free allocation reproduces this recovery
without task labels or parameter updates. A paired
three-seed fixed-support training intervention independently improves every
tested OOD length in every seed, identifying $z$ during co-adaptation. A stated
convex surrogate then yields \evq{}, a closed-form zero-learned-parameter
construction unique for that surrogate. Across fully frozen, matched low-rank
adaptation, and from-training interventions, the same coordinate remains
consequential throughout the model lifecycle. A finite RoPE table is jointly
determined by its sampled support and how its spectral budget is allocated.
