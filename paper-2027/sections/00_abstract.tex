RoPE gives each attention head a finite frequency budget, yet geometric
spacing couples the covered log-frequency range to one particular allocation
within it; writing $x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates sampled support from
interior allocation. Under the stated uniform separation prior,
phase-invariant canonical correlations yield an exact effective-dimension
identity: over separations up to $4$K, $23$ slow pairs---$46$ nominal
dimensions---have block-whitened effective dimension $2.00$. A paired
three-seed intervention then holds sampled support and the training protocol
fixed while moving only the interior frequencies; every seed improves at every
tested out-of-distribution length, identifying allocation as consequential
rather than a disguised base change. A convex surrogate has a unique continuous
minimizer whose inverse CDF yields \evq{}, a closed-form table with no learned
positional parameters. We test the consequences of this design axis in
from-scratch and continued training, low-rank adaptation, frozen checkpoints,
and a bidirectional video transformer, showing that RoPE's frequency budget is
not determined by range alone: allocation is a separate, controllable axis.
