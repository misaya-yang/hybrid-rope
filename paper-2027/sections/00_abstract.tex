Internal exponent allocation $z$ is a useful RoPE design variable: redistributing interior frequencies improves extrapolation even when the sampled support is fixed. We identify its independent contribution, characterize the positional structure it changes, and derive explicit allocations for learning and zero-training deployment. Across three paired training seeds with identical sampled endpoints, changing only $30$ interior frequencies lowers extrapolation loss at $2\times$, $4\times$, and $8\times$; matched-support interventions also improve task scores in frozen billion-parameter models. Full sine--cosine geometry reveals finite-window redundancy through an overlap--effective-rank identity and a shared slow-frequency limit. Cosh provides an analytic training-time allocation, reducing $16$K perplexity from $138.8$ to $95.6$ in three-seed MLA training. For frozen deployment, we introduce TailSpline, the exact finite-grid solution of a one-sided boundary-smoothing objective. Without updating weights or fitting activations, it improves length-averaged RULER-13 over MrRoPE-Pro by $3.20$ points on Llama-$3$-$8$B and $49.23$ points on OLMo; length-aggregated NIAH and PPL also improve on both. These results establish both the independent value of $z$ and the practical benefits of explicit allocation design.
