Redistributing RoPE's interior frequencies improves extrapolation even when the sampled frequency range is fixed. We identify this allocation freedom and develop explicit constructions for learning and training-free deployment. Across three paired training seeds with identical sampled endpoints, changing only $30$ interior frequencies lowers extrapolation loss at $2\times$, $4\times$, and $8\times$; matched-support interventions also improve task scores in frozen billion-parameter models. Full sine--cosine geometry reveals finite-window redundancy through an overlap--effective-rank identity and a shared slow-frequency limit. Cosh provides an analytic training-time allocation, reducing $16$K perplexity from $138.8$ to $95.6$ in three-seed MLA training. For frozen deployment, we introduce TailSpline, the exact finite-grid solution of a one-sided boundary-smoothing objective. Without updating weights or fitting activations, it improves length-averaged RULER-13 over MrRoPE-Pro by $3.20$ points on Llama-$3$-$8$B and $49.23$ points on OLMo, with NIAH and PPL gains on both. Together, controlled identification, positional geometry, and constructive evaluations establish internal frequency allocation as a useful degree of freedom for RoPE design.
