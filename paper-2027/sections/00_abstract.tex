A finite rotary position embedding (RoPE) table chooses both a frequency range and an allocation within that range. We study how this allocation organizes the positional basis, interacts with learned weights, and enables useful context extension. Paired training at identical sampled endpoints isolates its effect: changing only 30 interior exponents improves extrapolation loss at $2\times$, $4\times$, and $8\times$ the training length in all three seeds. Full sine--cosine subspace analysis characterizes finite-window concentration, while crossed evaluations and range retargeting reveal learned compatibility and reversals in allocation preference. For mature models, we formulate deployment as choosing one fixed table for a range of operating lengths. We separate band location, transition shape, and terminal compression as design variables, and derive a conditional interval optimum that differs from endpoint matching. Boundary-matched redistribution improves five-task natural-QA F1 from $21.62$ to $25.44$ on a frozen OLMo checkpoint. During learning, EVQ-Cosh reduces $16$K perplexity from $138.8$ to $95.6$ in three-seed MLA training. Together, these results connect allocation's independent effect to concrete constructions and to the design of context quality across a deployment interval.
