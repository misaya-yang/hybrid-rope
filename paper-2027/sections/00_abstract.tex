A finite RoPE table contains two design coordinates. Writing
$x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates its sampled support $(a,R)$ from the
interior allocation $z$ of a fixed number of rotary pairs. We ask whether $z$
remains independently identifiable and behaviourally consequential when the
pair count, sampled support, and RoPE operator are fixed. Exact phase-invariant
sin/cos geometry characterises this finite spectral budget and shows how slow
frequencies can collapse onto a much smaller positional basis. A paired
three-seed fixed-support intervention then changes only the interiors and
improves every tested OOD length in every seed; allowing the declared
$L_{\mathrm{target}}$ to retarget support reverses the tested ordering. Thus
support and allocation are distinct, interacting coordinates rather than two
names for changing frequencies. On this design space, a convex surrogate yields
closed-form zero-learned-parameter \evq{}, unique within that surrogate. Fully
frozen, matched-adaptation, and from-training interventions further show that
structured allocation changes extrapolation, downstream, retrieval, and
in-window behaviour across the model lifecycle. The scientific object is the
finite table's support--allocation decomposition; \evq{} is one analytic
construction on that object.
