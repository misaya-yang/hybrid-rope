A RoPE head allocates a finite table of $K=\drot/2$ rotary frequencies before
training. We show that its interior allocation is an independent training-time
design variable even when the sampled spectral range is pinned exactly. Each
frequency spans a two-dimensional sin/cos subspace; block-whitening their
cross-Grams gives phase-invariant canonical correlations and the exact static
identity $r_2=2K/(1+(K{-}1)\bar c)$. In a standard $4$K configuration, the $24$
slowest pairs occupy $48$ nominal dimensions but only $2.00$ stable-rank
dimensions. This geometry identifies redundancy, not model quality. In a
$151.9$M exact-range control where only $30$ interior frequencies differ,
an endpoint-normalised Cosh diagnostic changes trained-model OOD NLL by
$-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K. A separate $12$-configuration,
three-seed factorial finds the same non-uniform direction for the pre-specified
$1.25\times$ Cosh and a matched exponential in $10/12$ and $9/12$
configurations. Training matters:
crossing two $50$M weight sets with two runtime tables gives PPL $7.14/7.16$
for the self-consistent systems but $76.20/23.05$ after post-hoc swaps, and we
prove that no fixed, position-independent invertible Q/K maps can exactly
absorb unequal frequency multisets. We therefore treat a RoPE table as a
training-time coordinate system. \evq{} is one closed-form,
zero-learned-parameter construction on this axis, not its universal optimum.
At mature scale, matched table-conditioned adaptation produces an
effective-context crossover, raising $8$K RULER from
$2.02\%$ to $31.63\%$ at $1.485$B and $16$K RULER from $0.295\%$ to
$14.03\%$ at $8$B.
