RoPE specifies a rotation, but not how a finite head should spend its frequency
channels. We show that the standard geometric table pays a severe redundancy
tax: in a representative $4$K configuration, $23$ slow frequency pairs occupy
$46$ nominal dimensions but have R\'enyi-$2$ effective rank $2.00$. Writing
$x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ separates sampled support $(a,R)$ from interior
allocation $z$ and exposes the latter as an independent training variable. We
instantiate this variable with \evq{}, a closed-form fast-end allocation that
changes only the inverse-frequency table and adds no learned positional
parameters. In a three-seed fixed-support intervention, moving $30$ interior
frequencies while preserving both sampled endpoints improves every evaluated
OOD length in every seed. Full sin/cos subspace geometry gives an exact account
of the redundancy, while a frozen-transplant obstruction and a weight--table
crossing explain why the table and weights co-adapt. With only $16$ rotary
frequencies, three-seed $432$M MLA reduces $16$K PPL by $31.1\%$. The advantage
persists under $454$M \rs{} composition, $750$M full-parameter continuation,
and a $1.485$B from-initialisation comparison. Separate $8$B adaptation produces
causal dependence on distant source content. Frequency allocation is therefore
a first-class long-context training decision, not an implementation detail.
