RoPE typically pairs a tunable base with an equally spaced exponent schedule.
We study how reshaping that schedule changes positional representation and
length generalization. A phase-invariant analysis of the
full sine--cosine basis reveals how nearby slow frequencies converge to the same
positional subspace. This motivates a convex surrogate allocation problem whose
closed-form solution, \evq{}, redistributes exponents without adding learned
positional parameters. In a three-seed fixed-range training experiment, changing
only the interior exponents improves every evaluated out-of-distribution length
in every seed. The construction also improves long-context modeling with scarce
rotary channels and under matched adaptation: at $32$K, Llama-$3$-$8$B
perplexity falls from $991.5$ to $127.9$. We then study exponent adjustments in
frozen mature models, expressing frequency blends and mixed-radix transformations
in a common displacement coordinate. On OLMo, an intermediate-band redistribution improves mean token F1
from $21.62\%$ to $25.44\%$ across five natural-QA tasks. The two stages
provide a geometric account, explicit constructions, and controlled model
evidence for designing RoPE through its exponent distribution.
