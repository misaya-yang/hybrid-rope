\section{Frequency Allocation under a Fixed Rotary Budget}
\label{sec:intro}

An attention head can devote fewer dimensions to rotation without removing its
content channels. Does the way those remaining frequencies are placed determine
how much long-context quality is lost? We study this question at fixed total
query/key width, head count, parameter count, and training budget. The resource
is the number $K$ of rotating two-dimensional pairs; the remaining coordinates
retain identity rotation. This is a representation-efficiency question. In
ordinary multi-head attention it does not, by itself, reduce the KV tensor.

The relevant comparison is stronger than showing that a new frequency table
improves a geometric table at one width. A budget-specific claim requires an
allocation-by-budget interaction. A practical half-budget claim additionally
requires comparison with simple strategies that retain selected frequencies
from the full table. We therefore retain both a high-frequency subset and a
uniform subset as main controls. They may explain the entire benefit of reducing
rotation, in which case a special advantage for EVQ would be unsupported.

Slow rotary pairs offer a useful starting point for theory. With content
coefficients fixed, their positional response subspaces approach the same
span of a constant and a linear function of distance. We quantify the residual
energy outside that span on a finite distance distribution, bound the spectral
tail of the complete table, and impose a coefficient budget before discussing
approximation error. A softmax bridge then applies to a controlled read with
bounded centered logits. Content-dependent coefficients and communication
through other layers remain outside that argument; only model experiments can
determine whether the restriction is consequential in language modeling.

Our sole analytic candidate is anchored \evq{}, with one fixed shape strength
across budgets. Its previous surrogate derivation supplies a reproducible
construction; it does not establish optimality for the new budget objective.
Nor does an asymptotic quantization bound imply increasing gains as $K$ shrinks:
when $K=2$ and both endpoints are fixed, EVQ and geometric tables coincide.

\paragraph{Evidence and open test.}
A prior three-seed fixed-support intervention establishes that interior
allocation can change learning outcomes (App.~\ref{sec:exp-identify}). A
separate three-seed MLA study provides positive long-range evidence at a single
small rotary budget (\S\ref{sec:budget-external}). Neither establishes what
happens when only rotary width changes. The new six-arm experiment isolates
that question, with same-target long-document NLL, training-window retention,
and a position-preserving remote-prefix control. Its results are pending; the
current manuscript presents the theory and registered experiment without
claiming half-budget preservation.
