\subsection{Why the intermediate band carries the design freedom}
\label{sec:three-band-chain}

Three-band schedules share a skeleton. With $t_j(\Ltr)=\Ltr\omega_j/2\pi$ the turns pair $j$
accumulates over the window, a pair installed as $\omega'_j=\omega_j s^{-m_j}$ reproduces at
separation $\Delta$ the phase its native frequency had at $\Delta s^{-m_j}$, so it stays a
calibrated readout exactly while $\Delta\le\Ltr s^{m_j}$. At $s=4$, $m_j=1$ is the smallest
compression keeping all of $[0,4\Ltr]$ on the trained arc; that fixes the slow band once the
target is fixed and leaves the other two as choices. Leaving the fast band native is a strong
design constraint rather than a theorem: one scored intervention departs from it, and pays.

\paragraph{Moving the fast band is expensive and buys little reach.}
Cost tracks the slot, not the distance moved. Speeding the fast pairs up --- $m_j$ falling to
$-0.156$ across slots $1$--$23$, the transport of a compressed schedule's savings into the fast
band --- drops the Qwen-$2.5$-$3$B panel from $87.22/78.13$ to $70.14/67.36$ at $32$K/$128$K, and
extending the plateau into high-turn slots costs the $128$K row a further $9.8$ points. On Qwen-$2.5$-$3$B's $32$K in-window NLL instrument, a shift applied to
all $64$ pairs costs $+0.088$ nats although its largest displacement is $|m|=0.552$, smaller than
in-band displacements that are indistinguishable from zero ($+0.017$ or less); a larger shift of
the same kind costs $+3.17$ nats on that instrument and drives accuracy to $0.0000$ on a
$350$-row OLMo-$1$B panel. A four-row gradient
diagnostic at the native window agrees in ordering --- per-region mean $|\partial L/\partial m|$
of $40.9$, $2.13$ and $0.040$ over slots $0$--$23$, $24$--$39$ and $40$--$63$, concentrated on
slots $0$--$6$ --- but those four rows span an order of magnitude, so we treat it as a diagnostic
rather than a capability measurement. The asymmetry is also analytic: at $m_j=0$ the drift
accumulated at
$D=4\Ltr$ is $4\,t_j(\Ltr)\ln4$ turns, spanning $707\times$ across live pairs, so entering the
readable window at $4\times$ costs $m_j\gtrsim\log_4(t_j/4)$: $0.42$ at $j=22$ against $3.67$
at $j=0$ ($7.16$ and $651.9$ native turns on OLMo-$1$B).

\paragraph{The slow band's content is already degenerate.}
Large compression there is harmless because the pairs hold little independent positional
information. By Proposition~\ref{prop:collapse}, $23$ pairs with $\omega L\le1$ supply $46$
coordinate dimensions at a block-whitened rank of $2.00013$. Pair-level measurement agrees:
compressing the pair at $0.61$ native turns improves in-window NLL monotonically to $-0.046$
nats at $m=1$, and over twelve interior pairs and six compression levels the marginal in-window
damage stays within $10^{-3}$--$5\times10^{-2}$ nats and is not monotone in $t_j$. These are
NLL statements: in-window mean NLL improves by $0.023$ nats while in-window task accuracy falls
by $10.6$ points along the same schedule family, so both readouts must be quoted.

\paragraph{The trajectory matters through thresholds, not slopes.}
Of the $38$ tables we reconstructed, all but the native reference and one twelve-row probe are
$m_j=0$ on $j\le23$ and $m_j=1$ on $j\ge40$, with all variation interior. Inside that family
the interior separates outcomes by tens of points, but threshold-like rather than smoothly: on
a $350$-row OLMo-$1$B panel at $16$K the readable-slot count $N$ gives
$\operatorname{logit}(\mathrm{acc})=-20.45+1.19\,N$ with no paired ordering error across twelve
arms, whereas the continuous band-edge crossing location misorders $54$ of $60$ pairs. The same
schedule reverses sign across depths (pooled held-out deltas of $-5.37$ and $+6.54$ points at
$4$K and $16$K), and the response is checkpoint-dependent by two orders of magnitude:
family-spanning schedules move long-context NLL by $0.83$ nats between the OLMo-$1$B endpoints
and by $0.0077$ nats over nine arms on Qwen-$2.5$-$1.5$B, at a per-arm standard error of
$0.030$.

\paragraph{Scope.} Every scored arm satisfies $\max_j m_j\le1$, so the chain covers schedules
whose reach ends at $4\times$ the training window: at $8\times$ four such arms, the deployed
table included, each score $0/48$, and nothing with $\max_j m_j>1$ has been scored. The
coverage ordering that organizes the OLMo-$1$B arms does not carry to the Qwen panels, so the
account above is calibrated on one checkpoint family rather than stated as a general law. Two
limits bound the fast-band paragraph: its edge is $\beta=32$, inherited from \citet{peng2024yarn}
because it reproduces the deployed tables bit-exactly rather than because we derive it; and
per-slot damage was measured only for slots $14$--$36$, where it is flat within paired errors and
not monotone in $t_j$, with slots $0$--$13$ never singly perturbed.
