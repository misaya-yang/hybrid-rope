\section{Frequency Allocation Matters at Fixed Range}
\label{sec:controlled-findings}

Different grids within one range need not be interchangeable for a model.
We test this first with frozen weights, then with paired training, and use
equal-displacement and coordinate interventions to separate the effects.

\subsection{Different allocations with the same frozen weights}
\label{sec:exp-frozen-support}

On OLMo-$2$-$1$B and Qwen-$2.5$-$1.5$B, comparisons share weights, $64$ pairs,
amplitude, inputs and decoder. Endpoints run from the native fast frequency to
one-quarter of the native slow frequency. On OLMo's nine held-out RULER tasks
\citep{hsieh2024ruler}, uniform normalized exponents score $0.56\%$, a coarse
ramp $61.04\%$, and a residual-guided profile $60.47\%$. Qwen's four-task
comparison gives $57.75/64.00/66.50\%$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support}).
The same range supports markedly different quality depending on interior allocation.

\paragraph{Internal shape beyond total displacement.}\label{sec:equal-displacement}
A frozen OLMo comparison also matches total displacement $\sum_k d_k$, alongside endpoints, band and gain: the symmetric profile BM and uniform transition MrRoPE-Uni score $51.32$ and $32.12\%$ in a six-task, $48$-input $16$K follow-up comparison. Total displacement therefore does not explain this score difference (Appendix~\ref{sec:four-method-control}). The clean TailSpline--C comparison confirms a shape effect at matched displacement on Llama (Section~\ref{sec:clean-shape-main}).

\paragraph{The effect extends beyond one allocation family.}\label{sec:factorial}
A $50.9$M short-training factorial ($8.39$M tokens per arm) with three paired seeds tests two bases, two training lengths
and three head dimensions. Reference Cosh, its preassigned $1.25\times$
strength, and an RMS-movement-matched exponential improve OOD NLL in $7/12$,
$10/12$, and $9/12$ configurations. Cosh and the matched exponential perform
similarly (mean difference $+0.00074$ NLL, interval $[-0.006,0.008]$).
These results support nonuniform allocation across families, with strength-dependent
trade-offs; full settings and a separate standard-grid control are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:exact-range-factorial}.

\paragraph{A learned model uses more than a frequency range.}
Crossing the runtime tables of the paired $151.9$M models tests whether weights
can use a different allocation interchangeably. Both seeds favor the table
derived from their training allocation (Table~\ref{tab:crossing-151m}).
A complementary frozen intervention permutes
the same frequency multiset across coordinates and changes model performance
(Appendix~\ref{sec:slot-protocol}). These findings connect the two operating
settings: learning can adapt weights to an allocation, whereas frozen extension
must work with the model's existing frequency--coordinate association.
