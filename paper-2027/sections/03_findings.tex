\subsection{The effect remains in frozen models}
\label{sec:exp-frozen-support}

On OLMo-$2$-$1$B and Qwen-$2.5$-$1.5$B, we fix the checkpoint, sampled endpoints, $64$ pairs, attention amplitude, input rows, and decoder. The range extends from the native fast frequency to one-quarter of the native slow frequency. On OLMo's nine held-out RULER tasks \citep{hsieh2024ruler}, uniform normalized exponents score $0.56\%$, a coarse ramp $61.04\%$, and a residual-guided profile $60.47\%$. Qwen's four-task development comparison gives $57.75/64.00/66.50\%$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support}). Both reallocations yield large OLMo gains while retaining the same sampled range.

\paragraph{Internal shape beyond total displacement.}\label{sec:equal-displacement}
Fixed endpoints do not fix the sum of native-relative shifts $\sum_k d_k$. A separate frozen OLMo comparison holds that sum, endpoints, band and gain fixed: the symmetric profile BM and uniform transition MrRoPE-Uni score $51.32$ and $32.12\%$ on a six-task, $48$-input $16$K development panel. Total displacement therefore does not explain this score difference (Appendix~\ref{sec:four-method-control}). An additional development control fixes the increment centroid as well (Appendix~\ref{sec:fixed-total-profile}).

\paragraph{Beyond one shape.}\label{sec:factorial}
The three-seed short-run M4 factorial varies base, training length and head dimension, comparing Cosh strengths and an exponential matched in RMS node displacement at fixed support. Both families improve OOD loss in multiple configurations, with joint in-window/OOD gains (Appendix~\ref{sec:exact-range-factorial}). A separate three-seed native Std-RoPE comparison improves all five $512$--$8$K lengths (Appendix~\ref{sec:native-grid-control}).
