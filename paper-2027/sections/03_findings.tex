\section{Frequency Allocation Matters at Fixed Range}
\label{sec:controlled-findings}

We first isolate the effect of interior placement by fixing the frequency endpoints. We then examine how that effect depends on training, total frequency displacement, and the model's learned use of its coordinates.

\paragraph{The effect extends beyond one allocation family.}\label{sec:factorial}
A $50.9$M short-training factorial ($8.39$M tokens per arm) with three paired seeds tests two bases, two training lengths
and three head dimensions. Reference Cosh, its preassigned $1.25\times$
strength, and a displacement-matched exponential improve OOD NLL in $7/12$,
$10/12$, and $9/12$ configurations. Cosh and the matched exponential perform
similarly (mean difference $+0.00074$ NLL, interval $[-0.006,0.008]$).
These results support nonuniform allocation across families, with strength-dependent
trade-offs; full settings and a separate standard-grid control are in
Appendices~\ref{sec:exact-range-factorial} and~\ref{sec:native-grid-control}.

\subsection{The effect remains in frozen models}
\label{sec:exp-frozen-support}

On OLMo-$2$-$1$B and Qwen-$2.5$-$1.5$B, we fix the checkpoint, sampled endpoints, $64$ pairs, attention amplitude, input rows, and decoder. The range extends from the native fast frequency to one-quarter of the native slow frequency. On OLMo's nine held-out RULER tasks \citep{hsieh2024ruler}, uniform normalized exponents score $0.56\%$, a coarse ramp $61.04\%$, and a residual-guided profile $60.47\%$. Qwen's four-task development comparison gives $57.75/64.00/66.50\%$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:frozen-fixed-support}). Both reallocations yield large OLMo gains while retaining the same sampled range.

\paragraph{Internal shape beyond total displacement.}\label{sec:equal-displacement}
Fixed endpoints do not fix the sum of native-relative shifts $\sum_k d_k$. A separate frozen OLMo comparison holds that sum, endpoints, band and gain fixed: the symmetric profile BM and uniform transition MrRoPE-Uni score $51.32$ and $32.12\%$ in a six-task, $48$-input $16$K follow-up comparison. Total displacement therefore does not explain this score difference (Appendix~\ref{sec:four-method-control}). The clean TailSpline--C comparison confirms a shape effect at matched displacement on Llama (Section~\ref{sec:clean-shape-main}).

\paragraph{A learned model uses more than a frequency range.}
Crossing the runtime tables of the paired $151.9$M models tests whether weights
can use a different allocation interchangeably. Both seeds favor the table
derived from their training allocation (Fig.~\ref{fig:method-overview}c;
Appendix~\ref{sec:crossing-151m}). A complementary frozen intervention permutes
the same frequency multiset across coordinates and changes model performance
(Appendix~\ref{sec:slot-protocol}). These findings connect the two operating
settings: learning can adapt weights to an allocation, whereas frozen extension
must work with the model's existing frequency--coordinate association.
