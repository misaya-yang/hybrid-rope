\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

A finite RoPE table has two properties that should not be conflated. Its full
sin/cos Gram determines a static positional basis, for which
Theorem~\ref{thm:budget} gives an exact stable-rank accounting. Its trained
value depends on how model weights use that basis. The exact-range controls
connect these layers without identifying them: holding the sampled range fixed
while moving only interior channels changes the trained model, but a table with
higher static rank can still be catastrophically wrong for weights trained in
another coordinate system.

This distinction explains why allocation must be installed during training or
adaptation. The $50$M factorial crossing and mature-model comparisons show
strong table-by-weights dependence, while Theorem~\ref{thm:obstruction} rules
out exact preservation by fixed invertible Q/K maps when frequency multisets
differ. The theorem does not preclude approximate retraining; instead it says
why post-hoc replacement and training under a table are different
interventions. The small from-initialisation in-window NLL differences and the
large frozen-swap failures are therefore compatible.

Allocation also differs from range transport. The former chooses which finite
channels a model trains against; the latter changes how a realised spectrum is
deployed at a target length. In the exact-range experiment, target-aware
retargeting is the stronger lever once range is allowed to vary. LeRoPE supplies
the complementary learned-table result: a table obtained in one run retains
substantial value when frozen for a new training run
\citep{karypis2026lerope}. Our contribution is the phase-invariant finite-basis
geometry, fixed-range causal identification, exact frozen-retrofit obstruction,
and a closed-form point on the allocation axis.

No static geometry in this paper is an extrapolation guarantee, and neither the
surrogate nor the experiments make Cosh a unique task optimum. \evq{} is useful
because it is analytic, zero-learned-parameter, and reproducible. The broader
result is simpler: at finite $K$, interior frequency placement is part of the
training design, and evaluating it requires both basis geometry and the model
trained to use that basis.
