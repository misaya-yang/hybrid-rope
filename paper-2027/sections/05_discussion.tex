\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Exponent allocation connects a simple implementation choice to the positional
basis a model learns to use. The fixed-range experiments show that reshaping
the exponents changes length generalization; the subspace analysis explains
how that reshaping changes the distribution of positional directions.
\evq{} turns this view into a closed-form construction, with substantial
long-context gains across the trained and adapted models.

The frozen-model results add a second perspective. Expressing a change as a
displacement from the native exponents makes blends, log-frequency shifts, and
mixed-radix rules directly comparable. Their different outcomes across
checkpoints show that the original learned representation is part of the design
problem. An exponent table should therefore be specified together with its
sampled range and the way it is installed.

A natural next step is to study exponent allocation under sparse attention.
Selective access to tokens and compressed representations change which
positional distinctions reach the reader. The framework developed here provides
a way to ask how the exponent distribution should serve those architectures.
