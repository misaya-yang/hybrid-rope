\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

A finite RoPE table specifies both which positional scales are sampled and how pairs are distributed among them. Fixed-support experiments show interior allocation to be a consequential learning choice, while range-retargeting reversals and weight--table crossings establish that downstream behavior cannot be predicted from allocation shape alone. The geometric analysis clarifies supplied positional directions; model experiments determine how those directions are used.

Before or during learning, explicit allocations can be learned with the model, as the Cosh construction illustrates; for existing checkpoints, native-relative adjustments make gain, support, and table identity auditable. Static-profile and natural-QA comparisons show that this deployment space yields practical gains, but neither regime justifies replacing empirical validation with geometry-only rankings.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our strongest training control uses $151.9$M models and short training windows; the $50.9$M factorial is a short-training screen; and several mature-model comparisons condition on single checkpoints. The Cosh family optimizes its specified density criterion, not the full language-model objective. Block-whitened effective rank removes raw feature scaling and does not measure content-channel utility. Frozen deployment results depend on joint table--amplitude configurations, and reported RULER and natural-QA gains do not establish universal cross-task dominance. Extending these controls to sparse attention remains a promising direction.
