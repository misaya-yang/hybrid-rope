\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
A frequency range leaves a consequential choice open: where to place the
frequencies within it. Our controlled interventions establish that this choice
can improve learned extrapolation and frozen-model task performance.
Full-pair geometry characterizes the resulting positional overlap, while
learned-coordinate effects clarify how frequency changes interact with a
pretrained model.

TailSpline provides an explicit native-relative allocation that retains
standard rotary computation. Cosh offers a complementary transport for
extrapolation and supports the broader value of changing $z$. The clean RULER comparisons show TailSpline gains over MrPro at both
$2L$ and $4L$, with a small observed native-window trade-off. Natural-QA evaluation gives close observed F1 scores for TailSpline and MrPro. Together, these results establish frequency
allocation as a practical design dimension for quality within a chosen context window.
