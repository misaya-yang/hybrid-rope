\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

A finite RoPE table is specified by its sampled log-frequency support $(a,R)$
and the interior allocation $z$ of its fixed $K$ pairs. Position rescaling or
frequency-dependent transport may change the realised table as context grows,
but does not remove this decomposition. Our fixed-support intervention holds
$K$, both endpoints, and the RoPE operator constant and changes only $z$---a
design move outside a scalar-base sweep.

The three empirical routes intervene on this coordinate at different stages.
With weights fully frozen, $z$ alone changes mature-model behaviour; under
matched low-rank adaptation, pretrained representations learn to use a new
basis; from training, the paired fixed-support control identifies weight--table
co-adaptation. Exact full-subspace geometry explains how those tables differ as
positional bases, while the weights-by-table crossing and transplant
obstruction explain why the intervention stage matters.

Across the autoregressive text protocols that evaluate both the original
training or Native window and longer positions, the comparisons reveal a
within-protocol crossover: the baseline is stronger near the original window,
while the reallocated table overtakes it beyond that window. This recurring
behavioural signature of finite-budget reallocation directly motivates the
Native/long session route. The video DiT instead improves
both training-frame and extrapolated-frame MSE; its pre-specified diagnostic
locates the different regime: six of sixteen temporal channels accumulate less
than $0.1$ rad over the $32$ training frames, so reallocating them changes
in-window phase coverage rather than imposing the text-model crossover.

Extrapolation provides the sharpest present identification of $z$. The same
coordinate also changes which phase scales are available inside the original
window. LeRoPE reports in-window gains
from learned non-geometric tables; our fixed-support geometry and closed-form
construction provide complementary ways to analyse and instantiate the same
broader design space. Together they make joint native-window and long-range
allocation a natural next design objective.

The practical consequence is that a finite RoPE table should be specified by
both its sampled support and its interior allocation. A shared base, endpoint,
or pair count does not by itself define a matched finite table. Treating $z$ as
an explicit design object makes allocation measurable and controllable across
training, adaptation, and deployment; the exact geometry supplies the basis
diagnosis, and the controlled interventions establish its behavioural
consequences.
