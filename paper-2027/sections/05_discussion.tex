\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Internal exponent allocation $z$ is a useful design freedom within RoPE. Fixed-support experiments establish its extrapolation value, and equal-displacement controls show that the amount of frequency change does not fully describe its effects. Full-pair geometry explains why distinct frequencies can supply overlapping finite-window directions; Cosh and TailSpline turn frequency spacing into explicit density and boundary designs.

The experiments demonstrate language-modeling, retrieval and natural-QA gains through learning and zero-training deployment. Future work can extend these design principles to mature in-window enhancement and broader transfer. The practical conclusion is that finite frequency placement deserves explicit design: models can learn useful allocations, and frozen models can benefit from static constructions, with $z$ available for joint design alongside support, boundaries and amplitude.
