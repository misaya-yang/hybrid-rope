\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Interior allocation organizes the finite positional basis, interacts with learned projections, and supports useful designs. Fixed-support interventions identify its independent effect; full-pair geometry and crossed evaluations distinguish supplied directions from learned use. Native-relative redistribution and learning-time construction exploit this freedom in different ways, with measured benefits in language modeling, generation, and natural QA.

The next design objective is the quality of one fixed table throughout its usable window. A target horizon leaves band location, terminal depth, transition shape, and amplitude underdetermined. The conditional depth optimum supplies a concrete alternative to full terminal interpolation. Candidate designs are tested by native, intermediate, and endpoint behavior together, rather than inferred from a phase surrogate. The practical test is whether a constructed table improves the declared deployment interval relative to strong fixed-table baselines.

\paragraph{Evidence and open design questions.}
The main paired training identification uses a $256$-token window; larger learning studies have their stated grids and adaptation protocols. The interval comparisons are development evidence. The depth result optimizes its specified scalar distortion, and checkpoint attention replay remains a proposed predictor (Appendix~\ref{sec:interval-replay}). Full curves and protocol distinctions are retained in the appendices. The established contribution is an identifiable and useful allocation freedom, together with mathematical and empirical structure for designing how it is used.
