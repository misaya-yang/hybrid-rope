\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Finite RoPE exposes three intervention levels: support selects the sampled
interval, interior allocation $z$ places a fixed number of frequency samples
inside it, and range transport changes how realised phases are used at longer
lengths. These levels can interact, but they are not interchangeable. The
fixed-support intervention therefore changes the effective positional basis at
constant pair count, endpoints, and RoPE operator---a design move outside a
scalar-base sweep.

The paper links that coordinate to behaviour in three epistemic steps. Exact
full-subspace geometry diagnoses the positional basis available to a table.
Paired fixed-support training identifies the behavioural effect of changing
$z$. Protocol-separated studies then show that the control remains active after
a zero-training frozen intervention, under matched low-rank adaptation, and
when weights co-adapt from training. The weights-by-table crossing and transplant
obstruction explain why intervention stage matters: a table defines a basis,
and learned coefficients bind the model to it.

Across the autoregressive text protocols that evaluate both the original
training or Native window and longer positions, the comparisons repeat a
within-protocol crossover: the baseline is stronger near the original window,
while the reallocated table overtakes it beyond that window. This is not a
pooled effect across metrics or models; it is a recurring behavioural signature
of reallocating a finite phase-resolution budget, and it
directly motivates the Native/long session route. The video DiT instead improves
both training-frame and extrapolated-frame MSE; its pre-specified diagnostic
locates the different regime: six of sixteen temporal channels accumulate less
than $0.1$ rad over the $32$ training frames, so reallocating them changes
in-window phase coverage rather than imposing the text-model crossover.

This design space is broader than extrapolation. Different allocations place
the finite samples over phase scales relevant within and beyond the training
window. Learned-table work such as LeRoPE explores this coordinate through
optimisation; our fixed-support geometry and closed-form construction provide
complementary ways to analyse and instantiate it. A high-value next direction
is to design allocation and co-adaptation jointly for explicit window
objectives, rather than treating the geometric table as fixed infrastructure.

The field implication is direct: RoPE table allocation should be designed,
measured, and controlled as a first-class coordinate across training,
adaptation, and deployment. In the stated protocols, $z$ is an identifiable and
controllable determinant of long-context behaviour: static geometry supplies
the basis diagnosis, and controlled interventions establish behavioural
consequences.
