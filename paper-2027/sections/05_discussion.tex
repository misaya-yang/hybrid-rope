\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Internal frequency allocation is useful: controlled experiments isolate its extrapolation gains, and explicit constructions realize benefits during learning and training-free deployment. Finite-window geometry identifies structure that allocation changes; Cosh, TailSpline and BM turn declared design objectives into executable tables. The empirical contribution includes both language modeling and actual task outputs.

The remaining opportunity is joint design. Range, transition boundaries, terminal scaling, amplitude and allocation can be chosen together, with full methods evaluated on native, intermediate and extended lengths. The present results establish external-length value; systematic mature in-window enhancement and cross-model selection of boundary priors remain future work. A complete task-optimality theory is not required to exploit the demonstrated freedom.
