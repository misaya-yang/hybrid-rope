\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

A finite RoPE table specifies both which positional scales are sampled and how pairs are distributed among them. Fixed-support experiments show that interior allocation is a consequential learning choice. Range-retargeting reversals and weight--table crossings establish that downstream behavior cannot be predicted from allocation shape alone. The geometric analysis clarifies supplied positional directions, while model experiments determine how those directions are used.

Before or during learning, explicit allocations can be learned together with the model, as illustrated by the Cosh construction. For existing checkpoints, native-relative adjustments make gain, support, and table identity auditable. Static-profile and natural-QA comparisons show that this deployment space yields practical gains. Neither regime justifies replacing empirical validation with geometry-only rankings.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our strongest training control uses $151.9$M models and short training windows; the $50.9$M factorial is a short-training screen; and several mature-model comparisons condition on single checkpoints. The Cosh family optimizes its specified density criterion, not the full language-model objective. Block-whitened effective rank removes raw feature scaling and does not measure content-channel utility. Frozen deployment results depend on joint table--amplitude configurations, and reported RULER and natural-QA gains do not establish universal cross-task dominance. Extending these controls to sparse attention remains a promising direction.
