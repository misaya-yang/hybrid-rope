\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Internal exponent allocation $z$ is a useful design freedom within RoPE. Fixed-support experiments establish its extrapolation value, and equal-displacement controls show that the amount of frequency change does not fully describe its effects. Full-pair geometry explains why distinct frequencies can supply overlapping finite-window directions; Cosh and TailSpline turn frequency spacing into explicit density and boundary designs.

The experiments show language-modeling, retrieval and natural-QA gains, with local costs and model-dependent responses described in \S\ref{sec:experiments}. Broad transfer and systematic mature in-window enhancement remain open. The practical conclusion is that finite frequency placement deserves explicit design: models can learn useful allocations, and frozen models can benefit from static constructions, with $z$ available for joint design alongside support, boundaries and amplitude.
