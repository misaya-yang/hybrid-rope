\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Interior exponent allocation connects three levels of RoPE design. It organizes the positional functions supplied by a finite table; training associates those functions with learned projections; explicit constructions use that freedom to improve model behavior. Fixed-support training and frozen interventions establish the effect, full-pair geometry identifies the positional object, and range retargeting and crossings reveal how the learned model uses it. The resulting designs have practical value across language modeling, complete generation, and natural question answering.

The range reversal, weight--table crossings, and checkpoint-dependent deployment responses identify a joint design problem: an allocation's preferred operating point depends on the range and the learned model. This complements data-dependent accounts of frequency usage \citep{wu2026datashapes}. In practice, specify range and placement separately, evaluate learning-time designs with paired training, and adjust mature models relative to their native table. Analytic profiles and multi-shape comparisons make this design space usable.

\paragraph{Scope of the evidence.}
The primary training identification uses $151.9$M models at a $256$-token window, complemented by a $50.9$M configuration factorial and fixed-support mature-model tasks. Larger learning studies supply evidence under their own grid and adaptation protocols. Exact geometry describes the positional basis, while downstream effects are measured by the model experiments; the Cosh optimum refers to its stated density criterion. Full length curves, task outcomes, and exploratory profile diagnostics are retained in the appendices. The collective result is a useful additional design coordinate for RoPE: how a finite rotary budget is allocated within its frequency range.
