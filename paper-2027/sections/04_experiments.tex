\section{Model Quality Within and Beyond the Native Window}
\label{sec:experiments}\label{sec:exp-mature}

\subsection{Task quality with a frozen TailSpline table}
\label{sec:tailspline-evaluation}
We compare TailSpline (T), MrRoPE-Pro (P) and static YaRN (Y) across Llama,
OLMo, Qwen, GLM and Kanana, with a Llama-$70$B NF4 scale-transfer test.
T/P share weights, outer bands, endpoints, gain, inputs and decoder; only
internal allocation changes. Both use static $s=4$ tables except Kanana's
$64$K panel ($s=2$). YaRN retains its public recipe, including Kanana's
publisher-recommended factor $4.4$ \citep{peng2024yarn,kanana2025model}.
All methods are installed without training. RULER \citep{hsieh2024ruler}
uses matched source-order prompts and official scoring.

\paragraph{Quality across the window.}
At Llama $32$K, TailSpline improves Full-13 by $11.72$ percentage points over
MrPro on $2{,}600$ paired prompts, with gains in all four task families and
$12$ of $13$ tasks. At $16$K, $650$ pairs confirm the intermediate-window
benefit; RULER QA improves by $8/9$ points at $16/32$K. The same table scores
$85.16\%$ on $650$ native-length $8$K prompts, with a slight reduction from
original RoPE's $90.03\%$. Figure~\ref{fig:tailspline-main} shows the task-level differences.

\paragraph{Internal shape at equal displacement.}
\label{sec:clean-shape-main}
T--C matches endpoints, outer bands, gain and total log-frequency displacement.
At $32$K, T/C score $68.27/66.17\%$, a $+2.10$-point difference with interval
$[1.11,3.08]$; at $16$K they score $86.09/86.35\%$.
T assigns less stretch than C in the entry half of the transition and more
in its tail half, at the same total displacement. This comparison links the
target-length gain to how stretch is distributed (Appendix~\ref{sec:clean-shape-new}). C has slightly higher positional
effective rank at $32$K ($10.093/10.077$), complementing Section~\ref{sec:theory}.

\paragraph{Direct baselines and transfer.}
On OLMo, the Full-13 gain reaches $41.42$ points across $2{,}600$ pairs,
with all $13$ tasks improving.
The matched three-arm panels extend the comparison across model families,
including GLM's partial rotary subspace and Kanana's $8$-million base.
TailSpline scores highest on each Full-13 panel; on Kanana it exceeds the
publisher-recommended YaRN deployment by $7.01$ points at $64$K. Llama/OLMo NIAH-$8$ with $200$ inputs per task and $46$-document
PPL also favor TailSpline over both baselines. Llama's retrieval gains are
concentrated in multikey tasks; OLMo improves across retrieval types
(Appendix~\ref{sec:three-method-complete}). The allocation also transfers
across model scale: directly reusing the Llama-$8$B frequency tables on the
$70$B NF4 checkpoint improves Full-13 by $17.72$ points and PPL from
$2.5075$ to $2.4954$.

\paragraph{Natural question answering.}
Question answering over complete passages and books tests downstream quality
\citep{yen2024helmet}. TailSpline improves OLMo's five-task Natural-QA
score, with gains on every task, and leads the Qwen book-QA panel and both
GLM book pools (Table~\ref{tab:natural-transfer-main}). At Kanana's $128$K deployment,
TailSpline/recommended YaRN score $19.59/17.85\%$ book-QA F1
across $118$ questions from $23$ complete source contexts.

Natural-QA uses $631$ questions from $524$ source contexts. LongBench v2
\citep{bai2025longbenchv2} adds complete Llama inputs at $8$K--$32$K.
InfiniteBench En.QA \citep{zhang2024infinitebench} uses complete books;
the Qwen/GLM pool-I inputs span $100$K--$128$K. To test transfer to new
sources, GLM retains the same allocation on a second pool of $15$ books
disjoint from pool I's seven, with complete inputs spanning $70$K--$95$K.

\subsection{Native-window allocation at fixed support}
\label{sec:ncp-main}
The fixed-range findings also motivate an intervention within the native
window. We use Native Contrastive Proximal allocation (NCP), a public-parameter
construction that shifts interior frequencies while preserving both endpoints,
frequency ordering, model weights and unit gain. Its reference target--distractor
comparison favors a more stable aligned response while keeping shifts close
to the native grid (Appendix~\ref{sec:ncp-construction-new}). One table is
used across layers and inputs.

To test context use, we compare full and recent histories while keeping
the prediction targets fixed. Across $128$ windows from $103$ documents,
scoring the final $256$ tokens gives Native/NCP full-history NLL
$2.91527/2.90248$. NCP gains $0.00794$ nat/token more from the additional
history, interval $[0.00553,0.01035]$, connecting its language-modeling
improvement to better use of earlier context.

On OLMo's native $4$K Full-13 diagnostic panel ($780$ pairs), the score
rises from $69.37$ to $70.78\%$,
a $+1.41$-point gain $[0.20,2.58]$; a new $130$-pair panel adds
$+3.26$ $[0.81,5.92]$. Appendix~\ref{sec:compact-native} provides
complete task scores, natural-QA results and the same-target protocol.

\subsection{Allocation during training and adaptation}
\label{sec:exp-capability}
Training with Cosh lets weights adapt to a transported frequency allocation.
A $432$M MLA-style model with $16$ rotary pairs trains for $500$M
tokens at $8$K across three seeds \citep{deepseekv2,deepseekv3}. Cosh improves
all five extended lengths in every seed; $16$K PPL falls from $138.8$ to $95.6$,
and the benefit persists under a common wavelength blend
(Fig.~\ref{fig:allocation-learning}). In a matched $750$M continuation, Cosh reduces
$16$K PPL from $45.1$ to $24.4$ and raises $8$K answer-token exact match
from $0/40$ to $31/40$.

The long-range gains extend to recipe-matched $1.485$B OLMo training and
matched Llama-$3$-$8$B LoRA, whose $16$K PPL falls from $108.96$ to $24.07$.
With phase-exposure and answer-plus-EOS supervision, matched OLMo adaptation
also improves complete-answer retrieval: Native/Cosh score $18/98\%$ at $8$K
and $0/60\%$ at $16$K. Learning thus makes use of the transported allocation
in both language modeling and downstream retrieval.

adaptation protocols and their native-window results.
