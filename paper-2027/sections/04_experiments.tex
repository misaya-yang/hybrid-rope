\subsection{Learning an allocation: curves, continuation and readout}
\label{sec:exp-capability}
A $432$M MLA-style model allocates $16$ rotary pairs, trains for $500$M tokens at $8$K, and uses three seeds \citep{deepseekv2,deepseekv3}. Cosh improves all five extended lengths from $16$K through $32$K in every seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:allocation-basis}b). Its $16$K advantage is already present at half and three-quarters of training (Table~\ref{tab:training-models}). The same wavelength-blend rule applied to both learned tables preserves the advantage. This is a matched composition test, with the exact historical operator stated in Appendix~\ref{sec:mla-results}.

A $750$M pair continues from the same $2$K Geo checkpoint for $500$M tokens at $4$K. Cosh reduces $16$K PPL by $45.9\%$ and raises $8$K greedy answer-token exact match from $0/40$ to $31/40$, with a small $4$K PPL cost. This single-seed continuation includes $3\%$ passkey and $10\%$ downstream mix (Appendix~\ref{sec:larger-scale-supporting}).

Allocation can also support complete answers after matched adaptation. The OLMo pair in Table~\ref{tab:training-models} undergoes the same routing and answer-plus-EOS continuation. It uses physical inputs of at most $4$K with explicit target-range position IDs, then evaluates real contiguous prompts. The $8/16$K gains therefore demonstrate successful length transfer after phase exposure; they are separate from our zero-training results. Appendix~\ref{sec:olmo2-1b} retains the complete lineage, supervision and native-window controls.

\subsection{TailSpline on frozen Llama and OLMo}
\label{sec:tailspline-evaluation}
We compare exact finite-grid TailSpline and MrRoPE-Pro on frozen Llama-$3$-$8$B-Instruct, using $s=4$, native window $8192$, band $[18,35]$, and common gain $1.1386294361$. Each method installs one static table; weights, inputs, greedy decoder, precision and scorer are shared. RULER-13 has $10$ paired prompts per task at $8/16/32$K ($390$ per arm). Natural-text PPL uses $32$ ProofPile-test and $14$ PG19-test documents at the same lengths ($138$ rows per arm).

We aggregate lengths using normalized trapezoidal area in log length: weights $1/4,1/2,1/4$ at $8/16/32$K. TailSpline raises RULER-13 AUC from $75.60$ to $78.80\%$, a $+3.20$-point difference (paired $95\%$ interval $[+0.65,+5.79]$). NIAH AUC improves by $3.75$ points ($[+0.55,+7.03]$); its single-key passkey subtask has AUC $100.0$ versus $97.5\%$. These are task-equal scores under the recorded scorers, including partial credit.

PPL AUC decreases from $4.62112$ to $4.61664$, with paired difference $-0.00449$ and interval $[-0.00872,-0.00121]$. This small gain is driven by $32$K; $8/16$K PPL is slightly worse (Fig.~\ref{fig:tailspline-main}d). Full-13 improves at all three lengths. Across task families, retrieval, tracking and aggregation improve in AUC, while QA decreases by $1.25$ points. The complete task and termination results are in Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details}.


\paragraph{Prospective OLMo confirmation.}
The same frozen formula is tested on OLMo-$2$-$1$B-Instruct at $s=4$, band $[14,32]$ and $4/8/16$K, with $390$ RULER prompts and $138$ LM rows per arm. The exact table was frozen before reading this OLMo evaluation; historical OLMo development had supplied direction priors. Full-13 AUC rises from $17.36$ to $66.60\%$ ($+49.23$ points, $95\%$ interval $[45.34,53.04]$). NIAH improves by $64.45$ points and PPL AUC decreases by $3.853$; all $13$ task AUC differences are positive. This confirms the method direction on a second model family. TailSpline/MrPro reach the generation cap on $133/142$ of $390$ OLMo inputs, so official scores do not imply complete-answer-plus-EOS success. Full responses and interval definitions appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details}.

\subsection{BM on natural QA and broader evidence}
On frozen OLMo, symmetric boundary matching improves each of five natural-QA task means (Table~\ref{tab:bm-qa-main}). The complete available untruncated pool contains $778$ paired inputs; the reported long-input stratum contains $631$ inputs exceeding $4$K. With the same band, $s=4$, amplitude and decoder, macro F1 rises from $21.62$ to $25.44$, a $3.82$-point gain with paired interval $[1.32,6.29]$.

An equal-displacement control supplies a complementary shape test: BM and MrRoPE-Uni have the same endpoints, band, gain and total log-frequency displacement, yet score $51.32$ versus $32.12\%$ on the recorded six-task $16$K panel (Appendix~\ref{sec:four-method-control}). Thus total displacement alone does not explain that comparison.

These constructions have distinct tested scopes: BM's natural-QA result remains a separate experiment from TailSpline's two-model confirmation. Small Qwen $128$K panels favor MrPro over BM ($78.13/70.83\%$ at $3$B and $84.44/71.11\%$ at $7$B). Likewise, changing a table after learning can disrupt its association with Q/K coordinates; the range, crossed-table and slot controls in Appendix~\ref{sec:compatibility} explain why training and frozen deployment need different constructions. They do not impose a fixed-support restriction on complete methods.

Additional evidence broadens the picture without mixing protocols. A frozen Qwen-$0.5$B configuration improves $64$K RULER-13 over YaRN by $6.09$ points (Appendix~\ref{sec:index-adjustment}); selective-Q/K adaptation improves long-input natural QA (Appendix~\ref{sec:olmo2-1b}); Video DiT shows temporal extrapolation gains (Appendix~\ref{sec:video-dit}). M4 includes preliminary joint in-window/OOD improvements, while C42 isolates an internal-shape effect at fixed total displacement (Appendices~\ref{sec:exact-range-factorial},~\ref{sec:fixed-total-profile}). These support the wider design space rather than a universal table or a mature in-window enhancement claim.
