\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup and endpoints}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

NLL/PPL, teacher-forced NLL-gap retrieval, causal source use, strict
autoregressive exact match, task F1/exact match, and RULER macro are kept as
separate endpoints throughout. \evq{} installs a different frequency table
before training; every trained system is evaluated with its own table.
Controlled from-scratch runs use fixed GPT-style decoder stacks
\citep{radford2019gpt2}; corpora, hyperparameters and seed scope are in
App.~\ref{sec:supporting-experiments} and with each result.

\subsection{Identifying the allocation axis}
\label{sec:exp-identify}

\paragraph{Protocol.} In the seed-$42$ pair, two $151.9$M models
share architecture, trainable initialisation, token order, optimiser, LR
schedule, batch geometry, a $499{,}974{,}144$-token budget, and $32$ frozen
evaluation anchors. The baseline is our paper-faithful implementation of the
uniform-in-log rule in \citet[\S6.1]{oka2026fmrope}, using training base
$256$; the intervention is endpoint-normalised cosh spacing at $\tau{=}4$, a
diagnostic distinct from the deployed midpoint \evq{} grid. Their sampled
extrema and log-frequency span are identical, so only the
$K{-}2=30$ interior frequencies move. We measure paired final-$128$-token
teacher-forced NLL.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{fig_identification.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Allocation is identifiable.} (a)~With sampled extrema and
  log-span pinned, the endpoint-normalised diagnostic moves only the $30$
  interior frequencies and shifts trained-model OOD NLL (blue, seed $42$;
  anchors $32/32$, $27/32$, $22/32$); the contrast
  reverses once \emph{both} grids are target-retargeted (red; $5/32$, $1/32$,
  $2/32$). (b)~In a separate three-seed, $12$-configuration factorial the
  $1.25\times$ Cosh and matched-exponential arms favour non-uniform allocation in
  $10/12$ and $9/12$ configurations.}
  \label{fig:identification}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Result.} At the fixed training range, cosh-minus-uniform NLL is
$-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K in the seed-$42$ arm
(Fig.~\ref{fig:identification}a). Since every sampled scalar range quantity is
pinned, the effect cannot be attributed to base or span selection: \emph{fixed
range $+$ different interior allocation $\Rightarrow$ different trained-model
behaviour.}

\paragraph{Cross-configuration replication.} A pre-specified $50.9$M
factorial spans two bases, two training lengths, three head dimensions, and
three paired seeds, with additional boundary-$\tau$ arms. It repeats the
exact-range control and scores weighted
teacher-forced OOD NLL over $2\times/4\times/8\times$, using the $12$
structural configurations as the statistical units (Table~\ref{tab:m4}). The
pre-specified $1.25\times$ cosh and matched-exponential schedules favour the
non-uniform allocation in $10/12$ and $9/12$ configurations. The factorial
establishes cross-configuration direction; the $151.9$M pair provides the
direct effect size.

\paragraph{Range and allocation are different controls.} Retargeting
\emph{both} grids to each evaluation length reverses the seed-$42$ ordering
(Fig.~\ref{fig:identification}a, red). Fixed-range allocation and target-aware
range selection therefore answer different questions. The fixed-range arm
identifies the interior-allocation axis; when range is free to track the target,
target-aware range selection is the stronger lever in this protocol.

\subsection{Matched table-conditioned systems at mature scale}
\label{sec:exp-mature}

\paragraph{$1.485$B: an effective-context trade-off.}
On OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct \citep{olmo2furious}, Native and \evq{} start from
their matched Stage-A parents, freeze inherited V/O LoRA tensors, and continue
only Q/K LoRA for $300$ steps.  Both arms use the same data, order, optimiser,
trainable parameter count, and $1{:}1{:}2$ mixture of continuous-$4$K,
target-$8$K, and target-$16$K phase exposure; physical sequences remain at
most $4$K. Native/\evq{} macro from the official scorer over all $13$ RULER families
\citep{hsieh2024ruler} is $72.19/42.44\%$ at $4$K, $2.02/31.63\%$ at $8$K,
and $0.38/5.03\%$ at $16$K. Thus Native retains the stronger in-window score,
whereas \evq{} carries more capability beyond the physical training cap; equal
phase exposure does not erase the table-conditioned crossover.

\paragraph{$1.485$B held-out multi-hop QA.}
The same Q/K-only design, trained independently for 2WikiMultiHopQA, gives
Native/\evq{} exact match of $22.0/21.5\%$ at $4$K, $0/17.5\%$ at $8$K, and
$0/4.0\%$ at $16$K on $200$ held-out examples per length
\citep{bai2024longbench}; token F1 is $25.99/24.84\%$, $0.07/21.48\%$, and
$0/8.57\%$. The two substrates acquire nearly identical in-window QA while only
\evq{} carries that capability to the longer positions.

\paragraph{$1.485$B from initialisation.}
A same-initialisation, same-scientific-recipe step-$0\!\rightarrow\!1{,}000$
trajectory over $2.097$B tokens gives \evq{}-minus-Geo NLL of
$+0.072/+0.038/-0.044/-0.135$ at $2$/$4$/$8$/$16$K, with $122/128$ and
$126/128$ held-out documents favouring \evq{} at $8$/$16$K. The released Geo
checkpoint and \evq{} arm use different trainer implementations, so this is
not a bitwise-paired comparison. It shows that the large post-hoc mismatch of
Table~\ref{tab:coadapt} is not an intrinsic in-window training cost.

\paragraph{$8$B length-transfer trade-off.} On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, a separate matched physical-$8$K continuation
over all $13$ RULER families yields official-scorer macro $94.44/77.60\%$ at $8$K and
$0.295/14.03\%$ at $16$K; normalized exact is $17.69/21.54\%$ and
$0/1.54\%$, respectively (App.~Table~\ref{tab:ruler}). Native again has the
stronger in-window official score while \evq{} retains the stronger $2\times$
endpoint. Training and evaluation
rows are disjoint but share the generator families, so the OLMo and LLaMA
results establish task-adapted length transfer rather than unseen-task
transfer.

\subsection{Attributing the schedule and operating rule}
\label{sec:exp-tau}

\paragraph{The rule selects a basin, not a point optimum.} Across $21$
configurations in two studies, the best tested $\tau$ stays within a
$0.75\times$--$1.5\times$ window around the operating rule. An independent
sweep selects $\tau{=}5$ against a rule value of $5.657$, a $0.0119$ NLL
difference. Within the factorial, the rule is the best of the three tested Cosh
multipliers in $4/12$ configurations, while $1.25\times$ and $0.75\times$ are
best in $6/12$ and $2/12$. The scaling structure is useful; its finite
coefficient is empirical.

\paragraph{Cosh is a derived instance, not the only effective shape.} The
\emph{matched exponential} is the arm $\rho\propto e^{-\tau'\phi}$ whose
parameter is fixed so that its $L^1$ deformation from the uniform density equals
that of rule-cosh, so the two arms move the same amount of budget with different
curvature. It improves over Geo in $9/12$
configurations and is not separated from rule-cosh: cosh-minus-exponential is
$+0.00074$ NLL, bootstrap $[-0.0055,0.0075]$, exact sign-flip $p{=}0.836$.
The empirical result identifies interior allocation. Cosh contributes a
closed-form, zero-parameter solution of the stated convex surrogate, not a
uniqueness claim for trained objectives. Shape attribution therefore rests on
the zero-parameter fixed schedules above, where parameter count and
optimisation effort are identical by construction.
