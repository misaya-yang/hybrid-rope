\paragraph{Cross-model confirmation and direct baselines.}
OLMo's clean $16$K panel contains $2{,}600$ pairs and improves by $41.42$
points, with positive differences on all $13$ tasks and all four family
intervals. GLM extends this comparison to partial RoPE: at $128$K,
its Full-13 score rises from $31.56$ to $44.36\%$ on $65$ paired inputs.
Only its rotary subspace is changed; nonrotary coordinates are retained.

Direct comparisons with official static YaRN \citep{peng2024yarn}, installed
without training, test the rule against another established baseline.
On matched NIAH-8 and five-document PPL panels, TailSpline improves both
metrics over both baselines on Llama, OLMo and GLM. Its NIAH gains over YaRN
are $10.00/54.38/23.75$ points, respectively; Qwen's scores are close.
Complete three-method scores and paired intervals are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:three-method-complete}.

\paragraph{Natural question answering.}
The five-task Natural-QA pool contains $631$ questions from $524$ source
contexts. OLMo's task-macro whole-response F1 improves by $3.30$ points,
with positive differences on all five tasks; Llama's scores are close.
LongBench v2 \citep{bai2025longbenchv2} adds $117$ complete Llama inputs

\subsection{Improving task performance within RoPE's native context}
\label{sec:ncp-main}
Optimizing $z$ improves task performance within RoPE's native context. We construct
\emph{Native Contrastive Proximal} allocation (NCP) from the public native grid
and length. A binary reference comparison models an aligned target competing
with a uniformly phased distractor. Averaging its logistic loss over relative
separations gives a reference risk $\mathcal R(\phi)$ at phase span
$\phi=(L-1)\omega$. NCP selects log-frequency shifts $u_k$ through
\begin{equation}
\min_u\ \sum_k\left[\mathcal R(\phi_ke^{-u_k})+\frac{9}{8}u_k^2\right],
\qquad \omega'_k=\omega_ke^{-u_k}.
\label{eq:ncp-main}
\end{equation}
The quadratic term keeps the allocation near its native grid. We fix both
endpoints, bound shifts, and preserve frequency ordering and minimum log gaps;
Appendix~\ref{sec:ncp-construction-new} gives the reference model, constraints
and numerical construction. Gain remains $1$, and the same table is installed
across layers and inputs without weight updates or calibration.

On OLMo's native $4$K clean Full-13 panel ($780$ paired prompts), NCP raises
the score from $69.37$ to $70.78\%$, a $+1.41$-point gain with paired interval
$[0.20,2.58]$. This gain establishes a direct in-window benefit from optimizing
frequency allocation at fixed support. Appendix~\ref{sec:ncp-results-new}
reports the complete task breakdown, including the largest gain in variable
tracking ($+23$ points), and companion direction controls.

\subsection{Supporting extrapolation evidence with Cosh}
\label{sec:exp-capability}
Cosh studies connect frequency allocation during training to mature-model
adaptation; Section~\ref{sec:controlled-findings} isolates placement within
this line. A $432$M MLA-style model with $16$ rotary pairs trains for $500$M
tokens at $8$K across three seeds \citep{deepseekv2,deepseekv3}, using
unanchored midpoint Cosh. It improves
all five extended lengths in every seed; $16$K PPL falls from $138.8$ to $95.6$,
and the benefit persists under a common wavelength blend
(Fig.~\ref{fig:allocation-learning}). An endpoint-anchored $750$M shared-start continuation reduces
$16$K PPL from $45.1$ to $24.4$ and raises $8$K answer-token exact match
from $0/40$ to $31/40$.
\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.60\linewidth]{fig_allocation_learning.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Cosh improves extrapolation across training seeds.} Three-seed
mean and range for the $432$M MLA model; dashed curves use the common wavelength
blend ($s=4$). Evaluation uses common text chunks.}
\label{fig:allocation-learning}
\end{figure}
The long-range gains extend to recipe-matched $1.485$B OLMo training and
matched Llama-$3$-$8$B LoRA, whose $16$K PPL falls from $108.96$ to $24.07$.
With phase-exposure and answer-plus-EOS supervision, matched OLMo adaptation
also improves complete-answer retrieval: Native/Cosh score $18/98\%$ at $8$K
and $0/60\%$ at $16$K. Appendix~\ref{sec:compact-learning} separates these
training and adaptation protocols and their native-window results.
