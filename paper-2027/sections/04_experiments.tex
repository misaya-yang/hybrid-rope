\section{Turning Allocation into Model Gains}
\label{sec:experiments}
\label{sec:exp-mature}

The preceding results identify a useful design freedom and the role of learning in realizing it. We next construct an explicit allocation, test whether its effects extend across shapes, and follow its use through training, continuation, and adaptation.

\subsection{Evidence across allocation families}
\label{sec:factorial}

A $50.9$M factorial compares geometric allocation, three Cosh strengths, and a deformation-matched exponential family across two bases, two training lengths, three head dimensions, and three paired seeds. Each configuration retains identical sampled endpoints. Reference Cosh improves weighted OOD NLL in $7/12$ configurations; the preassigned $1.25\times$ strength improves $10/12$, and matched exponential improves $9/12$. The reference-Cosh-minus-exponential aggregate is $+0.00074$ NLL with $95\%$ interval $[-0.006,0.008]$ (Table~\ref{tab:m4}). Multiple analytic shapes therefore realize useful changes along the allocation coordinate. These $128$-step runs use a repeated WikiText-2 training stream and average about $0.01$ NLL improvement; Fig.~\ref{fig:m4-operating-points} jointly displays their training-window and OOD effects. They broaden the evidence across allocation families; the longer paired runs establish the main learning effect.

\subsection{Learning with the constructed basis}
\label{sec:exp-capability}

\paragraph{Allocating scarce rotary channels.}
In a $432$M MLA-style model, only $16$ pairs carry explicit rotation \citep{deepseekv2,deepseekv3}. Across three seeds, training on $500$M tokens at length $8192$ with the Cosh table reduces $16$K PPL from $138.8$ to $95.6$, while $8$K PPL is $35.4/35.8$ for Geo/Cosh (Table~\ref{tab:training-models}). Applying the same wavelength-blend rule to both tables gives $117.9/71.1$ at $16$K; the combined Cosh configuration retains the lowest mean PPL at $24$K and $32$K. Allocation has practical value when a small explicit rotary budget must support longer contexts.

\paragraph{Learning a new allocation from a shared checkpoint.}
A $750$M pair continues full-parameter training from the same $2$K Geo checkpoint for $500$M tokens at length $4096$. At $16$K, PPL falls from $45.1$ to $24.4$; at $4$K it is $22.0/22.3$. On $40$ associated $8$K passkey cases, fixed-length greedy answer-token exact match rises from $0/40$ to $31/40$, while both teacher-forced retrieval probes remain saturated. Continued learning can therefore turn the allocation change into an improvement in generated answers as well as text likelihood. The single-seed protocol includes $3\%$ passkey and $10\%$ downstream training mix (Appendix~\ref{sec:larger-scale-supporting}).

\paragraph{Learning to use distant sources.}
A matched $300$-step Llama-$3$-$8$B LoRA pair applies the same $8$K adaptation to native-endpoint Geo and midpoint Cosh tables \citep{hu2022lora,grattafiori2024llama3}. Native/Cosh PPL is $6.82/10.07$ at $8$K, $108.96/24.07$ at $16$K, and $991.48/127.91$ at $32$K. Removing remote gold-block access raises answer NLL by $1.5055$ for Cosh versus $-0.0095$ for Native. Source use improves, while $16$K exact retrieval remains zero and the registered QA comparison favors Native (Appendix~\ref{sec:llama8b}).

\paragraph{Learning to produce complete answers.}
A separate OLMo study tests successful readout under explicit task supervision. On OLMo-$1.485$B, matched $100$-step query-gap and $32$-step answer-plus-EOS continuations give Native/Cosh complete-string-plus-EOS accuracy of $95\%/100\%$ at $4$K, $18\%/98\%$ at $8$K, and $0\%/60\%$ at $16$K, with $100$ prompts per length. These runs use at most $4$K physical tokens and target-range position IDs. A separate selective-Q/K continuation improves held-out $2$Wiki F1 at $8/16$K from $0.07/0\%$ to $21.48/8.57\%$ ($200$ rows/length; Appendix~\ref{sec:olmo2-1b}).

\paragraph{Further evidence.}
The same design question extends to $454$M three-seed range composition ($16$K PPL $157.7\to107.5$), $1.485$B training, video DiT, and a five-configuration GQA/MLA compression study. Their full results and protocols appear in Apps.~\ref{sec:range-composition}, \ref{sec:olmo2-1b}, and~\ref{sec:compression-ablation}. Together with the paired identification and factorial, these studies show that allocation is usable through several forms of learning. We next examine how to exploit it when the weights are already fixed.
