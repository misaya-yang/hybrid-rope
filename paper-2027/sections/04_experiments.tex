We test Cosh under matched learning recipes and TailSpline/BM under frozen deployment. Matched adaptation tests readout after learning and is reported separately. Table~\ref{tab:protocol-glossary} summarizes the protocols.

\subsection{Learning to use an explicit allocation}
\label{sec:exp-capability}
Does allocation help across lengths and throughout learning? A $432$M MLA-style model uses $16$ rotary pairs, $500$M training tokens at $8$K and three seeds \citep{deepseekv2,deepseekv3}. This shared-corpus length stress test does not establish document-disjoint evaluation; its unanchored midpoint Cosh table also changes sampled endpoints (Appendix~\ref{sec:mla-results}).

Cosh improves all five extended lengths from $16$K through $32$K in every seed (Fig.~\ref{fig:allocation-basis}b); its $16$K advantage is present at half and three-quarters of training (Table~\ref{tab:training-models}).

Applying the same wavelength blend to both learned tables preserves the advantage. Thus allocation remains useful across training checkpoints and when composed with the shared scaling rule (Appendix~\ref{sec:mla-results}).

A $750$M pair continues from the same $2$K Geo checkpoint for $500$M tokens at $4$K. Cosh reduces $16$K PPL by $45.9\%$ and raises $8$K greedy answer-token exact match from $0/40$ to $31/40$, with a small $4$K PPL cost. This single-seed continuation includes $3\%$ passkey and $10\%$ downstream mix (Appendix~\ref{sec:larger-scale-supporting}).

Allocation can also support complete answers after matched adaptation. The OLMo pair in Table~\ref{tab:training-models} undergoes the same routing and answer-plus-EOS continuation. It uses physical inputs of at most $4$K with explicit target-range position IDs, then evaluates real contiguous prompts. The $8/16$K gains therefore demonstrate successful length transfer after phase exposure; they are separate from our zero-training results. Appendix~\ref{sec:olmo2-1b} retains the complete lineage, supervision and native-window controls.

\subsection{Zero-training deployment on Llama and OLMo}
\label{sec:tailspline-evaluation}
We compare exact finite-grid TailSpline and MrRoPE-Pro on frozen Llama-$3$-$8$B-Instruct, using $s=4$, native window $8192$, band $[18,35]$, and common gain $1.1386294361$.

Each method installs one static table; weights, inputs, greedy decoder, precision and scorer are shared. A depth-balanced RULER-13 panel has $10$ paired prompts per task at $8/16/32$K ($390$ per arm). NIAH depth selection and fixed-budget padding use no model outputs (Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-implementation}). PPL uses $32$ Proof-Pile-test and $14$ PG-19-test documents ($138$ rows per arm) \citep{azerbayev2022proofpile,rae2020compressive}.

We aggregate lengths using normalized trapezoidal area in log length: weights $1/4,1/2,1/4$ at $8/16/32$K. TailSpline raises RULER-13 AUC from $75.60$ to $78.80\%$, a $+3.20$-point difference (paired $95\%$ interval $[+0.65,+5.79]$). NIAH AUC improves by $3.75$ points ($[+0.55,+7.03]$); its single-key passkey subtask has AUC $100.0$ versus $97.5\%$. These are task-equal scores under the recorded scorers, including partial credit.

PPL AUC decreases from $4.62112$ to $4.61664$ ($-0.097\%$), with paired difference $-0.00449$ and interval $[-0.00872,-0.00121]$. Relative to MrPro, PPL slightly increases at $8/16$K and decreases at $32$K (Fig.~\ref{fig:tailspline-main}d). Full-13 improves at all three lengths. Retrieval, tracking and aggregation improve in AUC; QA decreases by $1.25$ points. Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details} gives the complete available results.


\paragraph{Prospective OLMo confirmation.}
The same frozen formula is tested on OLMo-$2$-$1$B-Instruct at $s=4$, band $[14,32]$ and $4/8/16$K, with $390$ RULER prompts and $138$ LM rows per arm. The exact table was frozen before reading this OLMo evaluation; historical OLMo development had supplied direction priors. Full-13 AUC rises from $17.36$ to $66.60\%$ ($+49.23$ points, $95\%$ interval $[45.34,53.04]$). NIAH improves by $64.45$ points and PPL AUC decreases by $3.853$; all $13$ task AUC differences are positive. This confirms the method direction on a second model family. TailSpline and MrPro reach the generation cap on $133$ and $142$ of $390$ OLMo inputs, respectively, so official scores do not imply complete-answer-plus-EOS success. Full responses and interval definitions appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:tailspline-details}.

\subsection{Natural QA with the symmetric construction}
The complementary symmetric construction, BM, tests allocation design on natural outputs. On frozen OLMo it improves each of five task means (Table~\ref{tab:bm-qa-main}). The complete available untruncated pool contains $778$ paired inputs; the reported long-input stratum contains $631$ inputs exceeding $4$K. With the same band, $s=4$, amplitude and decoder, macro F1 rises from $21.62$ to $25.44$, a $3.82$-point gain with paired interval $[1.32,6.29]$.

BM's natural-QA result complements TailSpline's two-model confirmation and the equal-displacement control in \S\ref{sec:equal-displacement}.
