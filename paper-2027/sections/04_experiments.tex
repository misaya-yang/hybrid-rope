\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup and endpoints}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

We test a sequence of increasingly demanding questions: whether allocation is
identifiable at fixed spectral range; whether the effect survives from $50.9$M
to $8$B; which parts of the closed-form prescription the data support; and
whether the same range operator behaves differently on the resulting
substrates. NLL/PPL, teacher-forced NLL-gap retrieval, causal source use,
strict autoregressive exact match, task F1/exact match, and RULER macro remain
separate endpoints throughout. \evq{} changes only the training-time frequency
table and adds no learned parameters.

The controlled from-scratch runs use fixed GPT-style decoder stacks
\citep{radford2019gpt2}, with per-experiment corpora and hyperparameters in
App.~\ref{sec:supporting-experiments}. Seed scope is reported with each result.

\paragraph{Range-scaling arm.} All arms labelled $+\rs{}$ use our fixed-index
smooth-ramp scaler from the NTK-by-parts / YaRN family \citep{peng2024yarn}.
The same implementation and scale are applied to Geo and \evq{}, isolating
substrate-dependent leverage of that fixed operator. It is not an official
YaRN reproduction or a jointly tuned range-method comparison; the exact rule
is given in App.~\ref{sec:ramp-scaler}.

\subsection{Identifying the allocation axis}
\label{sec:exp-identify}

\emph{Question.} Is training-time allocation separable from base and range
selection at all, or is the observed effect a reparameterisation of scalar
range quantities?

\paragraph{Protocol.} In the seed-$42$ pair, two $151.9$M models
share architecture, trainable initialisation, token order, optimiser, LR
schedule, batch geometry, a $499{,}974{,}144$-token budget, and $32$ frozen
evaluation anchors. The baseline is our paper-faithful implementation of the
uniform-in-log rule in \citet[\S6.1]{oka2026fmrope}, using training base
$256$; the intervention is endpoint-normalised cosh spacing at $\tau{=}4$.
Their sampled extrema and log-frequency span are identical, so only the
$K{-}2=30$ interior frequencies move. We measure paired final-$128$-token
teacher-forced NLL.

\paragraph{Result.} At the fixed training range, cosh-minus-uniform NLL is
$-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at $512$/$1$K/$2$K in the seed-$42$ arm
(Fig.~\ref{fig:identification}a). Since every sampled scalar range quantity is
pinned, the effect cannot be attributed to base or span selection: \emph{fixed
range $+$ different interior allocation $\Rightarrow$ different trained-model
behaviour.}

\paragraph{Cross-configuration replication.} A pre-specified $50.9$M
factorial spans two bases, two training lengths, three head dimensions, and
three paired seeds, with additional boundary-$\tau$ arms. It repeats the
exact-range control and scores weighted
teacher-forced OOD NLL over $2\times/4\times/8\times$, using the $12$
structural configurations as the statistical units (Table~\ref{tab:m4}). The
pre-specified $1.25\times$ cosh and matched-exponential schedules favour the
non-uniform allocation in $10/12$ and $9/12$ configurations. The factorial
establishes cross-configuration direction; the $151.9$M pair provides the
direct effect size.

\paragraph{Range and allocation are different controls.} Retargeting
\emph{both} grids to each evaluation length reverses the seed-$42$ ordering
(Fig.~\ref{fig:identification}a, red). Fixed-range allocation and target-aware
range selection therefore answer different questions: the former identifies
the interior-allocation axis; the latter can dominate once range is allowed to
track the target.

\subsection{Phase-matched attribution at mature scale}
\label{sec:exp-mature}

\emph{Question.} Once long-range phase exposure is held constant, does the
frequency table remain a binding variable on $1$B--$8$B checkpoints and
autoregressive effective-context tasks?

\paragraph{$1.485$B: matching phase exposure does not erase the gap.}
On OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct \citep{olmo2furious}, Native and \evq{} start from
their matched Stage-A parents, freeze inherited V/O LoRA tensors, and continue
only Q/K LoRA for $300$ steps.  Both arms use the same data, order, optimiser,
trainable parameter count, and $1{:}1{:}2$ mixture of continuous-$4$K,
target-$8$K, and target-$16$K phase exposure; physical sequences remain at
most $4$K.  The active method variable is the fixed frequency table.  On all
$13$ RULER families \citep{hsieh2024ruler}, Native/\evq{} official macro is
$72.19/42.44\%$ at $4$K, $2.02/31.63\%$ at $8$K, and $0.38/5.03\%$ at
$16$K.  Native receives the same long-phase exposure yet remains at $2.02\%$
at $8$K: exposure alone cannot explain the $15.7\times$ score gap.

\paragraph{$1.485$B held-out multi-hop QA.}
The same Q/K-only design, trained independently for 2WikiMultiHopQA, gives
Native/\evq{} exact match of $22.0/21.5\%$ at $4$K, $0/17.5\%$ at $8$K,
and $0/4.0\%$ at $16$K on $200$ held-out examples per length
\citep{bai2024longbench}; token F1 is $25.99/24.84\%$, $0.07/21.48\%$, and
$0/8.57\%$.  Thus the two substrates acquire nearly identical in-window QA
while only \evq{} carries that capability to the longer positions.  The
prompts use the official questions and contexts with deterministic,
answer-filtered distractor filling to realise each length.

\paragraph{Independent $1.485$B generation and language modelling.}
A matched rank-$64$ Q/K/V/O continuation \citep{hu2022lora} moves $8$K
\texttt{niah\_single\_1} strict first-generated-number exact from $0/100$ to
$69/100$; an independently trained \evq{} seed gives $67/100$.  On a disjoint
set where every source-to-generation gap exceeds the largest training gap,
the scores are $0/100$ versus $49/100$ and $48/100$.  The same matched seed
changes natural-text NLL from $3.735/4.851$ to $2.703/2.925$ at $8$/$16$K.
Separately, a same-initialisation step-$0\!\rightarrow\!1{,}000$ trajectory over
$2.097$B tokens yields \evq{}-minus-Geo NLL of
$+0.072/+0.038/-0.044/-0.135$ at $2$/$4$/$8$/$16$K, with $122/128$ and
$126/128$ held-out documents favouring \evq{} at $8$K and $16$K.

\paragraph{$8$B causal source use and RULER.} On LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori2024llama3}, matched seed-$42$ ordinary-LM adaptation gives
\evq{}-minus-Native-LoRA NLL of $+0.390/-1.510/-2.048$ at
$8$/$16$/$32$K. On ten frozen true-$16$K retrieval cases from this protocol,
median target-block hit@$16$ over $32$ heads rises from $18.75\%$ to
$64.06\%$; deleting the remote gold block changes NLL by $-0.0095$ for Native
and $+1.5055$ for \evq{}, establishing causal source use in this diagnostic.
A separate identical physical-$8$K, $13$-family adaptation yields official
macro $94.44/77.60\%$ at $8$K and $0.295/14.03\%$ at $16$K; normalized exact
is $17.69/21.54\%$ and $0/1.54\%$, respectively
(App.~Table~\ref{tab:ruler}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The 2Wiki and RULER rows are disjoint from training but use
the same task families, so they measure task-adapted length transfer.  Each
matched mature-scale protocol uses one training seed per arm; the second OLMo
\evq{} seed replicates only strict NIAH.  The $8$B deletion study and RULER
continuation are separate protocols.

\subsection{Attributing the schedule and operating rule}
\label{sec:exp-tau}

\emph{Question.} Do the trained-model gains identify cosh or the formula value
of $\tau$, rather than the broader allocation axis?

\paragraph{The rule selects a basin, not a point optimum.} Across the $12$
factorial configurations, the formula point is the best of the three
pre-specified cosh multipliers in $4$; $1.25\times$ wins $6$ and $0.75\times$
wins $2$. At the pre-specified boundaries, formula $\tau{=}1$ prefers
$1.5\times$, whereas formula $\tau{=}8$ prefers $0.75\times$. An independent
sweep selects $\tau{=}5$, with the rule value $5.657$ only $0.0119$ NLL away.
Thus $\tau{=}\deff/\sqrt{\Ltr}$ is a search-free basin selector, while the
finite coefficient is empirical.

\paragraph{Cosh is a derived instance, not the only effective shape.} The
exact-range deformation-matched exponential improves over Geo in $9/12$
configurations and is not separated from rule-cosh: cosh-minus-exponential is
$+0.00074$ NLL, bootstrap $[-0.0055,0.0075]$, exact sign-flip $p{=}0.836$.
The empirical result identifies interior allocation. Cosh contributes a
closed-form, zero-parameter solution of the stated convex surrogate, not a
uniqueness claim for trained objectives. The learned-frequency comparator and
its tuning budget appear in App.~\ref{sec:pe-dominant}; shape attribution rests
on the fixed schedules above.

\subsection{Composition under range scaling and channel scarcity}
\label{sec:exp-substrate}

\paragraph{Same operator, different substrate.} We next ask whether a fixed
range operator has substrate-dependent leverage. On a $454$M model with
$\Ltr{=}2048$ and a $10\%$ passkey mix ($3$ seeds),
$\evq{}+\rs{}$ reaches $100{\pm}0\%$ teacher-forced NLL-gap retrieval at $8$K
against $61{\pm}3\%$ for $\mathrm{Geo}+\rs{}$. At $12$K/$16$K the scores are
$79/68\%$ versus $59/51\%$, and PPL is $70.9/107.5$ versus $82.9/157.7$
(Table~\ref{tab:evq-ramp}). This is substrate-dependent leverage of the tested
fixed $s{=}8$ operator. \citet{karypis2026lerope} independently report the
same qualitative interaction for a learned non-geometric table.

\paragraph{Scarce-channel MLA.}\label{sec:exp-mla}
MLA \citep{deepseekv2, deepseekv3} provides a direct test with few rotary
frequencies. The $432$M configuration uses $\dhd{=}64$,
$\drot{=}32$ ($16$ frequency pairs), $d_{\mathrm{nope}}{=}32$, and KV rank
$256$; it trains at $\Ltr{=}8192$ for $500$M tokens with learning rate
$2\times10^{-4}$. Across three seeds, the pre-specified $\tau{=}1.414$ schedule
changes $2\times$ extrapolation PPL from $138.8$ to $95.6$ ($-31.1\%$), with
$+0.9\%$ at the training length (App.~\ref{sec:mla-results}). Thus the
allocation effect persists when only $16$ rotary frequency pairs are
available.
