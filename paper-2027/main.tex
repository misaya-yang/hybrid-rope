\begin{abstract}
RoPE has a finite spectral budget: each head must place only $K=\drot/2$
rotary frequencies before training. The standard geometric table spends this
budget uniformly in log-frequency, while long-context methods usually alter or
transport its range; we ask whether interior allocation still matters when
that range is fixed. In a $151.9$M exact-range matched pair, only the $30$
interior frequencies differ, and endpoint-normalized Cosh minus the uniform
grid changes trained-model OOD NLL by $-0.478/-0.205/-0.113$ at
$512$/$1$K/$2$K. In a separate $12$-configuration, three-seed factorial,
pre-specified Cosh and matched-exponential allocations beat the uniform grid
in $10/12$ and $9/12$ configurations. We then formulate a convex variational
collision surrogate whose unique minimiser is a cosh density; quantising its
inverse CDF gives \evq{}, a closed-form schedule with no learned parameters
and geometric RoPE as its $\tau{\to}0$ limit. The allocation remains binding
at mature scale. On a $1.485$B checkpoint, Native and \evq{} receive identical
Q/K-only adaptation and identical phase exposure through $16$K; their $8$K
RULER scores are $2.02\%$ and $31.63\%$. A matched $8$B continuation reaches
$14.03\%$ versus $0.295\%$ at $16$K. Thus range determines where the spectrum
lies, while allocation determines how a finite set of channels occupies it.
\end{abstract}
