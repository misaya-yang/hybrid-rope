Beyond the Base: Frequency Allocation in RoPE

Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) typically tie their frequency placement to a single base. We study frequency allocation as a design dimension for model quality within and beyond the native context window. Controlled interventions show that internal placement matters even when the frequency range and total log-frequency displacement are fixed. Full sine–cosine geometry characterizes positional overlap, while coordinate interventions distinguish this structure from the model's learned use of frequencies. We introduce TailSpline by formulating and solving a discrete allocation problem for the transition into the extended low-frequency tail. Its closed-form rule retains standard rotary computation without weight updates or calibration. Matched evaluations show strong long-context task gains across model families, including natural question answering, with a slight native-window reduction. A complementary native-window construction improves in-window task performance using only public RoPE parameters. Cosh transport supplies supporting learning and extrapolation evidence. Together, these results establish internal frequency placement as a practical design dimension for both context extension and in-window quality.
