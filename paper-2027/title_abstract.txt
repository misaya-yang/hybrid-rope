Beyond the Base: Frequency Allocation in RoPE

Rotary position embeddings (RoPE) typically tie their frequency placement to a single base. We study frequency allocation as a design dimension for quality within and beyond the native context window. Controlled interventions show that internal placement matters even when the frequency range and total log-frequency displacement are fixed. Full sine--cosine geometry characterizes positional overlap, while coordinate interventions distinguish this structure from the model's learned use of frequencies. We introduce TailSpline by formulating and solving a discrete allocation problem for the transition into the extended low-frequency tail. Its closed-form rule retains standard rotary computation without weight updates or calibration. Matched comparisons with established extension rules show improvements in retrieval and language modeling across model families and scales, together with gains on natural question answering. Fixed-support interventions improve native-window language modeling, while training and adaptation with Cosh transport yield stronger extrapolation. These results connect internal frequency placement to how effectively models use context, both within the native window and beyond it.
