Beyond the Base: Frequency Allocation in RoPE

In rotary position embeddings (RoPE), reallocating frequencies within a fixed range can substantially change how effectively a model uses context. Controlled interventions show that performance gains persist even when total log-frequency displacement is also matched. Analysis of complete sine–cosine pairs characterizes positional overlap; reassigning the same frequencies across coordinates changes task performance, demonstrating the role of learned frequency use. Viewing allocation as channel-wise distance scaling motivates TailSpline, a closed-form transition from native frequencies to the extended low-frequency tail. Derived from a discrete tail-connection problem, the construction uses only public RoPE parameters and retains standard rotary computation without weight updates or calibration. Matched comparisons with YaRN and MrRoPE show improvements in retrieval and language modeling across model families and scales, together with gains on natural question answering. Allocation also improves native-window language modeling with frozen weights and strengthens extrapolation during training and adaptation. These findings establish internal frequency allocation as a distinct design dimension of RoPE that shapes how models use context within and beyond the native window.
