\subsection{Reconstructing the twelve-profile response analysis}
\label{sec:profile-diagnostic-audit}

\paragraph{Statistic and evaluation unit.}
For OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct, $K=64$, $b=500{,}000$, native window $4096$, and test distance $D=16384$, define
\begin{equation}
T_j(D;m)=\frac{D}{2\pi}b^{-j/K}4^{-m_j},\qquad
N(m)=\sum_{j=0}^{63}\mathbf1\{0.25\le T_j(D;m)\le16\}.
\label{eq:profile-count}
\end{equation}
This is a band-count statistic. The thresholds are exploratory choices examined on the same twelve profiles, not calibrated-readout boundaries. All arms use $g=1+0.1\ln4=1.1386294$, $350$ shared row identities, seven equally sized RULER task groups, and $16$K inputs. The score is the mean saved task score in $[0,1]$, including partial credit; EOS is recorded separately. The $350$ inputs estimate each profile's task score, whereas the configuration-level analysis has twelve profiles. MrPro and BM use archived endpoint outputs; the other ten arms have separate saved outputs.

\paragraph{Fit and scope.}
Unweighted least squares of the logit-transformed mean score on $N$ gives
$\operatorname{logit}(\bar r)=-20.45+1.19N$. Among $66$ profile pairs, $39$ have different counts and concordant scores, $0$ have different counts and discordant scores, and $27$ tie in count. The count therefore describes between-group ordering and does not resolve within-group differences. This is a descriptive fit to fractional scores, not a binomial success model or an independent predictive test. Threshold choices and coefficients were analyzed using these same profiles. 

\paragraph{Reconstruction and provenance.}
The accompanying \path{figs/profile_diagnostic_inputs.json} records every $m_j$, full reconstruction digest, row-output digest, score sum, and fit coefficient. The common row-identity digest is \texttt{14736f284ac30c0c}. Matching row identities checks panel alignment in the saved outputs; the available records do not supply a new prompt-byte comparison for the archived endpoints. The analysis therefore reports the response of the recorded profiles.


\paragraph{Testing the interpretation beyond the fitted family.}
A subsequent matched continuous-text evaluation at $16$K on $16$ documents increased the count from $N=17$ for BM to $22$--$26$ by releasing slow-end interpolation. BM had NLL $2.8627$; the five modified tables gave $2.8971$, $2.8674$, $2.8697$, $2.8878$, and $3.0372$. Every modification increased the count while worsening this likelihood endpoint. These NLL measurements reject the claim that increasing the count guarantees lower natural-text loss. They address a different endpoint from the RULER ordering fit, whose independent same-metric validation remains separate.

The archived BM endpoint is also not an exact text-replay target: a later same-name rerun differed on $24/350$ row scores and $210/350$ generated texts despite tiny reconstructed tensor differences. Table~\ref{tab:profile-diagnostic} reports the saved archived outputs; it does not pool them with reruns.
