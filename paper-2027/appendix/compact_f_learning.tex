\subsection{Multi-head Latent Attention}
\label{sec:mla-results}

We report allocation at one fixed rotary budget in a 432M-parameter transformer following
the MLA architectural pattern \citep{deepseekv2}, with 24 layers, width 1024,
16 heads, and per-head width 64.
Its decoupled rotary
subspace has $d_{\mathrm{rope}}{=}32$ ($K{=}16$ frequencies); the remaining
$d_{\mathrm{nope}}{=}32$ dimensions are non-rotary, and the shared KV latent
rank is 256. The model trains from scratch on 500M FineWeb-Edu tokens
\citep{penedo2024fineweb} at
$L_{\mathrm{train}}{=}8192$ and base $500\mathrm{K}$ over seeds 42, 43, and
88. \evq{} uses the pre-specified $\tau=1.414$ setting.
At $16$K, Geo PPL is
$141.1/132.5/142.8$ for seeds $42/43/88$, whereas \evq{} is
$93.7/92.7/100.3$; all three paired directions agree.

\paragraph{Evaluation corpus and aggregation.}
The evaluator reads the shared cached FineWeb-Edu stream \texttt{val\_fineweb-edu\_5000000.pt}, nominally five million GPT-2 tokens. For each method and seed it resets NumPy RandomState to $9999$, iterates through lengths $8192$, $16384$, $20480$, $24576$, $28672$, and $32768$, and draws eight distinct start offsets per length. Every chunk scores all $L-1$ next-token targets, with full-sequence cross-entropy and no tail restriction. Its per-model PPL is the exponential of the mean chunk NLL; the table then takes the arithmetic mean and sample standard deviation of the three per-seed PPLs. Thus the requested targets per model are $8(L-1)$ at each length; starts are sampled without replacement, but overlapping windows are possible. The same offsets are reused across methods.

All methods use the same fixed FineWeb-Edu evaluation cache and paired chunk offsets. The archived result JSON retains every seed and length, allowing the reported means, seed variation and scaling comparisons to be recomputed.

For the supporting overlay, let $\lambda_k=2\pi/\omega_k$ and
$t_k=\operatorname{clip}((\lambda_k-L_{\mathrm{train}})/(31L_{\mathrm{train}}),0,1)$.
The MLA wavelength-blend operator sets
$\omega'_k=\omega_k/[1+t_k(s-1)]$: it preserves wavelengths below the training
length, applies the full factor $s$ above $32L_{\mathrm{train}}$, and interpolates
between them.

\subsection{Shared-start continuation and matched adaptation}
\label{sec:learning-summary}\label{sec:larger-scale-supporting}\label{sec:experiment-details}
The $750$M pair continues a common $2$K geometric checkpoint for $500$M
tokens at $4$K, with $3\%$ passkey and $10\%$ downstream mix (seed $42$).
Cosh uses $\tau=1.5$, $K=32$, inclusive nodes $u_k=k/(K-1)$ and
$\omega_k=\exp[-(K-1)\log(500000)Q_{1.5}(u_k)/K]$, retaining Geo endpoints.
Both arms update all weights. Greedy answer-token exact match generates the
expected passkey tokens and closing marker; it does not require a following EOS.

For MLA and $750$M continuation, AdamW uses $(0.9,0.95)$, $\epsilon=10^{-8}$,
weight decay $0.1$, clipping $1$, no dropout, peak LR $2\times10^{-4}$,
$2\%$ linear warmup and cosine decay to $0.1$ of peak. Batch sizes are $6$
(MLA) and $14$ (continuation: microbatch $7$, accumulation $2$). Text packing
is contiguous without cross-document masking. MLA uses unanchored midpoint
Cosh; the continuation fixes sampled endpoints.

\paragraph{Matched OLMo routing continuation.}\label{sec:olmo2-1b}
OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct uses fixed standard-grid Cosh with $\tau=2$,
$b=500000$ and rank-$64$, alpha-$128$ Q/K/V/O LoRA. Native/Cosh share
checkpoint, training rows, budgets and evaluation inputs. Starting from each
arm's $300$-step routing parent, a matched $100$-step query-gap continuation
uses peak LR $5\times10^{-5}$ and $20$ warmup steps; a $32$-step
answer-plus-EOS stage uses $10^{-5}$ and $4$ warmup steps. Both use seed
$20260728$, AdamW $(0.9,0.95)$, zero weight decay, clipping $1$, BF16,
batch $8$ (micro $4$, accumulation $2$), and cosine LR decay.

Each cycle has two counterfactual-routing updates and one natural-text update:
$67/33$ updates in the query-gap stage and $22/10$ in the final stage.
The $1024$ training pairs and $128$ calibration pairs use seeds
$20293725/20304725$ and different query templates. Examples contain eight
key--value blocks; changing the queried value changes the target answer.
Answer cross-entropy is combined with a counterfactual margin of $1$, weighted
by $0.5$, on positions where correct and swapped answer tokens differ.
The final stage supervises three answer tokens and immediate EOS, with EOS
weight one; prompt and padding tokens carry no answer loss. Natural replay
uses ordinary next-token loss.

\paragraph{Position schedule.}
Physical inputs stay at most $4096$ tokens. Let $q$ be the semantic query-block
start, $a$ the answer start, $h$ the end-exclusive source span and $n$ the active
sequence length. Both counterfactual variants use
$p_i=i+o\,\mathbf1\{i\ge q\}$. Each query-gap update uses four pair offsets:
one alternating between $0$ and $[1,4096]$, one in $[4097,8192]$, and two
in $[8193,12289]$. A seed-derived coprime stride traverses each band, followed
by a deterministic per-update permutation. In the final stage, realized gap
targets instead comprise one alternating between contiguous and $[4096,8191]$,
one in $[8192,12287]$, and two in $[12288,16079]$. For a noncontiguous target
$g$, the offset is
\[
o=\min\{g-(a-h),\ 12289,\ 16384-\min(n,4095)\}.
\]
The resulting gap must remain in its target band and active position IDs below
$16384$. Natural replay stays contiguous. The bundled
\path{figs/verify_routing_schedule.py} reproduces all $268$ query offsets and
$88$ final-stage gap targets against the recorded run receipts.

Evaluation uses real contiguous numeric-NIAH prompts, $100$ per length,
and requires the complete expected answer followed by terminal EOS.
Native/Cosh scores are $95/100$ versus $100/100$ at $4$K,
$18/100$ versus $98/100$ at $8$K, and $0/100$ versus $60/100$ at $16$K.
All outputs terminate with EOS. The Cosh long-gap subsets score $48/50$
at $8$K and $31/66$ at $16$K. This is matched learned adaptation, with
explicit long-phase exposure, distinct from the frozen allocations.
