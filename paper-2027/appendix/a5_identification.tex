\subsection{Exact-range control at \texorpdfstring{$151.9$M}{151.9M}}
\label{sec:exact-range-control}

\paragraph{Paired controls.} The two trained tables have identical frequency
endpoints. Within each seed pair the arms share: model architecture ($151{,}898{,}880$
parameters); trainable initialisation; training length $256$; token order;
optimiser, LR schedule, global batch; within-pair micro batch and accumulation;
training budget
($499{,}974{,}144$ tokens, $7{,}629$ optimiser steps); the $32$ frozen
evaluation anchors; the sampled highest frequency; the sampled lowest
frequency; and the log-frequency span. The single free variable is the location
of the $K{-}2 = 30$ interior frequencies.

The optimiser is fused AdamW with $\beta_1{=}0.9$, $\beta_2{=}0.95$, weight
decay $0.01$, and gradient clipping at $1.0$. Peak/minimum learning rates are
$6\times10^{-4}/6\times10^{-5}$ with $762$ warmup steps and cosine decay.
The global batch is $256$ sequences of length $256$ for every arm. Seed $42$
uses micro batch $64$ with four accumulation steps; seeds $137$ and $256$ use
micro batch $128$ with two accumulation steps. Each paired comparison therefore
has identical execution geometry, optimiser steps, and counted-token budget;
the aggregate uses training seed as its statistical unit under this shared
scientific protocol.

\paragraph{Model and data.} The model has $12$ layers, hidden size $768$,
$12$ heads of dimension $64$, MLP width $3{,}072$, and vocabulary size
$50{,}304$. Training uses the unshuffled FineWeb-Edu
\citep{penedo2024fineweb}
\texttt{sample/10BT/000\_00000.parquet} shard at pinned revision
\texttt{87f09149ef4734204d70ed1d046ddc9ca3f2b8f9}; validation uses the disjoint
\texttt{sample/10BT/004\_00000.parquet} shard. Both are tokenised with the
GPT-NeoX-20B tokenizer \citep{black2022gptneox} at revision
\texttt{c292233c833e336628618a88a648727eb3dff0a7}.

\paragraph{Arms.} \emph{Baseline:} FMRoPE at fixed training support,
implementing the uniform-in-log exponent grid specified in \S6.1 of
\citet{oka2026fmrope} at training base $256$.
\emph{Intervention:} anchored \evq{} at $\tau{=}4$. The anchored and
deployed midpoint \evq{} tables are different support
embeddings of the \emph{same normalised shape}: both use midpoint Cosh
quantiles $q_k$, and the diagnostic removes only their affine endpoint degrees
of freedom. This normalisation is what makes the exact-range control possible.

Writing $\Phi_\tau$ for the inverse CDF in \eqref{eq:warp}, let
$q_k{=}\Phi_\tau((k+\tfrac12)/K)$,
$s_k{=}(q_k-q_0)/(q_{K-1}-q_0)$, and
$R{=}((K-1)/K)\log B$.  The intervention uses
\begin{equation}
\omega_k^{\mathrm{anchor}}=\exp(-R s_k),
\qquad
\omega_k^{\mathrm{FMR}}=\exp\!\left(-R\frac{k}{K-1}\right).
\label{eq:exact-range-grid}
\end{equation}
Hence $k{=}0,K{-}1$ match exactly and only the $K{-}2$ interior entries differ.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Two conditions. \texttt{fixed}: each checkpoint retains
its training range. \texttt{target-matched}: the range is set to the declared
evaluation length while preserving each arm's normalised interior spacing.
Metric is paired final-$128$-token teacher-forced NLL over the $32$ frozen
anchors; reported PPL is $\exp(\mathrm{mean\ NLL})$.
The anchors are common end positions selected with Python's
\texttt{random.Random(20260723)}: the eligible validation stream is divided
into $32$ equal-width bins, with one end position drawn per bin and a
$2{,}048$-token guard before the next bin. At anchor $e$, length $L$ uses
validation tokens $[e-L,e)$ and scores the final $128$ targets $[e-128,e)$.
Longer contexts therefore add earlier text around the same target tokens;
the longest windows at distinct anchors are disjoint.

\paragraph{Exact range transformation.}
\[
R(L)=\frac{K-1}{K}\log L,\qquad
\omega_k^{\rm target}(L)=\exp[-R(L)z_k].
\]
Here $z_k=k/(K-1)$ for FMRoPE and the anchored Cosh shape for its paired
arm. The fixed condition uses $L=256$ at every evaluation length;
the target-matched condition substitutes the declared evaluation length.
Both conditions use amplitude one.

\paragraph{Three-seed contrasts.}
Negative values favour anchored \evq{}. The aggregation unit is the training seed;
evaluation anchors remain paired observations within a seed.

\subsection{Weights-by-table co-adaptation at \texorpdfstring{$151.9$M}{151.9M}}
\label{sec:crossing-151m}

The three-seed experiment above identifies the training-time effect of $z$.
A separate frozen crossing asks whether the learned weights remain compatible
with the coordinate system on which they were trained. Seeds $137$ and $256$
provide paired FMRoPE and anchored-\evq{} checkpoints whose training tables
share exact endpoints. For each frozen checkpoint, we install the factor-four
runtime table derived from either training coordinate, keep the checkpoint,
amplitude, $32$ FineWeb-Edu validation anchors, and final-$128$-token NLL
endpoint fixed, and evaluate at $512$ and $1{,}024$ tokens.

For each training table $\Omega$, the derived table uses the following
label-free residual construction. Set $L_0=256$, $\Delta_j=2j$ for
$j=0,\ldots,127$, and
$p_j=(2L_0-4j-1)/(L_0(L_0+1)/2)$, the grouped causal-separation weights.
Let $X_k$ have columns $\sqrt{p_j}\cos(\omega_k\Delta_j)$ and
$\sqrt{p_j}\sin(\omega_k\Delta_j)$, and let $X_{-k}$ collect all other pairs.
Define
\[
U_k=\frac{\|X_k-X_{-k}X_{-k}^{\dagger}X_k\|_F^2}{\|X_k\|_F^2},
\qquad
\bar U_k=\frac{U_k-\min_i U_i}{\max_i U_i-\min_i U_i},
\qquad w_k=(1-\bar U_k)^2.
\]
The pseudoinverse uses relative singular-value cutoff $10^{-10}$; the
zero-span convention is $\bar U_k=0$. The frequency blend is
$\omega'_k=\omega_k(1-3w_k/4)$, cast to FP32 with its endpoints set exactly
to $\omega'_0=\omega_0$ and $\omega'_{K-1}=\omega_{K-1}/4$.
Both derived tables use $g=1+0.1\log4$ and remain unchanged across the
$512$- and $1{,}024$-token evaluations.

\subsection{First-principles physical mechanisms of the four counterintuitive facts}
\label{sec:four-facts-mechanisms}

During the historical development and empirical auditing of RoPE length-extension schedules, four sharp counterintuitive phenomena were observed across multiple model families. Any physically sound theory of rotary embeddings must explain all four phenomena from first principles.

\paragraph{Fact 1: Tiny-MAE failure at transition knee slot 19.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Empirical phenomenon:} The two-parameter clipped-affine curve $C2$ fits the empirical coordinate movement vector $\mathbf{m}^*$ with mean absolute error $\mathrm{MAE} = 0.001223$ and root mean square error $\mathrm{RMSE} = 0.006118$. Despite this minuscule coordinate-level difference, $C2$ fails the pre-registered Native in-window retention gate on OLMo-1B ($0.870971 < 0.8750$, missing the floor by $0.004615$ nats) and on Qwen-2.5-1.5B ($0.868902 < 0.8750$), even while preserving long-context retrieval (Qwen 64K core-4 score $0.6775$, 128K score $0.5725$).
  \item \emph{Physical mechanism:} Unweighted $L_1/L_2$ coordinate metrics treat all rotary slots symmetrically. However, because $C2$ minimized unweighted coordinate error, $81.2\%$ of its total squared residual was concentrated at \textbf{slot 19} (the knee of the transition curve; the top three transition slots account for $99.3\%$ of residual). Slot 19 sits precisely at the transition boundary between resolvable high frequencies ($\omega L_0 / 2\pi > 1$) with multiple full turns across native context $L_0$ and slow unresolvable frequencies ($\omega L_0 / 2\pi < 1$). Pre-trained projection matrices $W_Q, W_K$ place peak associative responsibility and maximum channel modulus $\rho_{h, 19}$ at this boundary. Furthermore, native retention allows at most $\approx 0.1335$ nats NLL degradation, and attention gain scaling ($g = 1 + 0.074\ln 4$) consumes $86\%$ of this budget, leaving $< 0.02$ nats of operational margin. Concentrating coordinate error on the stiffest Hessian eigenvector at slot 19 ($\Delta m_{19} = 0.044109$, $\delta\omega = -6.94 \times 10^{-4}$ rad/token) accumulates $22.74$ radians across $L_0 = 32{,}768$, exhausting the remaining $< 0.02$ nats margin.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Fact 2: Same-multiset permutation collapse.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Empirical phenomenon:} Retaining the exact multiset of 64 physical frequencies $\{\omega_k\}$ while randomly permuting their assignment to rotary slots causes catastrophic collapse: OLMo-1B $1\times$ PG-19 tail NLL more than doubles from $3.10423$ to $6.86493$ ($\Delta\mathrm{NLL} = +3.760692$) and Qwen-2.5-1.5B 64K core-4 task macro score plunges from $0.7000$ to flat $0.0000$ across all four benchmark tasks.
  \item \emph{Physical mechanism:} Rotary attention logits are mediated by bilinear projections: $S_h(\Delta) = \sum_k q_{h, k}^\top R(\omega_k\Delta) k_{h, k}$. Transformers do not compute an invariant spectral integral; rather, training co-adapts specific row subspaces of $W_Q, W_K$ to specific slot frequencies $\omega_k$. Scrambling the assignment routes slow semantic projections through high-frequency channels (causing rapid trigonometric oscillations that erase long-range associative recall) and routes fast syntax detectors through near-zero channels (destroying local token ordering). This decisively proves that learned projection weights break frequency exchangeability.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Fact 3: The $2\times \to 4\times \to 8\times$ cliff and distractor dilution margin exhaustion.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Empirical phenomenon:} Static frequency tables maintain strong retention at $2\times$ extrapolation ($s=2$ NLL $\Delta = +0.016$, macro retention $1.041$), maintain narrow headroom at $4\times$ ($s=4$ headroom is only $0.000344$ nats, with the theoretical boundary at $s \approx 4.01$), but collapse abruptly at $8\times$ ($s=8$ core-4 drops to $0.3025$, single-key-3 retrieval collapses to $0.0000$, and effective retrieval radius is truncated at $47.8$K).
  \item \emph{Physical mechanism:} In long-context needle retrieval, target needle attention probability is:
  \begin{equation}
  A(j^*) = \frac{1}{1 + \sum_{i \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{dist}}} \exp(s(i) - s(j^*))} \approx \frac{1}{1 + (S L_0)\mu e^{-B}},
  \end{equation}
  where $B = s(j^*) - \mathbb{E}[s(i)]$ is the logit margin, $S$ is context extension factor, and $\mu = \mathbb{E}[\exp(s(i) - \mathbb{E}[s(i)])]$. Top-1 retrieval ($A(j^*) \ge 0.5$) strictly forces:
  \begin{equation}
  B \ge \ln S + \ln(L_0 \mu).
  \end{equation}
  Each context doubling ($4\times \to 8\times$) strictly subtracts $\ln 2 \approx 0.693$ nats from the available margin. Pre-trained models possess an intrinsic, finite logit margin $B_{\max}$. At $2\times$ and $4\times$, $B_{\max} > \ln S + \text{const}$. At $8\times$, the distractor dilution ceiling $S_{\mathrm{dilution}}$ exceeds $B_{\max}$, completely exhausting the decision margin at $s \approx 4.01$ and extinguishing target needle attention.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Fact 4: Support-retargeting reversal under non-separable coordinate dilation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Empirical phenomenon:} Under fixed training support ($a, R$), Anchored \evq{} outperforms FMRoPE by $-0.281, -0.176, -0.146$ NLL at $2\times, 4\times, 8\times$ across all three seeds. However, retargeting support to match the evaluation length completely reverses the ranking across all three seeds ($+0.060, +0.227, +0.460$ NLL).
  \item \emph{Physical mechanism:} Log-frequency coordinates decompose into $x_k = a + R z_k$. The support span $R$ and interior allocation $\mathbf{z}$ are coupled, interacting coordinates. EVQ-Cosh concentrates interior frequencies non-linearly to minimize a specific density surrogate under the training support $R_{\mathrm{train}}$. When $R$ is dilated to $R(L) = ((K-1)/K)\log L$, the non-linear Cosh profile undergoes non-uniform phase stretching, distorting the harmonic ratios to which projection weights $W_Q, W_K$ co-adapted during training. In contrast, uniform geometric spacing ($z_k = k/(K-1)$) expands scale-invariantly under logarithmic dilation, preserving harmonic ratios. Thus, interior allocation shape and support dilation cannot be optimized independently.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Binary elimination of candidate pre-hoc structural conditions}
\label{sec:binary-elimination}

To determine whether downstream length-extension capability can be predicted before running expensive task benchmarks, eight candidate pre-hoc structural conditions ($C_1$ through $C_8$) were proposed. We evaluate each candidate against the four counterintuitive facts under a strict \textbf{binary veto rule}: any candidate condition that fails to correctly explain or predict any single fact pre-hoc is permanently eliminated.

\paragraph{Candidate definitions and refutations.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Condition $C_1$ (Coordinate-Space Movement Distance $\|\mathbf{m} - \mathbf{m}^*\|_p$):}
  Asserts that downstream performance is preserved if the coordinate-space movement vector $\mathbf{m}$ is close to an empirical reference $\mathbf{m}^*$.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 1:} $C2$ achieved $\mathrm{MAE} = 0.001223$, yet failed Native retention because unweighted $L_p$ distance is blind to the fact that $81.2\%$ of the error concentrated on the stiffest Hessian eigenvector at slot 19. Also vetoed by Facts 2, 3, 4.
  
  \item \textbf{Condition $C_2$ (Unordered Spectral Multiset $\mu_\Omega$):}
  Asserts that downstream capability depends on the multiset of physical frequencies $\{\omega_k\}$.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 2:} Permuting slot assignments preserves the multiset identically ($\Phi(\Omega) = \Phi(\pi(\Omega))$), yet causes OLMo tail NLL to double ($\Delta = +3.76$) and Qwen 64K core-4 score to plunge from $0.7000$ to $0.0000$. Also vetoed by Facts 3, 4.

  \item \textbf{Condition $C_3$ (Discrete Scale-Orbit Symmetry Metric $q(\Omega, s)$):}
  Asserts that rational scale-orbit commensurability classes govern extrapolation.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 3:} Perturbing exact chain $\Omega^{\mathrm{exact}}$ by a $1$-ULP float32 jitter shatters orbit classes, jumping $q$ from $6$ to $64$ ($\Delta q = 58$), yet produces zero measurable behavioral difference ($\Delta\mathrm{NLL} = +0.000013, p > 0.95$). Also vetoed by Facts 1, 2.

  \item \textbf{Condition $C_4$ (Uncentered Background Derivative Energy Minimization $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bg}}$):}
  Asserts that minimizing $\int |F'(u)|^2 du$ preserves long-context retrieval.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 3:} The carrier intervention reduced background energy by $73.85\%$, but collapsed 128K retrieval to $0.00$ and caused severe EOS runaway, because scalar carrier shifts introduce first-order phase errors $\Delta\theta = c\Delta/s$ that rotate target signals out of alignment. Also vetoed by Facts 1, 2, 4.

  \item \textbf{Condition $C_5$ (Monotonic Group Delay and Continuous Spacing):}
  Asserts that smooth, monotonic group delay $\tau_g > 0$ guarantees stable extrapolation.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 1:} $C2$ has strictly monotonic group delay yet fails retention due to slot-19 error concentration.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 4:} Both EVQ-Cosh and FMRoPE possess strictly monotonic group delay, which cannot predict why rankings completely reverse under support dilation.

  \item \textbf{Condition $C_6$ (Isolated Base Scaling / $S$-bound):}
  Asserts that length extension is governed purely by base dilation, treating interior allocation as an invariant geometric ladder.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 4:} Anchored EVQ beats FMRoPE across $3/3$ seeds under identical base and endpoints, proving that interior allocation $z$ is an independent causal variable; base scaling cannot account for the retargeting reversal. Also vetoed by Facts 1, 2, 3.

  \item \textbf{Condition $C_7$ (Effective Resolution Cutoff Boundaries):}
  Asserts that obeying fast plateau ($m=0$ for $\text{turns} > 32$) and slow plateau ($m=1$ for $\text{turns} < 1$) boundaries suffices for retention.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 1:} $C2$ strictly satisfies these cutoff boundaries but fails retention because the transition trajectory concentrated error at slot 19. Also vetoed by Fact 2.

  \item \textbf{Condition $C_8$ (Ordered Operator Norm Closeness $\|\mathcal{P}_\Omega - \mathcal{P}_{\Omega^*}\|_2$):}
  Asserts that bounding $\max_k |\omega_k - \omega_k^*|$ bounds loss degradation.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 1:} In $C2$, maximum frequency error at slot 19 is $\le 3.2 \times 10^{-4}$ in operator norm, yet model sensitivity at slot 19 is orders of magnitude larger than at low-frequency slots due to learned content coupling weights.
  \emph{Vetoed by Fact 3:} Operator norm difference is constant across lengths, yet performance collapses non-linearly between $2\times, 4\times, 8\times$ due to softmax distractor dilution.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{MrRoPE-Pro: Strict characterization as a satisficing design}
\label{sec:mrrope-pro-satisficing}

\paragraph{Mathematical parameterization.}
\citet{tian2026mrrope} parameterized length-extension frequency schedules by defining discrete turn boundaries.
For intermediate band $k \in [l, h]$ of width $N = h - l$, with normalized coordinate $q = \operatorname{clip}(k - l, 0, N)$, MrRoPE-Pro defines cumulative exponent shifts via:
\begin{equation}
m_q^{\mathrm{Pro}} = \frac{q(q + 1)}{N(N + 1)}, \qquad \omega_k^{\mathrm{Pro}} = \omega_k^N s^{-m_q^{\mathrm{Pro}}},
\label{eq:mrrope-pro-formula}
\end{equation}
paired with attention temperature gain scaling $g = 1 + 0.1\ln s$.
On the fast band ($k < l$, $\text{turns} > 32$), $m = 0 \implies \delta\omega = 0$.
On the slow band ($k > h$, $\text{turns} < 1$), $m = 1 \implies \omega' = \omega / s$.

\paragraph{Why MrRoPE-Pro is a robust satisficing design.}
MrRoPE-Pro succeeds across diverse models (Qwen-2.5, OLMo-2) not because it is an analytic global optimum, but because it is an expertly engineered \textbf{satisficing design} that strictly respects universal physical boundary constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Zero fast compression:} By enforcing $\delta\omega_k = 0$ on all slots with $\text{turns} > 32$, it unconditionally avoids perturbing the highest-norm subspaces of $W_Q, W_K$ that govern short-range syntax and token recognition.
  \item \emph{Uniform slow scaling:} By scaling slow slots by $1/s$, the maximum accumulated phase across the extended context $s L_0$ is strictly bounded: $\phi_k^{\mathrm{ext}} = (\omega_k / s)(s L_0) = \omega_k L_0 < 2\pi$. On Qwen-2.5-1.5B at $128$K ($s=4$), the maximum phase across all slow slots is $5.8271$ rad ($< 2\pi = 6.2832$), completely preventing out-of-distribution phase wrapping.
  \item \emph{Convex quadratic delay:} The progression $q(q+1)/[N(N+1)]$ ensures that the earliest intermediate slots experience minimal phase disturbance, keeping cumulative error away from the sensitive knee slots.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Definite empirical residuals: FullLagP2 and Boundary-Matched (BM).}
Because MrRoPE-Pro reads zero checkpoint weight matrices and evaluates zero activation covariances, it is not an unconstrained global optimum.
This is definitively confirmed by two empirical residuals in the repository:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{FullLagP2 on Qwen-2.5-1.5B at 64K Context:}
  FullLagP2 adapted frequency movements to empirical subspace uniqueness $U_k$ across all $32{,}768$ integer lags. In paired head-to-head testing on Qwen-2.5-1.5B 64K, FullLagP2 decisively outperformed MrRoPE-Pro on Multi-Key 2 retrieval ($37.50\%$ vs $12.50\%$, a $+25.0\%$ win), Variable Tracking ($87.50\%$ vs $82.50\%$, $+5.0\%$), and Frequent Words Extraction ($70.83\%$ vs $45.83\%$, $+25.0\%$), achieving an overall paired record of 9 wins, 13 ties, and only 2 losses.
  \item \textbf{Boundary-Matched (BM) on OLMo-1B at 16K Context:}
  On OLMo-1B at 16K context, MrRoPE-Pro drops to $2.78\%$ on the 6-task confirmation panel and $21.62\%$ on 631 natural QA prompts, whereas Boundary-Matched (BM) achieves $51.32\%$ and $25.44\%$, respectively.
\end{enumerate}
These results establish that MrRoPE-Pro is a robust, physically constrained satisficing heuristic rather than an unconstrained global optimum.
