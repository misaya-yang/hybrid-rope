\section{TailSpline: Construction and Frozen Evaluation}
\label{sec:tailspline-details}
\subsection{Finite-grid proof}
Let $D\epsilon=(\epsilon_{q+1}-\epsilon_q)_{q=1}^{n-1}$ and $H=D^\top D+e_ne_n^\top$. A zero value of $\epsilon^\top H\epsilon$ requires constant increments and a zero terminal increment, hence $\epsilon=0$. Thus $H$ is positive definite. For $n>1$, the proposed $\epsilon_q=c(n+q)(n-q+1)$, with $c=3/[n(n+1)(2n+1)]$, satisfies
\[
\epsilon_1-\epsilon_2=2c,\quad
2\epsilon_q-\epsilon_{q-1}-\epsilon_{q+1}=2c,\quad
2\epsilon_n-\epsilon_{n-1}=2c.
\]
Therefore $H\epsilon=2c\mathbf1$, the equality-constrained stationarity equation. The increments are positive and sum to one; summing them yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline}. The objective minimum is $2c=6/[n(n+1)(2n+1)]$. For $n=1$, the constraint gives $\epsilon_1=1$, agreeing with the formulas. Positivity makes additional nonnegativity constraints inactive.

Adding $\epsilon_1^2$ yields the symmetric objective, with unique solution
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_q^{\rm BM}=\frac{6q(n-q+1)}{n(n+1)(n+2)},\qquad
m_q^{\rm BM}=\frac{q(q+1)(3n+2-2q)}{n(n+1)(n+2)}.
\label{eq:bm-formula}
\end{equation}
Both profiles are monotone with $m_0=0,m_n=1$. The portable CPU check independently solves the constrained tridiagonal system and verifies each closed form.

\paragraph{What the objective smooths.}
On the geometric native grid, Eq.~\eqref{eq:increment-log-gap} makes each change between neighboring log gaps equal to $(\epsilon_{q+1}-\epsilon_q)\log s$. The terminal change is $-\epsilon_n\log s$ because the fully interpolated tail has constant displacement. Thus $(\log s)^2$ times Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline-objective} is exactly the squared log-gap variation within the transition and at its low-frequency junction. The high-frequency junction is deliberately unpenalized.

At the terminal gap,
\[
\epsilon_n^{\rm Pro}=\frac2{n+1},\quad
\epsilon_n^{\rm TS}=\frac6{(n+1)(2n+1)},\quad
\frac{\epsilon_n^{\rm TS}}{\epsilon_n^{\rm Pro}}=\frac3{2n+1}.
\]
The opposite entry behavior is also explicit: $\epsilon_1^{\rm Pro}=2/[n(n+1)]$, whereas $\epsilon_1^{\rm TS}=3/(2n+1)$. TailSpline trades a larger entry jump for a smaller terminal jump. A useful boundary prior need not minimize both. In the continuum limit, $m^{\rm TS}(u)=(3u-u^3)/2$ solves $\min\int_0^1(m''(u))^2du$ under $m(0)=0,m(1)=1,m'(1)=0$; the natural entry condition is $m''(0)=0$. The finite-grid formula is used in every model experiment, not this limiting polynomial.

\subsection{An exact equal-displacement control}
\label{sec:tailspline-dose-control}
Use local indices $q=0,\ldots,n$. Write $T_q=m_q^{\rm TS}$, $B_q=m_q^{\rm BM}$, $U_q=q/n$, and $P_q=q(q+1)/[n(n+1)]$. Define the front-loaded profile $F_q=2U_q-P_q$ and the control
\begin{equation}
C_q=(1-w_n)U_q+w_nF_q,\qquad w_n=\frac{3n}{2(2n+1)}.
\label{eq:tailspline-dose-control}
\end{equation}
Substitution of the finite-grid formulas gives $T=(1-w_n)B+w_nF$. Subtracting $C$ gives
\begin{equation}
m^{\rm TS}-m^C=(1-w_n)(m^{\rm BM}-m^{\rm Uni}).
\label{eq:tailspline-shape-contrast}
\end{equation}
In local coordinates, the explicit difference is
\begin{equation}
T_q-C_q=(1-w_n)(B_q-U_q)=\frac{q(n-q)(2q-n)}{2n(n+1)(2n+1)}.
\label{eq:tailspline-antisymmetric-contrast}
\end{equation}
This cumulative-profile difference is antisymmetric under $q\mapsto n-q$, vanishes at both endpoints, and sums to zero. Since $U$ and $F$ have positive increments and $0<w_n<1$, $C$ is monotone with $C_0=0,C_n=1$. Extending both $T$ and $C$ by the same constant outer bands gives identical endpoints and total log displacement at a common scale. Their equal increment centroids follow from Eq.~\eqref{eq:displacement-centroid}, rather than imposing a further constraint. For $n=1,2$, $B=U$ and $T=C$; for wider bands the contrast tests higher-order shape.

The reported TailSpline--MrPro results below change total displacement and shape together. The completed $T$--$C$ diagnostic in Appendix~\ref{sec:current-controls} tests the zero-total correction above under the recorded cross-batch runtimes. Its primary interval spans both signs. The algebra specifies the contrast, while the runtime-qualified result supplies its observed task response. The independent CPU check in \path{figs/allocation_design.py} verifies the finite-sum and profile identities with exact rational arithmetic.

\subsection{Evaluation implementation}
\label{sec:tailspline-implementation}
Generation is greedy, with repetition penalty $1$, no repeat-ngram restriction and the model's EOS IDs. Llama uses batch size one with cached $8192$-token prefill chunks; OLMo uses batches of at most four identical-length inputs. The static FP32 frequency table is installed after model loading and its installed values are checked against the receipt. NIAH point-depth tasks use two prompts at each target depth $10/30/50/70/90\%$ per length. Other tasks keep their recorded task-specific output budgets.

\paragraph{Input construction.}
The generator pins NVIDIA RULER revision \texttt{c3f5e3b}. Each NIAH task--length cell selects $10$ rows from $64$ candidates using only evidence positions and row identifiers. For single-evidence tasks, the five target depths above each receive two prompts; selection minimizes absolute depth error with tolerance $0.10$. Multi-evidence tasks alternate dispersed targets $(0.10,0.35,0.65,0.90)$ and middle-cluster targets $(0.35,0.45,0.55,0.65)$, with mean absolute tolerance $0.18$. Depths use token positions divided by the context cap, after padding. Task scores are not used in selection.

The filler unit is \texttt{Irrelevant archive padding record; it does not answer the question.} followed by a newline, tokenized without special tokens. Its token sequence is repeated and truncated to the required padding count, then inserted immediately after the user header, before the original task template, until the input occupies cap minus $128$ tokens. The original context, question and references are preserved. In the pinned aggregation templates, the numbered list or coded text follows the task instruction; filler is placed before that instruction. The task-specific generation budgets remain $128$ (NIAH), $30$ (tracking), $120$ (common words), $50$ (frequent words) and $32$ (QA). The row records store padding-token counts and achieved depth errors.

The Llama and OLMo preparation seeds are $20260924$ and $20260925$; each cell adds $300000+10000i+100j$ for length index $i$ and task index $j$. QA uses question-index blocks starting at $5000$, $5010$ and $5020$ for the three lengths. These rules specify sample selection within each model; the two model panels have separate tokenizations. Both exact tables were fixed before their corresponding comparison; historical Llama and OLMo experiments informed the design direction.

\paragraph{Natural-text sources.}
Proof-Pile uses the original \texttt{hoskinson-center/proof-pile} test archive, selecting the first $32$ arXiv records with at least $300000$ characters and retaining up to the first million characters. PG-19 uses individual test-book prefixes. The frozen $46$-document source manifest records the selected $32$ Proof-Pile and $14$ PG-19 identities; each tokenizer takes a prefix of maximum evaluation length plus one token. Documents are scored separately rather than concatenated.

Full-13 intervals use $20{,}000$ paired resamples within each task--length cell, retaining fixed task weights; NIAH intervals aggregate the corresponding retrieval tasks. The report treats different length cells separately for this panel. PPL intervals instead resample the $46$ document identities jointly across all lengths, also using $20{,}000$ draws. The two uncertainty calculations therefore preserve different experimental units. These are conditional evaluation intervals, not uncertainty across training seeds or model families.

\subsection{The fixed T/C exchange and task response}
\label{sec:tc-task-connection}
The profile difference in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tailspline-antisymmetric-contrast} satisfies
$\delta_{n-q}=-\delta_q$, $\sum_q\delta_q=0$ and $\delta_q<0$ for
$0<q<n/2$. For a continuously differentiable utility $\mathcal V$ on a
neighborhood of the C-to-T path, define
\begin{equation}
G_q=\int_0^1\partial_{m_q}\mathcal V(C+t\delta)\,dt,
\qquad
\mathcal V(T)-\mathcal V(C)=\sum_{q<n/2}(-\delta_q)(G_{n-q}-G_q).
\label{eq:tc-path-utility}
\end{equation}
The fundamental theorem of calculus gives the path integral; pairing indices
gives the second expression. The hypothesis is that later-half slowdown has
higher marginal task value for this fixed exchange. A sequence log-likelihood
can be differentiable; greedy accuracy and F1 do not satisfy this premise.
The $G_q$ identity is explanatory, not an independent predictor or an
optimization procedure. This exposition was clarified after the E1 outputs
were available; it does not retroactively preregister a new prediction.
No equivalence margin was preregistered for the current comparison.

\paragraph{From logits to value readout.}
For fixed visible keys and current-layer content Q/K/V, let $\eta_j$ be the
finite score change caused by switching tables. Direct softmax algebra yields
\begin{equation}
p'_j=\frac{p_j e^{\eta_j}}{\mathbb E_p e^\eta},\qquad
\log\frac{p'_j/p'_r}{p_j/p_r}=\eta_j-\eta_r,\qquad
o'-o=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}_p(v,e^\eta)}{\mathbb E_p e^\eta}.
\label{eq:finite-readout}
\end{equation}
A common logit shift has no effect. Reweighting keys only helps an output when
it changes the useful value mixture. A scalar gain absorbs a score change
exactly only when the target scores equal a common positive multiple of the
original scores plus a row constant; a shared gain requires the same multiple
across queries. These are standard identities, related to LeRoPE's
frequency--attention--value gradient chain \citep{karypis2026lerope}.
They do not hold the entire Transformer trajectory fixed after a table swap.
A mechanism claim requires a separate output intervention with runtime-matched
no-op and shuffled controls; no such completed result is claimed here.
