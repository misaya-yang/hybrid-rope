# Reviewer report — Seat R3: Perspective Reviewer

## 1. Paper summary

The paper decomposes a finite RoPE table into support location $a$, log-span $R$, and interior allocation $z$ via $x_k=-\log\omega_k=a+Rz_k$ (Eq. 1.1), and argues that prior work has treated the geometric grid $z_k=k/(K-1)$ as part of the definition of RoPE rather than as a design choice. The theoretical core is a second-moment account of the $K$ two-dimensional sin/cos pair subspaces $V_\omega=\operatorname{span}\{\cos\omega\Delta,\sin\omega\Delta\}$ under a separation prior: the block-whitened correlation Gram $\Gamma$ satisfies $r_2(\Gamma)=2K/(1+(K-1)\bar c)$ exactly (Thm. 1), where $\bar c$ is the mean squared canonical correlation (equivalently, mean squared cosine of principal angles) between pairs. A fourth-order expansion (Prop. 1) shows all deep-slow pairs ($\omega L\ll 1$) collapse onto a common two-dimensional limit — hence the headline "46 nominal dimensions, Rényi-2 rank 2.00" for the 23 pairs with $\omega L\le 1$ in standard OLMo-scale RoPE. Two exact obstructions discipline the story: post-hoc frozen swaps cannot be compensated by any fixed invertible linear Q/K map (Thm. 3), and trained weights are co-adapted to their training table (Table 3.1, which I read as an unusually honest self-falsification of "geometry explains behavior"). On the constructive side, a declared convex surrogate $\mathcal C_{\rm app}$ — an $L^2$ load term plus a Brownian (min-kernel) collision penalty on log-frequency — has the unique minimizer $\rho_\tau\propto\cosh(\tau(1-\phi))$, whose inverse-CDF quantiles give the closed-form EVQ-Cosh table. Behaviorally, the paper's load-bearing results are a pinned-support, pinned-endpoints, three-seed 151.9M training intervention where only the 30 interior frequencies move (3/3 seeds favor EVQ at every OOD length), zero-training frozen-checkpoint RULER recovery (0.56% → 60.47% on OLMo at matched support), matched LoRA and continued-pretraining studies through 1.485B/8B, an MLA study at $K{=}16$, and a single-seed video-DiT check.

From my harmonic-analysis seat, the mathematics is correct, elementary-but-right, and closer to classical frame/sampling/quantization theory than the paper admits. From my industrial-seat, the practical proposal (fix $z$ at pretraining; route at deployment) is plausible and cheap, but the paper's own transplant and co-adaptation results mean it is primarily a *training-time* contribution wearing a deployment demonstration.

## 2. Strengths

**S1 — The exact machinery is correct and the right objects are measured.** The cross-Gram (Eq. A.1.1), whitening invariance proof (App. A.1 "Basis invariance," a1_proofs.tex:27-36), budget identity (Thm. 1), and fourth-order collapse coefficient $19/12600$ (Prop. 1) are verifiable by hand; I re-derived the headline number: at $u_k=k/64$, $b=5\cdot10^5$, $L=4096$, the condition $\omega L\le1$ selects exactly 23 pairs, adjacent-pair collision deficits are $\sim 2.6\times10^{-5}$, giving $\bar c\approx0.9997$ and $r_2=46/(1+22\bar c)=2.001$. The 99.99%-trace-in-2-eigendirections claim and the Rényi-2 number are mutually consistent. Phase invariance (the insistence on full 2D subspaces rather than a cosine slice, 03_theory.tex:56-78) is a genuine methodological improvement over slice analyses in the recent literature.

**S2 — The fixed-support three-seed control is the cleanest causal isolation of interior allocation I have seen.** Every scalar a base/range method can express is pinned (App. A.5, a5_identification.tex:7-16), and the target-matched reversal is reported honestly rather than hidden (a5_identification.tex:106-117: "conditional on the selected range; they do not identify a support-by-allocation interaction law"). This is exactly how a mechanistic intervention should be written.

**S3 — Table 3.1 (co-adaptation) is a model of scientific honesty.** The authors built a redundancy metric, and then present the cell where their own metric *improves* (4.57→12.54) while perplexity collapses 10× as the headline discipline on scope (03_theory.tex:147-161). The worst cell being the largest $r_2$ gain is stated in the caption. Many papers would have buried this.

**S4 — The transplant obstruction (Thm. 3) is exact, short, and correctly scoped,** including the integer-position $2\pi$-alias refinement (a1_proofs.tex:150-157) and the explicit carve-out that approximate retraining, nonlinear maps, and V/O paths are separate intervention classes (a1_proofs.tex:159-161).

**S5 — The surrogate is declared, its validation is functional, and the uniqueness claim is properly conditional.** $\mathcal C_{\rm app}$ is stated up front with its two terms' roles (03_theory.tex:186-198); validation is against the closed-form cosine kernel $K_{\cos}$ in all 12 configurations (App. A.1 surrogate-validation, a1_proofs.tex:218-261); the self-consistency identity $\tau^2T_2+T_1=\tau\coth\tau$ (Thm. A.13) is a neat exact cross-check; and EVQ is consistently called a "bounded constructive witness," not a solution of the true objective.

**S6 — Lemma A.11 (full in-window rank coexisting with exact out-of-window aliasing)** cleanly separates "how much dimension" from "how far it extends," pre-empting the obvious objection that maximizing $r_2$ solves extrapolation. This is the kind of boundary claim that keeps a metrics paper from overreaching.

**S7 — Reproducibility discipline:** pinned corpus/tokenizer revisions, per-protocol realized $\tau$, three grid conventions explicitly labeled (03_theory.tex:20-25; Reproducibility statement), pre-specified factorial with completed 180/180 + 12/12 runs, and an evidence-map table separating intervention, unit, and endpoint (Table A.2.1).

## 3. Weaknesses

**W1 — MAJOR. The theory is diagnostic only; no quantitative bridge from geometry to behavior is ever established, and the crossover phenomenon the paper observes repeatedly is exactly where such a bridge is possible.** The paper correctly says interventions carry behavioral claims (03_theory.tex:105-108, 05_discussion.tex:12-18), but then the theory predicts nothing behavioral at all — it is a measurement, and Table 3.1 shows it can point the wrong way for frozen weights. Meanwhile the most robust *behavioral* regularity in the paper — the in-window/OOD crossover in every autoregressive protocol (05_discussion.tex:20-30) — has a candidate geometric predictor sitting right there in the appendix: at $\tau{=}4$ the median wavelength moves $1/\omega: 707\to 9.69$ and the $u{=}0.9$ point $134{,}609\to1{,}720$ (a2_experiment_details.tex:140-151), and the crossover length should plausibly track where the phase-coverage or collision profile $F_\tau$/$q(Lb^{-\phi})$ crosses a threshold. *Fix:* across the 432M/750M/1.485B crossovers, test whether crossover length is predicted by a stated geometric functional (e.g., the $L$ where full-table $r_2$ under $\mu_L$ reaches some fraction of $2K$). One successful falsifiable prediction converts the theory section from taxonomic decoration into load-bearing content. If it fails, saying so is itself valuable and consistent with the paper's own standards.

**W2 — MAJOR. All static numbers live under $\Delta\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,L]$; sensitivity to the separation prior is acknowledged (a1_proofs.tex:69-73) but never quantified, and the block-whitening deliberately removes the content-energy weighting that the same reference list says matters.** Two distinct issues hide under this flag. (i) The collapse limit is prior-robust — for any measure with support on ≥3 separations, $V_\omega\to\operatorname{span}\{1,\Delta\}$ — and the softmax-metric variant (Prop. 1, second sentence) is a genuinely good answer to "which prior?" for the slow cluster. But the *full-table* $r_2$, the surrogate-validation numbers, and the "budget spent" accounting are all prior-specific, and attention priors are empirically far from uniform (long-range decay, retrieval spikes). (ii) Block whitening removes per-channel energy (03_theory.tex:108-109), yet barbero2025round and wu2026datashapes (cited, 02_related.tex:20-35) say slow channels carry semantic/content function — so "more than one third of rotary pairs spends 46 nominal dimensions on ~2 positional directions" (01_intro.tex:23-26) overstates the *opportunity cost* if those dimensions are being rented to content. *Fix:* a short table reporting slow-cluster $r_2$ and full-table $r_2$ under three priors (uniform, power-law $p\propto\Delta^{-\alpha}$, and a checkpoint-derived attention-distance histogram), plus one energy-weighted collision variant; state which numbers move and by how much. Given the closed forms (Eq. A.1.1), this is a few minutes of existing code.

**W3 — MAJOR. The novelty positioning against YaRN's ramp and LongRoPE's channel-wise schedules is question-begging.** Related work claims prior methods "change support or realised phases" and not $z$ (02_related.tex:10-30). But under the paper's own definition — with endpoints and log-span pinned, $z$ is everything interior — YaRN's non-interpolated/interpolated bands and LongRoPE's searched piecewise scaling schedules *are* interior allocations; they simply confound the change with endpoint moves. The paper's taxonomy draws the $a$/$R$ vs $z$ boundary exactly at the endpoints, and never defends why "moved the endpoints too" removes YaRN/LongRoPE from the allocation axis. The honest claim is: *z was never isolated or measured, and no prior construction derives it in closed form* — which is plenty. Also, the frozen-table control (same-support log-linear, 0.56) is arguably a weak anchor for the 60.47 headline: official YaRN (7.94) is the meaningful deployment reference, and the abstract's choice of the 0.56 baseline (00_abstract.tex:8-9; Table 4.1) will read as anchor-shopping even though derived−YaRN = +52.5 points is still large. *Fix:* one sentence in §2 stating that YaRN/LongRoPE ramps are (piecewise-uniform or searched) interior allocations *combined with* support transport, i.e., move them within the taxonomy honestly; label the abstract's delta against both anchors.

**W4 — MAJOR. The gap between $\mathcal C_{\rm app}$ and the true oscillatory objective is never bounded, and validation is on the cosine slice only.** The true pairwise collision at separation prior $[0,L]$ is built from $a(t)=\sin t/t$ and $b_\star(t)$ at $t=(\omega{\mp}\nu)L$ — so it is *band-limited localization*: pairs with $|\omega-\nu|L\gtrsim\pi$ barely collide regardless of how slow both are, and conversely resonance structure at $(\omega-\nu)L\approx 2\pi n$ (the object of wang2024resonance, cited at 02_related.tex:53-54) is invisible to $\min(\phi,\psi)$, which penalizes any two slow channels monotonically. The surrogate therefore over-penalizes fast-vs-fast pairs that are actually resolvable and cannot see aliasing resonances at all; both biases push density toward the fast end more than the true optimum would, and the bias is largest exactly in the small-$L$/all-slow regime (the video configuration, $L=32$, where the entire table sits in the collapse band). The 12-config validation (Table A.1.4) is the right instinct but tests Geo vs EVQ under $K_{\cos}$, not whether the Cosh *family and its $\tau$* track the minimizer of the phase-invariant full-pair objective. *Fix:* (a) numerically evaluate the true $\bar c(z;\tau)$ (closed forms already exist, a1_proofs.tex:12-18) for the Cosh family over a $\tau$-sweep in the 12 configurations and report argmin displacement from the surrogate's operating rule — a figure, not a theorem; (b) if cheap, an $O(1/(\omega_{\min}L))$-type comparison of the two kernels' off-diagonal decay for smooth $\rho$ via integration by parts. Without either, "unique for that surrogate" is rigorously true but the surrogate's representativeness rests on 12 Geo-vs-EVQ comparisons.

**W5 — MINOR. The paper does not cash its own classical checks.** The block-whitened correlation Gram *is* a (projected) frame-correlation / Gram matrix; $\operatorname{tr}\Gamma^2$ *is* the frame potential; the budget identity is a Welch-bound rearrangement; $c_{\omega\nu}$ is the mean squared cosine of principal angles; the resolution floor "two frequencies are indistinguishable when $|\omega-\nu|L\lesssim\pi$" is exactly Landau-type necessary density for the log-frequency line, and it *predicts the direction of the Cosh answer* (admissible sample density is proportional to $\omega$, so mass must move fast-ward — the maximum-entropy-density view of the same fact). The high-resolution distortion bound (eq. A.1.26, $1/(12K^2)\int w/\rho^2$) is textbook quantization theory (Gersho/Gray–Neuhoff) used uncredited. A trained reader will notice the apparatus is borrowed and unacknowledged; the claims become *stronger*, not weaker, when anchored ("our identity is the Welch bound conditioned on the fixed trace"). *Fix:* a paragraph in §3.2 or Related naming the frame/Welch/principal-angles/Landau/quantization lineage with citations.

**W6 — MINOR. The 50.9M factorial shows the *shape family* works, not that Cosh is the shape; the paper's "witness" hedge is correct but the abstract still sells EVQ as the construction.** Matched-exponential minus Geo ($-0.0106$, $p{=}0.071$) is statistically indistinguishable from rule-minus-exponential ($+0.0007$, CI $[-0.006,0.008]$), and the *rule* arm itself has CI crossing zero ($[-0.021,0.002]$, $p{=}0.125$; table_m4.tex) — the significant arm is $1.25\times$ the rule. Combined with the staged 99-run result that $c{=}1$ beats Geo in $7/9$ configuration means (a1_proofs.tex:385-398), the behavioral evidence identifies "a fast-ward deformation of $z$" and leaves the analytic Cosh form doing only mathematical work. *Fix:* say so once, in the intro's contribution (iii), with the same crispness the discussion already has; state the factorial's power limitation where it is cited (02_identification.tex:26-31).

**W7 — MINOR. The "modality breadth" claim is carried by the weakest experiment.** Single seed (42), synthetic Oscillating Moving MNIST, 129.6M DiT, and a $\tau$ chosen as best-of-sweep at $0.53\times$ the paper's own text scaling rule (a2_experiment_details.tex:92-98) — i.e., the $d_{\rm head}/\sqrt{L}$ rule is *broken* in this modality and patched by tuning. This supports "non-uniform spacing sometimes helps outside text," not breadth of the *mechanism*; the low-phase-channel diagnostic (a2_experiment_details.tex:133-137) is good but analytic, not behavioral. The MLA result (3 seeds, $K{=}16$, all seeds favor EVQ at 16K, Table A.3.1) is the real architecture-breadth claim and needs no help from the DiT. *Fix:* demote DiT to "scope check," or add two seeds; either path is cheap.

**W8 — MINOR. Statistical units and anchors need one more pass of visibility.** The primary identification rests on $n=3$ seeds ("every tested OOD length in every seed" is a sign test with $p\ge 1/64$ per length if sign-exchangeable — never stated); mature LoRA results are single trained pairs with row-level CIs that condition on the pair (a6_mature_scale.tex:52-54 — correctly stated, but the §4.2 prose lets "every seed" and "all 24 packs" do more work than 1-2 pairs can); Qwen derived-minus-log-linear CI is $[0.25,17.50]$, which excludes zero only barely, while derived−YaRN on Qwen is not interval-reported (04_experiments.tex:26-27 vs Table 4.1). *Fix:* state the exact sign-test p-values for the 3-seed contrasts; add the Qwen intervals.

## 4. Questions for authors

**Q1.** For the Cosh family $\rho_\tau$, what is the minimizer of the *true* phase-invariant full-pair objective $\bar c(z;\mu_L)$ (closed form available from Eq. A.1.1), and how far is it, in $\tau$, from the surrogate-selected operating point in each validated configuration? Does the surrogate ever mis-order Geo vs a moderate $\tau$?

**Q2.** How sensitive are (i) the full-table $r_2$ values and (ii) the surrogate-validation rankings to replacing $\mathrm{Unif}[0,L]$ with a power-law attention-distance prior or a checkpoint-derived empirical prior? Is there any prior in the natural class under which Geo's slow-cluster dimension exceeds, say, 4?

**Q3.** Can the crossover length (baseline-ahead-in-window / EVQ-ahead-beyond) observed at 432M, 750M, and 1.485B be *predicted* from a static geometric functional of the two tables, up to one shared constant? If yes, report it; if it has been tried and failed, report that.

**Q4.** Under your endpoint-pinned definition, YaRN's ramp is a piecewise-uniform interior allocation plus support transport. Would you accept the narrower novelty claim — "$z$ was never *isolated*, never *measured*, and never *derived in closed form*" — and if not, what in §2's taxonomy excludes YaRN's ramp from the $z$-axis besides intent?

**Q5.** The movement-profile ramp reproduces the derived table's OLMo recovery (61.04 vs 60.47; 04_experiments.tex:28-30). If a label-free fixed ramp matches the residual-energy-derived table, what does the checkpoint-specific derivation add operationally, and does that change your recommendation to a pretraining team choosing between "one published ramp" and "per-checkpoint derivation"?

**Q6.** What are the static full-pair $r_2$ values, under $\mathrm{Unif}[0,\ell]$ at $\ell\in\{256,512,1024,2048\}$, of the FMRoPE and anchored-EVQ tables of the §3.1 intervention? Without them, the paper's cleanest causal experiment is the only headline experiment with no reported connection to the theory's measurement object.

**Q7.** Given the transplant obstruction, do you recommend adopting $z$ *only* at pretraining, and would you advise a team mid-pretraining-run to switch tables at a resume point (your 750M continue@4K design is nearly this ablation — was the in-window PPL cost of the switch measured at the resume point, e.g., first-100M-token loss gap)?

## 5. Cross-disciplinary connections the paper should acknowledge or exploit

- **Frame potential / Welch bound (Benedetto–Fickus 1999; Welch 1974):** $\operatorname{tr}(\Gamma^2)$ is a frame potential; Thm. 1 is a Welch-type budget trade-off between mean pairwise redundancy and effective rank — naming it strengthens, not dilutes, the identity.
- **Principal angles / canonical correlations (Jordan; Hotelling 1936):** $c_{\omega\nu}$ is the mean squared cosine of the principal angles between pair subspaces; the whitening-invariance proof is the standard congruence argument — cite to signal the metric is canonical, not ad hoc.
- **Prolate spheroidal concentration and Landau–Widom necessary density (Slepian–Pollak; Landau–Widom 1980):** the resolution floor $|\omega-\nu|\gtrsim\pi/L$ is a necessary-density constraint whose admissible log-density is $\propto\omega$; this *derives the direction* of the cosh fast-ward tilt from 1980s sampling theory and would be the paper's best free argument for $\rho_\tau$'s shape.
- **Sinusoid frequency-estimation Fisher information (Rife–Boorstyn; Kay 1993):** variance $\propto$ (window second moment)$^{-1}$·SNR$^{-1}$ formalizes when two nearby frequencies are resolvable from the same data the attention head actually sees — the natural bridge from W2's "attention prior" to geometry.
- **High-resolution quantization / optimal point density for integration (Gersho; Gray–Neuhoff; Fejes Toth for energy-minimizing point placement):** eq. A.1.26 is Gersho's formula; the optimal-density structure for $\int w/\rho^2$-type distortions is $\rho\propto w^{1/3}$ — worth checking whether the true objective's weight reproduces a cosh-like law or a different exponent (a possible route to improving EVQ itself).
- **Optimal design of experiments on harmonic regression bases (Fedorov; Pukelsheim):** choosing frequencies to condition a regression on $[0,L]$ is a classical D/A-optimal design problem; the $\min(\phi,\psi)$ kernel is Brownian covariance, making $\mathcal C_{\rm app}$ an integrated-MSE design criterion in a random-walk-on-log-frequency prior — stating the surrogate this way makes its assumptions auditable.
- **Nonuniform Fourier sampling / annihilation kernels (Vetterli–Marzattola–Blue; Dragottti–Vetterli):** the dual question (recover sparse spectra from nonuniform frequency placement) has exact aliasing/annihilator machinery directly relevant to Lemma A.11 and to the $2\pi$-alias structure the surrogate cannot see (W4).
- **Feature-map conditioning in attention (Chiang–Yogatama, cited at 02_related.tex:37-38):** log-det / Shannon rank are the D-optimal analogues of $r_2$ and are the quantities that govern *learnability* of coefficients in the basis — one reason the co-adaptation collapse may track log-det better than Rényi-2; a one-row test would be informative.

## 6. Adoption-realism assessment

As a consultant to pretraining teams: the decomposition is instantly usable as a *specification standard*, and that alone is worth the paper — "base = 500K" does not define a table, and today's papers mostly write it as if it did. The cost side is genuinely small: one $K$-vector, no kernel changes (the RoPE application operator is untouched — this is what makes adoption cheap in a fused-attention stack), one calibration pass for the derived variant, and the $c{=}1$ rule ($\tau=d_{\rm head}/\sqrt{L_{\rm train}}$) is a one-liner. The evidence, however, supports *two different products*, and the paper should be clearer about which is which. (1) *Pretraining-time $z$*: the three-seed identification, 50.9M factorial, MLA, 750M/1.485B crossovers — a real, directionally consistent but not-yet-blast-radius result: in-window deltas are ~zero and OOD deltas are large; a team switching at a resume point is executing exactly the 750M "continue@4K" experiment, whose first-token cost the paper does not measure (Q7). (2) *Deployment-time $z$*: the OLMo/Qwen zero-training story is persuasive *for checkpoints that already fail at target length* (Native 0.00), where any swap is upside — but the transplant obstruction and co-adaptation table are the authors' own evidence that a swap is a lossy re-basing, and the session-routing policy (Native short, long-table routed) is the honest interface, at the cost of two KV-cache regimes and doubled in-window regression testing. I do not read the paper as supporting "switch tables mid-lifecycle freely"; I read it as supporting "choose $z$ at construction; route at deployment." The zero-training story is coherent — the derived table's residual-energy dependence is a calibration, not training, and the fact that the label-free ramp matches it (61.04 vs 60.47) actually *simplifies* adoption (Q5). What would move a team today: effect sizes at frontier scale (all mature evidence is ≤8B, mostly ≤1.5B), a comparison against simply raising the base within the geometric family on the same checkpoints (the 151.9M target-matched columns gesture at this but stop short of a base-sweep Pareto frontier), and the crossover-prediction result of Q3, which would let a team *choose* $\tau$ for its budget instead of inheriting $c{=}1$ whose optimality evidence is 7/9 configuration means.

## 7. Ratings

- Soundness (1–4): **3** (claims are inside scope and unusually well-hedged; deductions for prior-dependence of static numbers, unbounded surrogate gap, and thin seed counts on headline deployment/8B rows)
- Presentation (1–4): **3** (disciplined and honest, but dense; three grid conventions, anchored-vs-deployed tables, and the abstract's baseline anchoring demand a first pass that §3.1's clarity does not always extend to)

## 8. One-sentence recommendation to the area chair

Accept (lean) — the $a/R/z$ decomposition, its pinned-support causal identification, and the model-discipline of the co-adaptation and transplant results are a durable contribution, contingent on the authors bounding or numerically checking the surrogate-to-true-objective gap, quantifying separation-prior sensitivity of the 46→2 headline, and repositioning YaRN/LongRoPE and the single-seed DiT "breadth" claims to match their own evidentiary standards.
