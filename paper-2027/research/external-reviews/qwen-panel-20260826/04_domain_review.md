# Reviewer report — Seat R2: Domain Reviewer

## 1. Paper summary

The paper decomposes a finite RoPE table into $x_k=-\log\omega_k = a + Rz_k$ — sampled support $(a,R)$ plus an interior allocation $z$ on the fixed $K=d_{rot}/2$ pairs — and argues that geometric RoPE silently fixes $z_k = k/(K-1)$, coupling support and allocation into one scalar base. Its three pillars are: (i) an exact, phase-invariant sin/cos pair-subspace geometry (a Rényi-2 budget identity, a slow-channel collapse law, a transplant obstruction for linear Q/K compensation, and a co-adaptation crossover experiment); (ii) controlled interventions isolating $z$ — most notably a 151.9M three-seed from-training control with both endpoints and log-span pinned against an FMRoPE-at-$\theta{=}L_{train}{=}256$ geometric baseline, and a zero-training frozen-checkpoint swap on OLMo-2 1B that moves nine-task 16K RULER from 0.56% to 60.47% at matched support and amplitude; and (iii) EVQ-Cosh, a closed-form inverse-CDF table (quantiles of $\rho_\tau \propto \cosh(\tau(1-\phi))$) derived as the unique optimum of a stated convex surrogate.

From where the field sits, the mechanisms are individually known: YaRN's ramp, LongRoPE's searched per-channel scale factors, and LeRoPE's learned non-geometric profiles are all interior moves, and base-scaling is the standard support move. The paper's real novelty is not the observation that non-uniform frequency placement helps — the field has conceded that since 2024 — but (a) the pinned-support causal control, which no prior method paper executes cleanly, (b) the phase-invariant effective-dimension diagnostic, and (c) a zero-search closed-form profile. The manuscript's own controls repeatedly show the profile *shape* barely matters (ramp ≈ derived; matched-exponential ≈ EVQ), which sharpens the question of what the constructive theory contributes, and its related-work section under-engages the NTK-by-parts / base-scaling family that the decomposition is explicitly defined against.

## 2. Strengths

**S1 — The pinned-support control design is a genuine methodological contribution to this subfield** (Sec. 2 / §2.1, 02_identification.tex:7–22; App. A5 "What is pinned", a5_identification.tex:7–26). Every scalar a base/range method expresses — sampled extrema, log-span, init, data order, token budget, 32 evaluation anchors — is held equal; only 30 interiors move. I know of no prior RoPE paper that runs a from-training experiment with both endpoints *and* span pinned. The honest reporting of the target-matched reversal (FMRoPE favoured 3/3 seeds when each arm's support is retargeted; a5_identification.tex:82–117) is exactly the disclosure this literature lacks.

**S2 — Phase-invariant full-pair geometry, done correctly.** The paper is right that most RoPE analyses see a cosine-phase slice, and the closed-form cross-Gram (App. A1 eq. 3) with whitened-canonical-correlation collision (03_theory.tex:53–78) is the correct object. Theorem 1's identity checks out (see §6 below), and the headline static number reproduces: I recomputed the 23 pairs with $\omega L\le 1$ at $L{=}4096, b{=}500\text{K}, K{=}64$ and get $\bar c = 0.99986$, $r_2 = 2.0003$ — the "46 nominal dims, ~2 effective" claim is exact.

**S3 — The co-adaptation 2×2 (Table coadapt) is the most scientifically useful table in the paper.** The weights×tables crossing with interaction contrast $-3.537$, CI $[-5.17,-3.04]$ (03_theory.tex:153–161), and the observation that the worst cell is the one whose static $r_2$ *improves* most, cleanly bounds what the geometry can claim. This should be more prominent, not buried mid-section.

**S4 — The matched-support log-linear arm is the right operationalization of "just raise the base."** Uniform exponents between the pinned 4K→16K-equivalent endpoints (= a pure base extension) scores 0.56 vs derived 60.47 (Table frozen-fixed-support, 04_experiments.tex:43–54). This answers the practitioner counterfactual head-on, and the movement-profile ramp reaching 61.04 without labels or optimisation (App. A6, a6_mature_scale.tex:90–104) is a practically valuable recipe.

**S5 — Statistical hygiene**: pre-specified arms, configuration-level bootstrap CIs, exact sign-flip $p$-values with multiplicity honestly flagged (Table m4 caption), seed-as-unit conventions, and a completed 180/180 factorial (a5_identification.tex:127).

**S6 — Breadth of the persistence ladder** — MLA at $K{=}16$ (App. A3, Table mla), 750M full-parameter continuation, 1.485B early training, 8B LoRA with a causal attention-blocking probe (a6_mature_scale.tex:133–143), and a video DiT with an analytic low-phase diagnostic (App. A2). Each row keeps its own unit and endpoint rather than pooling.

## 3. Weaknesses

**W1 — [MAJOR] The related-work framing misclassifies the allocation-equivalent prior art, and the "not a disguised base change" claim is stated more strongly than it is true.** Anchors: 02_related.tex:10–30; 01_intro.tex:40–42; 03_theory.tex:27–32.
- "Range and support" lists PI, YaRN, LongRoPE as *support* movers. But YaRN's ramp is precisely an interior allocation on a re-extended support, and **LongRoPE's evolutionary search over per-channel scale factors is literally a search over $z$** — the paper cites it with one clause ("channel-wise range transport"). LeRoPE is acknowledged, correctly, as the learned-$z$ family. The novelty is therefore the *pinned-support causal isolation* and the *closed-form law*, not the existence of the interior degree of freedom. Sec. 2's claim "fixed support + different z ⇒ different trained-model behaviour … not a disguised base change" is (i) trivially true against the geometric family (a geometric table is exactly $z=$ uniform, so pinning $(a,R)$ fixes the table by definition — 03_theory.tex:27–32 concedes this) and (ii) not the interesting comparison. Against the *interesting* competitors — NTK-aware/dynamic-NTK scaling (never named in the manuscript; I verified zero occurrences), YaRN with boundary retuning, LongRoPE-searched factors — the paper's own evidence (ramp ≈ derived; matched-exponential ≈ EVQ rule, $p{=}0.836$, Table m4) says those families would land in the same behavioral basin. Fix: rename the claim to "not a disguised *geometric-base* change"; add an explicit NTK-aware/dynamic-NTK paragraph (the canonical support-move-with-uniform-$z$); recategorize LongRoPE as searched-$z$; and state precisely what is new vs. FMRoPE (scalar base with uniform exponent grid — i.e., the $z$-axis FMRoPE leaves untouched), which the current text does only implicitly.
- NTK-aware scaling and dynamic NTK appear nowhere in text or bib; for a paper whose abstract headline is "RoPE has a spectral budget" written as $(a,R,z)$, omitting the most-used support-move of the base-scaling era is a coverage hole (see Missing references).

**W2 — [MAJOR] The paper's own controls show the closed-form *shape* is doing almost no behavioral work, yet uniqueness-under-surrogate is sold as a headline contribution.** Anchors: Abstract ("unique for that surrogate"); 01_intro.tex:44–46; a6_mature_scale.tex:100–105 (derived-vs-ramp CIs contain zero on both models); Table m4 row "EVQ rule − exponential" (+0.0007, $p{=}0.836$); a5_identification.tex:154–157 (boundary-τ multipliers order interchange). The honest reading of the empirical portfolio is: *any reasonable monotone interior warp at fixed endpoints* (cosh, exponential, movement-profile ramp) beats uniform-$z$ — i.e., non-uniformity, not the Cosh law, is the active ingredient. "EVQ-Cosh" as *the* named contribution overstates profile specificity; the theory then supplies a decoration (a unique optimum of a surrogate the paper never shows predicts behavior better than the matched exponential). Fix: either (a) find and report a regime where profile shape is separable with CIs (per-task profiles in RULER; head-dimension sweeps; comparison against LeRoPE's learned 52M profiles at matched budget — see W6), or (b) rescope the contribution as "the allocation axis plus one admissible closed-form instance" and demote "unique" to the appendix. As written, the abstract sentence "closed-form … unique for that surrogate" is technically accurate and practically inert.

**W3 — [MAJOR] The surrogate-validation metric contradicts the paper's own phase-invariance doctrine, and the surrogate's relation to the exact objective is qualitative at best.** Anchors: 03_theory.tex:61–62 ("an analysis that fixes one content phase … is not invariant to rotation inside the pair"); 03_theory.tex:186–202 (C_app); App. A1 "Cosine-slice diagnostic" (a1_proofs.tex:218–261, Table surrogate-validation). The one functional validation of the constructive theory is a *cosine-feature kernel* collision sweep — exactly the slice the theory section says is not the right object — run on an endpoint-inclusive grid that no deployed table uses. Two further gaps: (i) the exact small-$x$ collapse from Prop. 2 gives a *per-channel linear* penalty $\propto (\omega L)^2 = (L b^{-\phi})^2$, while the surrogate's $\min(\phi,\psi)$ term is a *pairwise cumulative-slow-mass* functional with no $L$ or $b$ dependence at all — so the surrogate does not encode "slow relative to the context window," which is the entire content of the budget story (the paper's own $\tau \propto d_{head}/\sqrt{L}$ scaling lives in a separate post-softmax model, a1_proofs.tex:342–430, doing the $L$-dependence the surrogate lacks). (ii) The full phase-invariant $c_{\omega\nu}$ is available in closed form (App. A1 eq. 3) at trivial CPU cost — the authors simply did not run the 12-configuration sweep with it. Fix: re-run Table surrogate-validation with $\bar c$/$r_2$ under the uniform prior; add the missing $(\omega L)^2$ justification linking C_app to Prop. 2; or state plainly that Thm. 4 picks one warp among behaviorally-equivalent warps.

**W4 — [MAJOR] The from-training causal evidence at realistic bases is thin, and the headline frozen number rests on one checkpoint with 20 rows per task.** Anchors: 02_identification.tex:9–19 (θ=256 control); a5_identification.tex:123–125 ("2 bases" — the values are *never stated* in the manuscript; Table m4 effects are $\Delta$NLL ≈ −0.01 against a 6.21 floor with $p$ 0.027–0.125); Table frozen-fixed-support (OLMo nine tasks × 20 rows, CIs conditioned on that task set; Qwen derived−log-linear CI [0.25, 17.50] points wide). At $\theta{=}256$, $K{=}32$, the table spans only 8 octaves and no channel is deeply collapsed (the paper concedes this, 03_theory.tex:133–135) — this is precisely the regime where an interior redistribution into short-range channels has the most room to help, which is exactly the practitioner's objection ("this is a small-base effect"). The b=500K trained results (750M: 45.1→24.4 PPL@16K; MLA: 138.8→95.6) are at modern bases but are *deployed*-grid comparisons where endpoints also shift slightly (midpoint vs geometric quantiles), so they are not $z$-only. Fix: state the factorial's bases; add one anchored exact-range control at b ≥ 10K within the 50–150M budget; and report the frozen OLMo result under task resampling, not only row resampling within the nine confirmation tasks.

**W5 — [MINOR, but a real error] Proposition 2 as stated is off by a factor of 2.** Anchors: 03_theory.tex:117–119; a1_proofs.tex:53–59. I verified the Taylor expansion against the paper's own closed-form cross-Gram: the leading term is $2-\lVert Q_{x,y}\rVert_F^2 = \tfrac{19}{6300}(x^2-y^2)^2 + O(\epsilon^6)$, while $1-c_{\omega\nu} = \tfrac12(2-\lVert Q\rVert_F^2) = \tfrac{19}{12600}(x^2-y^2)^2$. The diagnostic clincher is in App. A1 itself: the quoted "exact-to-leading ratio 1.00058" at $x{=}0.05, y{=}0.10$ is with respect to the *doubled* constant — my recomputation gives ratio 2.0011 against the stated formula and 1.00057 against $1-c$. The headline $r_2{=}2.00$ is unaffected (it uses $c$, correctly). Fix: restate the deficit as $1-c_{\omega\nu}$.

**W6 — [MINOR] Learned-table comparison is under-powered relative to the field's state of the art.** Anchors: Table pe-dominant (125M, $L_{train}{=}128$ → 8K — an extreme regime; learnable-$\tau$ and learned-inv-freq arms get one LR sweep); 02_related.tex:46–50 (LeRoPE's 52M–2.5B learned non-geometric profiles cited but never compared). The paper's own Table pe-dominant shows learned tables beat Geo but lose to EVQ — a genuinely informative result that is nearly invisible in the main text and never discussed in the intro's contribution list. A matched-budget LeRoPE-style run, or at minimum fitting the free-form learned profile against the cosh density (does LeRoPE's learned shape resemble $\rho_\tau$?), would connect the constructive and learned routes and strengthen the claim that $z$ is the shared coordinate.

**W7 — [MINOR] Terminology and artifact hygiene.** (i) "EVQ" is never expanded anywhere in the manuscript (I checked all sections, appendices, and the intro). (ii) The 151.9M baseline is named "FMRoPE" but is, as the paper itself says, the geometric exponent grid at $\theta=L_{train}$ — label it "geometric (FMRoPE identity)" in the main text or readers will assume FMRoPE-as-a-method has a non-uniform interior. (iii) Three different "HoPE" papers sit in the bib (chen2025hope cited; li2025hope and dai2025hyperbolicrope uncited) — disambiguate. (iv) ~32 of the 73 bib entries are never cited in the compiled document (including kazemnejad2023impact — the exact length-generalization paper the decomposition begs to engage — press2022alibi, sun2022xpos, zhang2024found, liu2024lost, jin2024selfextend, zhu2024pose, chi2022kerple, chen2024clex); this creates a false impression of coverage. (v) Dead artifacts: `appendix/a4_supporting_experiments.tex` and `tables/table_evq_ramp.tex` use an undefined macro and reference an unbuilt label — excluded from main.tex (so the PDF is fine), but they will confuse anyone reading the released source. (vi) Table coadapt's $r_2$ values: my recomputation under the stated uniform prior and midpoint grid gives 4.78/13.68 vs the printed 4.57/12.54 — plausible convention differences (softmax metric vs uniform prior), but the exact prior and grid for that table should be pinned in the caption, since geometry values are load-bearing there.

**W8 — [MINOR] The title promises the budget governs behavior; the theory never predicts a behavioral quantity.** There is no prediction anywhere of, e.g., the crossover length from $(K,R,\tau)$ or $r_2$, or the OOD NLL gap from $\Delta\bar c$. The co-adapt table shows $r_2$ can anti-correlate with loss (12.54 ↔ 76.20 PPL) when weights mismatch. The paper's framing ("geometry diagnoses; interventions establish") is the correct epistemics, but a constructive theory that also *predicted* one crossover — the repeated 4K→8K/16K crossover at 750M and 1.485B would be the target — would move this from "measurement vocabulary" to "explanatory theory" and justify the title's assertiveness.

## 4. Questions for authors

**Q1.** Which two bases does the 50.9M factorial span (a5_identification.tex:123–125)? Report the anchored-EVQ-minus-geometric contrast split by base: does the from-training allocation effect survive at b ≥ 10K, or is it concentrated at small base?

**Q2.** The movement-profile ramp is "the discrete YaRN-family linear ramp"; official YaRN is the same ramp with *default* boundaries. Given ramp ≈ derived (CIs contain zero), do you agree that the OLMo derived-vs-YaRN +52.5 points is substantially a *boundary-placement* result, and that YaRN with your model-relative split would be statistically indistinguishable? If yes, say so in §4.1; if no, show the profile-specific contrast.

**Q3.** Proposition 2: confirm the factor-of-2 correction (deficit $1-c_{\omega\nu}$, not $2-\lVert Q\rVert_F^2$), given your own ratio 1.00058 implies the doubled constant as written.

**Q4.** What do you conclude from the co-adapt asymmetry — Geo weights under EVQ table collapses to 76.20, EVQ weights under Geo table only to 23.05? That asymmetry looks like a curvature/anisotropy statement about the binding distribution and is unexplained.

**Q5.** Will you run the 12-configuration surrogate validation under the phase-invariant closed-form $c_{\omega\nu}$ (App. A1 eq. 3)? Your own §3.1 argues the cosine slice "is not invariant" — the cheap, non-contradictory version of that table exists in closed form.

**Q6.** Can the budget identity quantitatively predict the observed crossover length (Geo-vs-EVQ, 750M and 1.485B) from $(K, R, \tau, L_{train})$? Even one out-of-sample prediction would close the theory-to-behavior loop the title implies.

**Q7.** Prop. 2's slow limit is $\mathrm{span}\{1,\Delta\}$ — a constant plus a *linear-in-distance* direction, i.e., an ALiBi-like bias subspace produced redundantly by 23 pairs. Given that reading (and the NoPE evidence of Kazemnejad et al. 2024), what is the minimal-budget argument: would 1–2 slow channels plus reallocation of the other 21 suffice? This would make the budget account sharply quantitative.

## 6. Theory verification notes

- **Theorem 1 (budget identity)** — Verified. $\mathrm{tr}\,\Gamma = 2K$; off-diagonal blocks give $\sum_{i\ne j}\mathrm{tr}(Q_{ij}Q_{ji}) = \sum_{i\ne j}\lVert Q_{ij}\rVert_F^2 = 2K(K{-}1)\bar c$ using $\Gamma_{ji}=\Gamma_{ij}^\top$; $c_{\omega\nu}\in[0,1]$ because $Q$'s singular values are canonical correlations. Arithmetic correct. The result is exact bookkeeping given the definitions — but the definitions (phase-invariance, block whitening) are the contribution.
- **Proposition 2 (collapse)** — Taylor algebra re-derived numerically against the paper's own cross-Gram: **the stated constant is wrong by a factor of 2 for the stated quantity** (see W5); the corrected form $1-c_{\omega\nu}=\tfrac{19}{12600}(x^2-y^2)^2+O(\epsilon^6)$ is verified (ratios 1.00057/1.00021/1.00053 at three test points; $O(\epsilon^6)$ scaling consistent). The $\mathrm{span}\{1,\Delta\}$ limit and the softmax-metric centring argument ($F\mathbf 1 = 0$, $\sin(\omega\Delta)/\omega \to \Delta-\mathbb{E}\Delta$, nondegenerate 3-point support needed for the quadratic direction) check out. $r_2 = 2.00$ for the 23 slow pairs reproduced exactly (my value 2.0003); the "23 pairs" counting at 4096/500K/K=64 verified.
- **Theorem 3 (transplant obstruction)** — Proof chain verified: $\Delta{=}0 \Rightarrow A^\top B = I$; similarity of the one-parameter groups; differentiate at 0 $\Rightarrow$ similar generators; spectrum $\{\pm i\omega_k\}$ with multiplicity gives multiset equality; the integer-position variant via eigenvalues of $\mathcal R(1)$ up to $2\pi$-alias is also correct. The argument is elementary (≈ folklore rigidity of rotation representations); I'm not aware of a prior exact statement for fixed linear Q/K compensation, but the same spirit circulates in geometric RoPE analyses (incl. the cited frayed-RoPE line). Correct, thin.
- **Theorem 4 + Corollary (cosh density)** — Euler–Lagrange derivation verified end to end: $g'=\int_\phi^1\rho$, $g''=-\rho$; BCs $\rho'(0)=-\tau^2$ (via $g'(0)=\int\rho=1$), $\rho'(1)=0$; $\rho_\tau=\tau\cosh(\tau(1-\phi))/\sinh\tau$ satisfies the ODE, both BCs, and mass 1; PSD of the $\min$ kernel via the $(\int_s^1 f)^2$ identity verified; strict convexity gives uniqueness; positivity of $\rho_\tau$ makes the inequality constraint inactive — correct. Corollary: $F_\tau(\phi)=1-\sinh(\tau(1{-}\phi))/\sinh\tau$ and the arcsinh quantile both verified, including $\tau\to0$ limits. Textbook calculus of variations executed correctly; note the surrogate contains no $L$ or $b$ (W3).
- **Self-consistency theorem and closed-form $T_2$ (App. A1)** — Verified by hand: $\tau^2T_2+T_1=\tau\coth\tau$ with $T_1=(2\tau^2+\tau\sinh 2\tau)/(4\sinh^2\tau)$ and $T_2=(\sinh 2\tau-2\tau)/(4\tau\sinh^2\tau)$; numerics at $\tau{=}1,2$ agree.
- **Single-crossing lemma** — Bound $\phi_c\le 1-1/\sqrt3$ via $\sinh\tau/\tau \ge \cosh(\tau/\sqrt3)$ (coefficient-wise $3^n \ge 2n+1$): correct.
- **Lemma (Fourier aliasing)** — Correct; trivial but well-deployed.
- **$S_{\chi^2}$ small-$\tau$ expansion** — $\rho_\tau = 1+\tau^2\eta+O(\tau^4)$ with $\eta=(1-\phi)^2/2-1/6$, and $\int_0^1\eta^2 = 1/20-1/18+1/36 = 1/45$: verified; $\tau^4/(45d_{head})$ correct.
- **Spot checks** — $\tau{=}4$ quantile shifts (707→9.69, 134,609→1,720): verified; $\rho_2(0.1){=}1.71$, $\rho_2(1){=}0.55$: verified; Table frozen arithmetic (60.47−0.56=59.91≈59.92; 60.47−7.94=52.53≈52.54): rounding only. Co-adapt $r_2$ values close but not exactly reproduced under the stated uniform prior (4.78/13.68 vs 4.57/12.54) — convention not fully pinned (W7.vi).

## 7. Ratings

- Soundness (1–4): **3** — Interventions are unusually well-controlled and honestly reported; but the headline "0.56→60.47" rests on one frozen checkpoint with 20 rows/task, the from-training causal effect at realistic bases is unspecified/weak, the Prop. 2 statement carries a verified factor-of-2 error, and the constructive theory is decoupled from behavior by the authors' own shape-insensitivity controls.
- Presentation (1–4): **3** — Structurally careful, with excellent explicit-scope language, but punishingly dense, "EVQ" never expanded, ~32 uncited bib entries misrepresent the engaged literature, and the Sec. 2 novelty claim is imprecise (W1).
- Contribution significance (1–4): **3** — The pinned-support identification and phase-invariant geometry are real, reusable contributions to the RoPE design literature; the Cosh law itself is shown, by the paper's own data, to be one of several equivalent warps.
- Overall score (1–10, ICLR scale): **6** (borderline accept — lean accept after the framing is corrected).
- Confidence (1–5): **4** — I work in this exact subarea and verified the theory numerically/algebraically; I did not (cannot, read-only) verify training-run provenance.

## 8. One-sentence recommendation to the area chair

Accept in direction but require the authors to fix the framing before it enters the literature: the paper has earned the claim that interior allocation $z$ is a causal, separately identifiable coordinate, but not yet the claim that their closed-form Cosh profile earns its own name — and it must stop classifying NTK-style base scaling and YaRN/LongRoPE-style non-uniform transport as work its taxonomy hasn't engaged.
