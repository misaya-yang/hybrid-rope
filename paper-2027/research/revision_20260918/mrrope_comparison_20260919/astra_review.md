# Independent comparative review of Papers A and B

I read both PDFs in full, including their appendices (A: 16 pages; B: 35 pages), using the supplied text extractions and rendered pages. I inspected A’s radix derivation, main PPL/NIAH page, cosine/attention plots and YaRN proof, and B’s complete-pair geometry, TailSpline construction, phase bounds, NCP derivation and high-factor results as rendered PDF pages. I used no repository files, external sources, earlier reviews or author guidance. Publication-status headers have no bearing on this judgment. Page numbers below are printed PDF pages.

**Overall judgment: B is the stronger research paper as presented.** Its advantage is not simply more experiments or more equations. It identifies more precisely what a comparison changes, supports important claims with paired controls and uncertainty estimates, and distinguishes mathematical properties of a frequency table from learned task performance. A has a useful, focused method and an accessible mixed-radix interpretation, with substantial reported training-free gains. However, its strongest theoretical and consistency claims outrun its supporting arguments. B remains a conditional practical advance: natural-task gains are substantially weaker than its retrieval gains, and native-window and high-extension costs are real.

## What A establishes, and where it overreaches

**A has a substantive practical contribution.** MrRoPE-Pro is a simple closed-form progressive allocation inside an inherited transition band. It requires no weight updates and preserves standard rotary computation. The Uni/Pro alternatives make the radix interpretation operational rather than merely renaming RoPE. In Table 2 (p. 8), Llama’s reported 128K RULER score rises from 79.9 to 86.6, and Qwen’s 64K score from 63.2 to 70.4. Table 3 reports substantial Llama InfiniteBench gains on KV retrieval (9 to 27%) and dialogue QA (10 to 22%), with ties or smaller gains on other tasks. This is a credible reason to investigate and use the method, conditional on reproducing the protocol.

A’s main scientific weaknesses are specific:

1. **The radix interpretation is not established as the claimed proof.** On p. 3, Eq. (7) equates a sum weighted by `β^(j−1)` with one weighted by `b^((1−j)/Dr)`, although `β=b^(1/Dr)` requires the opposite exponent sign. This is present in the rendered PDF. Correcting the sign would repair that algebraic equality, but the subsequent empirical linearity plot still does not prove a positional-recovery theorem or provide an approximation-error guarantee. The construction can be a useful analogy and reparameterization without claiming more. Likewise, PI is broadly advertised as unified in the introduction, while App. A.1 (p. 12) explicitly says it cannot be expressed as the stated radix conversion; it is instead included as position normalization. That distinction should appear in the main claim.

2. **The regressive-YaRN proof omits a material condition.** App. A.2.1, Eqs. (23)–(25), sets `c=β−sα` and then uses a comparison whose direction depends on the sign of `(s−1)c`. Under its stated defaults and tested `s=4,16`, `c>0`, so this objection does not overturn those tested settings. But the p. 14 claim that the factors always decrease for all admissible bases is too broad: `s=β/α` gives equality; larger scales reverse the relevant inequality. For a concrete check of its own formula, take `α=1, β=32, s=64`, and consecutive turn counts `8,4,2`: `λ_previous=472/220≈2.14545`, `λ_current=220/94≈2.34043`, so the ratio is about 1.09088, not at most one. This is a scope/correctness repair, not a demand for a new experiment.

3. **Its strongest context-limit and optimality statements are unsupported.** Section 4.4 and Fig. 5 (p. 9) use an unweighted cosine sum to discuss a theoretical context upper bound. The paper does not supply the content/training assumptions required to make that diagnostic an upper bound on the learned model’s usable context. The random-pair attention plots in Fig. 6 likewise do not establish the claimed mechanism of matching pretraining distributions. The conclusion’s statement that the method pushes the effective context upper bound “to its maximum” (p. 10) has no maximization theorem or exhaustive comparison behind it. The concern is the explicit maximum/bound claim, not the absence of an unclaimed theorem proving optimal task accuracy.

4. **Some empirical prose contradicts the printed results.** Table 1 (p. 7) prints Qwen MrRoPE-Pro PPL **3.579** at 32K versus YaRN **2.589** and Uni **2.581**, while the prose says Pro is lowest at every length. I checked the rendered table; this is not extraction corruption. It may be a typo, but the PDF does not establish the intended number. Table 4 (p. 15) also has Uni outperforming Pro at Llama-2 16K and 32K. These do not erase the favorable aggregate evidence, but they invalidate the blanket consistency language as printed. Appendix B also reverses the α/β labels between the boundary formula/defaults and its later recommendation (pp. 14–15), and Table 5’s Llama caption/subtable/model-window labels disagree (p. 16). Those ambiguities directly matter for reproduction.

A gives sample counts for PPL (ten sequences) and InfiniteBench (100 per subset), but not enough detail or uncertainty for several broad claims. RULER lacks per-task results/sample counts in the PDF. Native RoPE is absent from the main short-window comparison, so beating another extension recipe at native length does not demonstrate preservation of native quality. Comparisons with GPT-4/Yi/Kimi are informative context, but version and exact shared-evaluation details are insufficient for strong general cross-model superiority claims. No fine-tuning comparison is necessary to validate A’s explicitly training-free objective.

## What B adds beyond A, and what still limits it

**B’s advance is more than a different smooth curve.** Earlier methods already establish that frequency changes matter; B acknowledges this on pp. 1 and 9. Its strongest conceptual addition is identifying effects beyond both sampled endpoints and total log-frequency movement, while distinguishing placement from assignment to learned coordinates.

The 151.9M experiment (pp. 3, 21; Table 5) fixes endpoints and pairs initialization, token order, training budget and optimizer within three seeds. Cosh improves extended-length NLL with retained support, while retargeting the support reverses the result in every seed. This is informative positive and negative evidence: it establishes that placement matters and that its benefit interacts with support policy. The short-training factorial (pp. 22–23; Table 7) also reports a matched exponential doing similarly to Cosh, appropriately weakening any Cosh-exclusive interpretation.

The frozen **T–C equal-displacement experiment** is especially strong: at Llama 32K, TailSpline scores 68.27 versus 66.17%, with paired difference +2.10 points and interval [1.11, 3.08], sharing endpoints, bands, gain and total displacement (pp. 7, 24–25). At 16K the difference is −0.25 points with an interval crossing zero. Thus higher-order shape matters in an observed target condition, but is not universally beneficial. The full T–P gain of 11.72 points must not all be called an equal-displacement shape effect; B correctly distinguishes the contrasts. The permutation experiments preserve the frequency multiset and strongly change results (p. 23), demonstrating learned coordinate association without proving that every possible permutation is harmful.

**B’s theory is adequate for its actual, bounded claims.** Complete-pair canonical correlations avoid privileging a cosine phase, and the effective-rank identity and slow-subspace limit are precisely stated (pp. 3–5, 14–17). These specialize classical linear algebra rather than constitute an entirely new general theory of attention. Their value is the distinction they clarify, reinforced by an explicit cosine-only counterexample and a successful task allocation with lower positional effective rank. The discrete TailSpline optimization and phase-residual comparison (pp. 5–6, 17–20) are transparent and checkable. I independently checked the displayed finite-grid formulas for several widths: positive increments sum to one, the stationarity equations agree, and T/C sums match numerically. The proofs support their declared structural objectives, not the proposition that smoothness or rank determines downstream success. B explicitly disclaims the latter. Its Cosh density objective and NCP binary reference objective remain design choices; demonstrating their practical utility does not require proving global task optimality.

The main remaining limitations are:

1. **Native-window costs deserve stronger main-text emphasis.** At Llama 8K the fixed TailSpline table scores 85.16 versus native RoPE’s 90.03%, a −4.87-point difference with interval [−6.57, −3.15] (pp. 7, 24). Calling this merely a “slight reduction” understates a reliable practical cost, concentrated in FWE, where the printed scores are 9.33 versus 87.33%. Keeping FWE in the aggregate is scientifically good. NCP’s separate OLMo native-window gains do not cancel TailSpline’s Llama cost. Similarly, Llama Cosh-LoRA raises native 8K PPL from 6.817 to 10.068 while greatly improving 16K PPL (p. 34, Table 23). A quality-throughout-the-window paper should make these costs readily visible next to the gains.

2. **Natural-task evidence supports conditional gains, not general superiority.** OLMo Natural-QA improves by 3.30 points with marginal interval [0.39, 6.37], while Llama is effectively tied and YaRN has the highest Llama point estimate (pp. 8, 25). Qwen’s seven-book gain over MrPro is promising but rests on few independent sources. The simultaneous intervals on p. 30 cross zero for OLMo and Qwen’s marginally positive comparisons; GLM book pools and Llama LongBench v2 are also uncertain. These are properly disclosed limitations, not evidence that no gain exists. The abstract’s modest claim of gains on natural QA is defensible; a blanket natural-task win would not be.

3. **Higher factors expose substantial trade-offs.** Table 18 (p. 30) shows Llama 128K retrieval and PPL improving while book-QA F1 falls from 20.17 to 13.21, with a negative paired interval. Qwen 256K single-needle retrieval improves, but five-book PPL rises from 23.93 to 30.34. These results are useful, honest boundary evidence. They prevent exporting the main `s=4` success as universal ultra-long quality. Bringing their direction into the main text would improve calibration.

4. **The breadth has unequal causal strength.** The six-run fixed-support study is a clean identification experiment. The 1.485B comparison uses unanchored frequencies and different trainer implementations (p. 33); it supports a recipe-level result, not equally clean pure-placement attribution. MLA likewise uses unanchored midpoint Cosh. B reports these facts and need not discard the experiments, but readers should preserve the distinction. Small 10/task transfer panels, one NF4 70B checkpoint, and one-model NCP evidence establish useful transfer/existence examples rather than deployment-wide guarantees.

B is considerably more reproducible from the PDF: precise table constructors, gain, grids, revisions, source counts, scoring, decoding caps, same-target evaluation and bootstrap units are given. Its many named constructions and legacy-versus-clean panels also create cognitive overhead. Tightening the main hierarchy around identification, TailSpline deployment, and conditional corroboration would improve the story without deleting negative results or broader evidence.

## Residual uncertainty and fair overall comparison

A major unresolved cross-paper discrepancy should be investigated before drawing direct numerical method rankings from the two papers. A reports Llama-3-8B-Instruct MrRoPE-Pro RULER **86.6% at 128K, s=16** (p. 8, Table 2); B reports MrPro **1.28% at Llama 128K, s=16** (p. 30, Table 18). Both label the endpoint as all 13 tasks. Their prompts, samples, precision and implementation details are not demonstrated to match. B also distinguishes released index-ramp YaRN from a turn-count formulation (p. 19). These observations make protocol reconciliation essential, but do not establish the cause of the MrPro discrepancy or prove an implementation error in either paper. B’s paired results remain stronger documented within-protocol evidence; they are not automatically a reproduction or refutation of A’s scores.

A’s unique strengths are its focused mixed-radix lens, simple progressive method, and reported strong high-factor performance without training. B’s unique strengths are stronger causal identification, mathematically bounded claims, paired uncertainty, the native-window same-target context-use test, and evidence across frozen, training and adaptation settings with informative reversals. On the same standards, I favor **B overall for scientific reliability, explanatory precision and demonstrated breadth**, while treating A as a useful predecessor with important practical results and substantially weaker theoretical claim discipline.

Neither paper needs to solve optimal allocation for every task, compare against all training-based systems, or prove all model-scale transfer to be valuable. Those are possible future research directions. The required repairs are narrower: correct A’s explicit algebra/scope/result contradictions; keep B’s practical costs and heterogeneous evidence visible; and reconcile the large cross-paper baseline gap before making head-to-head numerical claims. I cannot verify raw outputs, actual implementation identity, archive completeness, or novelty against sources beyond what these PDFs describe.
