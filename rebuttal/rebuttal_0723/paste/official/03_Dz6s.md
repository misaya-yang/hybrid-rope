**3. The FMRoPE distinction, and the control that tests it.**

In \\(\\omega_i = b^{-u_i}\\) with \\(u_i = 2i/d\\), a method can parameterize the realized vector, the base, or the exponent. FMRoPE selects the base: its §6.1 sets \\(\\theta = L_{\\rm train}\\) at training and \\(\\theta = L_{\\rm target}\\) at inference, and its abstract describes the effect as shifting the band toward lower frequencies, with the exponents uniform in i and the normalized geometric order preserved. EVQ-Cosh holds the base fixed and treats the exponent map \\(u_i \\to \\varphi_\\tau(u_i)\\) as the unknown, deriving it from a stated variational objective before the model learns anything.

If the effect reduced to where the band sits, pinning the band would remove it. We pinned both sampled extrema and the log span — the quantities a base or range choice sets — together with initialization, token order, optimizer, budget and all 32 anchors, and varied only the 30 interior frequencies. OOD NLL improves by **0.478/0.205/0.113** at 512/1K/2K, winning **32/32, 27/32 and 22/32** anchors; a three-seed factorial reproduces the direction across bases 500K/1M and head dimensions 32/64/128, favouring non-geometric allocation in 10/12 configurations.

The boundary belongs alongside it: when both grids are retargeted to a declared length the ordering reverses (**+0.061/+0.182/+0.279**), which is what a target-aware range method is for, and the two compose rather than compete — the target-range rule applied on top of an EVQ grid improves that model by **0.098/0.529/0.638** NLL. We should have cited Oka et al.; the revision will.
