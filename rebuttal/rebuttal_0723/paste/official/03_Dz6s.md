Your review did not raise the relation to FMRoPE, so our answer to it sits in the other threads. The metareview makes it the first of its three conditions, so you may be asked about it.

**The distinction is read off the frequency table.** In \\(\\omega_i = b^{-u_i}\\) with \\(u_i = 2i/d\\), a method can parameterize the realized vector, the base, or the exponent. FMRoPE selects the base: its §6.1 sets \\(\\theta = L_{\\rm train}\\) at training and \\(\\theta = L_{\\rm target}\\) at inference, and its abstract describes the effect as shifting the band toward lower frequencies. Under that rule the exponents stay uniform in i and the normalized geometric order is preserved exactly. EVQ-Cosh holds the base fixed and treats the exponent map \\(u_i \\to \\varphi_\\tau(u_i)\\) as the unknown, deriving it from a stated variational objective before the model learns anything. The two act on different objects at different stages.

**The control that tests it.** If the effect reduced to where the band sits, pinning the band would remove it. We pinned both sampled extrema and the log span — the quantities a base or range choice sets — together with initialization, token order, optimizer, budget and all 32 anchors, and varied only the 30 interior frequencies. OOD NLL improves by **0.478/0.205/0.113** at 512/1K/2K, winning **32/32, 27/32 and 22/32** anchors; a three-seed factorial reproduces the direction across bases 500K/1M and head dimensions 32/64/128, favouring non-geometric allocation in 10/12 configurations.

The boundary belongs alongside it. When both grids are retargeted to a declared length the ordering reverses (**+0.061/+0.182/+0.279**), which is what a target-aware range method is for, and the two compose rather than compete — applying the target-range rule on top of an EVQ grid improves that model by **0.098/0.529/0.638** NLL.
