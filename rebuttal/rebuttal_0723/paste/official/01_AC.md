Three things did not fit the character limit of our first response, and we close with what we would and would not claim.

**1. The third condition, anchored on what the submission already did.**

The metareview credits the manuscript with distinguishing exact theoretical results, surrogate assumptions and heuristic operating choices. That separation is submitted Table 1. What this cycle adds is an empirical layer under each row of it rather than a new claim above it, and the collision-score and τ-window figures already given sit under the second and third rows.

Two results we did not report belong there as well. On the surrogate row: the collision reduction the closed form achieves is not merely better than geometric — a collision-only oracle search performed directly on the exact kernel reaches comparable reductions, so the closed form arrives where a search arrives, without the search. On the attribution row: within the zero-parameter ladder, a deformation-matched exponential is statistically indistinguishable from Cosh (+0.0007 NLL, exact sign-flip p = 0.836). What that ladder identifies is therefore the allocation axis rather than Cosh specifically, and §6 will say so. We would rather the committee weigh that than a stronger claim we cannot support.

**2. Evidence that the gain is remote-content use, not local reconstruction.**

A generation endpoint can be met by a model that has learned the task format. The matched 8B adaptation tests the harder question directly. Native/EVQ NLL is 1.919/2.309 at 8K, **4.691/3.181** at 16K and 6.899/4.851 at 32K, with the 16K and 32K direction holding in **all 24 evaluation packs and all three domains**. At true 16K, deleting the remote gold block from every head worsens EVQ NLL by **1.506** while leaving the matched Native control essentially unchanged, and median target-block hit@16 rises from **18.75% to 64.06%**. A model that degrades when the distant block is removed, and retrieves it more often, is using it.

**3. Sampling, stated per result rather than in aggregate.**

Three agreeing seeds: the fixed-schedule ladder, the exact-range factorial, the base and head-dimension controls, and the 432M MLA study. One training seed per arm, matched across arms: the 1.485B and 8B adaptations, with a second independently trained EVQ seed reproducing the OLMo strict-retrieval result (**69/100 and 67/100** at 8K against Native 0/100). One trajectory: the 1.485B from-scratch branch. The submission also reported a 750M continuation whose 8K strict autoregressive exact goes from **0% to 77.5%** (Table 12).

**What we would ask the committee to weigh.**

Our claim after this cycle is narrower than the submission's. Training-time exponent allocation is a separately identifiable design axis — identifiable under controls that fix the positional operator, parameter count, tuning budget, reference grid, deformation magnitude and spectral range — whose effect survives to 8B in free-running generation and reaches real-document multi-hop QA.

We do not claim that Cosh is a universal optimum, that τ = d/√L is exact, or that EVQ replaces target-aware range scaling; under retargeting the FMRoPE rule obtains lower NLL and we report that. The in-window trade-off is real, and its size differs sharply between the two modes of use. Training from scratch at 1.485B it is **+0.0381 NLL** at 4K, against −0.0437 at 8K and −0.1351 at 16K. Retrofitting a mature checkpoint it is the much larger RULER macro drop we reported, improving to 42.44% when adaptation is restricted to Q and K. The revision will report the two separately rather than as one number. Oka et al. report the same ordering for base selection, with FMRoPE underperforming conventional RoPE in short contexts, which suggests the trade-off belongs to this design space rather than to our rule.

The three conditions were the right ones to set, and the experiments that answer them exist because they were named.
