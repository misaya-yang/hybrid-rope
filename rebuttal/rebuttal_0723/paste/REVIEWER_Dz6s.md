In one line each: the endpoint concern is answered by matched free-running generation on pretrained 1.485B and 8B models, where 8K full-answer-plus-EOS exact goes from 18/100 to 98/100 and 8B 16K RULER macro from 0.295% to 14.03%; the theory-to-practice concern by an explicit four-level separation; and the YaRN question by a precise substrate-dependence result over three seeds.

**1. Evidence breadth and endpoint type.** Your concern has two parts: the endpoints were PE-dominated and teacher-forced, and the models were small rather than pretrained LLMs. We agree that extrapolated perplexity and teacher-forced NLL-gap retrieval cannot establish usable context. The new evidence changes both: free-running generation instead of teacher-forced scoring, and mature pretrained models instead of small ones.

We built the readout in three stages, each one tightening the attribution.

*Stage 1 — does the effect exist at scale?* The submitted version already showed it does: LLaMA-3-8B LoRA PPL goes from 176.3 to 21.5 at 16K and from 1942.5 to 104.3 at 32K, unusable to usable at 2× and 4×.

*Stage 2 — can a mature model use that headroom when generating freely?* On OLMo-2 (1.485B actual parameters), matched Native and EVQ arms received an identical downstream chain — a 300-step LoRA parent, a 100-step query-gap alignment and a 32-step answer-plus-EOS stage — with the same trainer, seed, data, optimizer, budget and objective, and every backward pass capped at 4,096 physical tokens.

*Stage 3 — what does it cost?* That chain adapts Q, K, V and O. Since RoPE acts only on Q and K, we froze every inherited V/O LoRA tensor — verified bitwise — and continued only Q/K, again at physical 4K.

Row 1 is the strictest endpoint available: literal equality of the complete answer string followed by a terminal EOS token, judged from raw token IDs. On the beyond-training-gap subsets EVQ is 48/50 at 8K and 31/66 at 16K, and its 16K failures are all wrong-number retrievals — none is an incomplete answer, a format error or an EOS failure.

Rows 2 and 3 answer whether the in-window cost belongs to the frequency table or to the adaptation. Restricting adaptation to the tensors RoPE acts on raises the EVQ arm from 37.51% to 42.44% at 4K and from 21.29% to 31.63% at 8K, and narrows the 4K gap from 44.7 to 29.8 points. Adaptation scope therefore affects the trade-off materially; the gap that remains means it does not account for all of it. On row 4, under the same restriction, the two arms are level in-window, from unadapted parents that score 0% at every length.

In all three stages the two arms share the base model, the LoRA rank, the data, the seed, the optimizer, the budget and the evaluation rows. The single difference is the fixed frequency table.

At 8B, under identical physical-8K supervision over the same 13 families, EVQ raises 16K official macro from 0.295% to 14.03%, where Native-LoRA is at essentially zero. Two of those families are built from real-document QA datasets — SQuAD v2 and HotpotQA distractor, with human-written questions over real passages — and at 16K Native-LoRA's normalized exact is zero on all 13 tasks while EVQ-LoRA succeeds on several including HotpotQA, so the effect reaches real-document multi-hop QA and not needle retrieval alone; the same two families separate the same way on OLMo-2, with 8K HotpotQA at 0% versus 25%. At 8K — the training length itself — the two metrics disagree and we report both: Native leads official macro (94.44% versus 77.60%), which credits partial and substring matches, while EVQ leads normalized exact (21.54% versus 17.69%).

A matched 8B adaptation speaks to whether the model uses distant content rather than reconstructing it locally. Native-LoRA/EVQ-LoRA NLL is 1.919/2.309 at 8K, 4.691/3.181 at 16K and 6.899/4.851 at 32K, with the 16K and 32K direction holding in all 24 evaluation packs and all three domains. At true 16K, median target-block hit@16 rises from 18.75% to 64.06%, and deleting the remote gold block from every head worsens EVQ NLL by 1.506 while leaving the Native control essentially unchanged. The submission also contained a 750M continuation (8K strict autoregressive exact 0% → 77.5%) and a 432M scarce-channel MLA study over three seeds (PPL@16K 138.8±5.5 → 95.6±4.1 with only 16 rotary channels, all three seeds agreeing), alongside the 129M/382M video-DiT experiments.

**2. Theory-to-practice separation.** We will keep four levels explicitly distinct: (i) the conditional theorem, exact only for the stated convex surrogate C_app; (ii) exact-kernel diagnostics, which are empirical checks rather than proofs; (iii) trained-model results, which test the predicted direction without validating the surrogate itself; and (iv) the τ rule, where the derivation motivates τ ∝ d_eff / √L_train while setting the proportionality constant to one is an empirical operating convention rather than a theorem or a point optimum.

The strongest trained-model connection we have is a repeated direction rather than a confirmation of the surrogate. Reallocating a finite channel budget toward long range should incur a small in-window cost and produce a larger extrapolation gain. In the 1.485B from-scratch branch the EVQ−Geo NLL delta moves monotonically with the extrapolation ratio: +0.0724 at 2K, +0.0381 at 4K, −0.0437 at 8K, −0.1351 at 16K, with paired document-level bootstrap 95% intervals excluding zero at every length. The same ordering reappears in the 1.485B adaptation (4K 2.235 → 2.548 against 16K 4.851 → 2.925) and at 8B (8K +0.390 against 32K −2.048). Several independent trained models could have contradicted that prediction and did not. We present this as directional consistency, not as evidence that the surrogate is correct: it supplies the direction, not the crossover length.

**3. YaRN.** We agree that a common fixed scale does not rule out a better-tuned Geo+YaRN or a channel-wise search, and we did not run that joint sweep. Submitted Table 3 applies the same fixed s = 8 transform to both substrates across three training seeds: Geo+YaRN versus EVQ+YaRN obtains 61±3% versus 100±0% teacher-forced NLL-gap retrieval at 8K, and PPL 82.9 versus 70.9. The supported conclusion is substrate dependence — the training-time frequency table changes what the same inference-time transform can recover — and we will state it that way rather than as complementarity, which would suggest a stable additive gain we have not established. Settling the tuned comparison would require a joint tuning sweep for both substrates at matched budget.

One clarification on scope. The tuned comparison bounds what we may claim about EVQ×YaRN. It does not bear on the standalone allocation result, which rests on fixed zero-parameter schedule controls in which the positional operator, training protocol, parameter count and tuning budget are identical across arms and only the frequency table differs.

These are task-family-adapted results: training and evaluation rows are disjoint but the generator families are shared, so this is length transfer within a task family, and each matched comparison above uses one training seed per arm. Seed replication comes from a separate strict first-number retrieval experiment on the same model, where two independently trained EVQ seeds score 69/100 and 67/100 at 8K against Native 0/100, and 49/100 and 48/100 on fresh all-long-gap prompts against Native 0/100.
