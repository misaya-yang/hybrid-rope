We bring one external development to the record. It was posted after our submission and after all reviews were submitted, so it appears in neither, and it bears on all three of the metareview's conditions. We also give a status index of the three conditions; full numbers and protocols are in our responses.

**Status of the three conditions.**
1. *Novelty over FMRoPE* — answered by the parameterization-level distinction (base/range selection versus training-time exponent allocation) and by the exact-range control, which pins the quantities a base choice sets; the missing citation is conceded, with the citation and contrast table committed to in our response.
2. *Direct controlled comparison* — run, with both the fixed-range advantage and the retargeting boundary reported.
3. *Stronger evaluation* — the pre-specified 1.485B from-scratch comparison from the public step-0 initialization, matched 1.485B and 8B retrofits with free-running generation and 13-family RULER endpoints, and the three-seed held-out base/head suite.

**New: concurrent work bearing on all three conditions.** LeRoPE (arXiv 2607.10134, posted 11 July 2026 — after our submission and after all reviews were submitted) trained the RoPE frequencies as free parameters — one shared scalar per band — on a 52M–2.5B ladder. Three findings:

- The learned allocation is non-geometric, consistent across every seed and scale tested, and the point where it departs from the geometric grid tracks the training length. This is independent evidence that the geometric table is a design choice rather than a neutral default.
- Training with frequencies **frozen** to a separately learned table — zero learned positional parameters — captures **63.6%** of the full method's gain (p-RoPE captures 10.4%). This bears directly on the metareview's disentanglement point: a second group independently locates most of the benefit in the table itself, which is the axis our exact-range control and fixed-schedule ladder isolate.
- The two methods sit at opposite ends of the same trade-off. LeRoPE's gain concentrates in a band at roughly 2.2 times the training length, whose logit contribution changes sign beyond trained distances, and its authors report that it degrades more sharply than RoPE under naive extrapolation. Our responses report the mirror-image ordering for EVQ-Cosh: the from-scratch comparison pays in-range and gains beyond the training window, increasingly so as the evaluation length grows. Two independent methods, betting on opposite sides of the same boundary, suggest the boundary is structural. The in-window side of that trade was bounded in advance in the submission itself: the waterbed inequality (§3.6) bounds the allocation's in-range cost as quartic in τ, and the multi-scale measurement reports ±1.7% in-range against 10–46% long-range gains (Appendix C, Table 20 and Figure 9, supporting tier). Oka et al. state the same trade for base selection in their Section 6: matching the base to the training length "improves extrapolation but hurts interpolation, and this trade-off persists under position interpolation such as YaRN."

Scope: we cite LeRoPE as evidence that the frequency table is a design axis worth optimizing, not as evidence for our particular allocation. Our response committed to citing Oka et al. with a parameterization-contrast table; the revision will cite LeRoPE alongside it.
