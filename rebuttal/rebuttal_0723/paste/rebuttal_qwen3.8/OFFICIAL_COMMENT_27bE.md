We add one development posted after our submission and after all reviews were submitted, surface two items already in the submission, and give an index of where your five requests stand. The development bears on the question your review pressed hardest: separating allocation shape from parameterization and optimization effort. In §4 of our response we rested that attribution on the three-level fixed-schedule ladder, precisely because a comparison against a learned baseline cannot isolate the variable. A concurrent work has independently isolated it from the opposite direction.

**The new evidence.** LeRoPE (arXiv 2607.10134, posted 11 July 2026 — after our submission and after all reviews were submitted, so concurrent work) trained the RoPE frequencies as free parameters — one shared scalar per frequency band, 32 in total — on a 52M–2.5B ladder. Its Table 2 (217M) is the relevant result: training with the frequencies **frozen** to values learned by an independent run — zero learned positional parameters during training — captures **63.6%** of the full method's gain, while p-RoPE captures 10.4% (validation PPL 18.2377 → 18.1714, versus 18.1335 with full learning). Their own reading is that both the final frequency set and the joint training dynamics contribute; the majority, however, is carried by the set itself. Their route is learn-then-freeze; ours constructs the table in closed form with no learning step. Both routes converge on the same side of your distinction: the table itself — not the capacity used to obtain it — is what carries most of the benefit.

Two of their other findings connect to your questions on base/head generality and on the operating rule. The learned profile is non-geometric and consistent across every seed and scale they test, and the point where it departs from the geometric grid tracks the training length — directionally consistent with a training-length-dependent allocation rule, although their objective differs from ours and we claim no equivalence between the two tables. On scope: LeRoPE optimizes in-distribution loss, and its authors report that it degrades more sharply than RoPE under naive extrapolation — the regime we address. We cite it as independent evidence that the frequency table is a design axis, not as evidence for our particular allocation.

**Two items from the submission that bear on surrogate validation and base coverage.** (i) The surrogate–exact agreement was quantified in Appendix A, Table 6: the operating-point prefactor predicted by the surrogate versus the exact-kernel collision minimizer across nine configurations gives a ratio of 0.981±0.003 (CV 0.28%) — agreement at the 2% level, on the kernel itself, independent of any trained-model outcome. (ii) On bases: the trained text models in the submission used b=500K, and the submission included trained runs at other bases in the video-DiT family — a sweep from base 100 to 50,000, with EVQ holding its advantage at every base of 1,000 and above (one to two seeds) — plus exact-kernel validation at 10K/100K/500K. The post-submission factorial extends trained-text coverage to 500K/1M across head dimensions.
