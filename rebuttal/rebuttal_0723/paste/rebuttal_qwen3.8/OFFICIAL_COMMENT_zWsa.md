**1. The in-window trade-off, quoted from the source your review cites.** Our response reports the parallel ordering for training-time allocation: the from-scratch comparison pays in-range and gains once evaluation moves past the training window. For precision we now quote the source rather than paraphrase it. Section 6 of Oka et al. states the trade-off as a property of the design itself: matching the base to the training length "improves extrapolation but hurts interpolation, and this trade-off persists under position interpolation such as YaRN." The closing clause is the substantive addition: the source states the trade-off as persisting under YaRN-style transforms, so it is structural to the design space — documented by the paper your review cites — rather than an idiosyncrasy of our rule.

**2. The standard of novelty.** We add one framing not stated in our response. This field routinely accepts within-family variants as contributions — AdamW relative to Adam differs by decoupled weight decay; YaRN relative to NTK-aware scaling by its interpolation ramp and attention temperature. Our difference from FMRoPE is not of that kind: it is a difference in what is parameterized, and at what stage, not a difference within the same parameterization (the levels are detailed in our response). We ask that the novelty determination apply at least the standard the field applies to within-family variants: is the distinction real, and is it consequential? The exact-range control reported in our response tests exactly that question.

**3. Concurrent work on learned frequency tables.** LeRoPE (arXiv 2607.10134, posted 11 July 2026 — after our submission and after all reviews were submitted, so concurrent work) trains the RoPE frequencies as free parameters on a 52M–2.5B ladder and cites Oka et al. We state the full result and its scope in our comment to the AC.
