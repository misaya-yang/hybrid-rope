Additions to the record, bearing on your first two concerns: independent measurements from a concurrent work posted after our submission and after all reviews were submitted, and two evaluations already in the submission.

**On your second point — the Table 3 phenomenon, observed independently.** Our response committed to a specific reading of that result; what we add now is an independent measurement of the same phenomenon. LeRoPE (arXiv 2607.10134, posted 11 July 2026 — after our submission and after all reviews were submitted, so concurrent work) learns the RoPE frequencies during training — one shared scalar per band — and evaluates the trained models under inference-time extension: different training-time tables, the same transform family, compared on what each recovers. Its authors report that LeRoPE with NTK-by-parts scaling and YaRN's attention temperature outperforms the same recipe applied to RoPE and p-RoPE, with YaRN's temperature swept per method for fairness, and that the single best extrapolation configuration overall is LeRoPE with YaRN. On single-needle retrieval at 2.52B under YaRN, evaluated at lengths up to 32k, LeRoPE outperforms RoPE and p-RoPE at every evaluated length. The submission also contained a dedicated quantification of this leverage: Appendix C, Table 19 (three seeds, training length 256) measures the same-scale YaRN gain at 8K as −3.1%±1.6 in PPL on the geometric substrate against −40.7%±1.6 on EVQ at the rule-prescribed τ=4.0. The revision will carry through that framing, as promised in our response, and cite this work.

**On your first point — a real-document QA evaluation was already in the submission.** Your concern that the strongest endpoints were synthetic or PE-dominated is fair, and our response added free-running generation to address it. For the record, one evaluation in the submission itself belongs in this discussion: the QuALITY downstream study (Appendix C, Table 21; 454M, n=2,086 real-document questions, single seed, supporting tier). Four-option accuracy hovers near the 25% random baseline for every configuration — capacity, not positional encoding, is the binding constraint at this scale, the paper's own reading — but the gold-answer NLL advantage grows from −1.7% in-distribution to −30.1% at 2× and −21.4% at 4× extrapolation: a probability-space signal on a real downstream task, available at review time.

**Independent evidence for the conceptual frame.** Your review described the operator/table separation as an effective way to think about design choices that are often considered fixed. LeRoPE's results support exactly that separation: with the frequencies free, every seed and scale they test departs from the geometric allocation, in the same direction, and the point of departure tracks the training length. Training with frequencies frozen to a separately learned table captures 63.6% of the gain at 217M (p-RoPE: 10.4%) — the majority of the benefit lies in the table itself, with the remainder attributed by its authors to joint training dynamics.

**Scope.** LeRoPE targets in-distribution loss, and its authors report that it degrades more sharply than RoPE under naive extrapolation — the regime our method addresses. We cite it as evidence that the frequency table is a design axis worth optimizing, not as evidence for our particular allocation. Nothing in the evidence reported in our response changes.
