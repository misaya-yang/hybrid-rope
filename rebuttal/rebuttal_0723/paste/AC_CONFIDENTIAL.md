We are writing about the novelty determination, since the metareview identifies it as the decisive issue and we believe it turns on a question that can be settled technically.

**The distinction, stated so it can be checked.** For methods acting on the RoPE frequency table, written as ω_i = b^(-u_i) with u_i = 2i/d, we distinguish three parameterization levels. Position interpolation, YaRN and LongRoPE optimize a transport ω → g_T(ω) applied to an already-pretrained spectrum, possibly per-frequency. NTK-aware scaling, ABF and FMRoPE optimize the scalar base/range b → b(T) for a declared context, preserving the normalized geometric order. EVQ-Cosh optimizes the third level: u_i → φ_τ(u_i), the choice of training grid at fixed nominal base, fixed before the model learns anything, obtained as the closed-form inverse CDF of the stationary density of a stated variational problem. Most prior RoPE extrapolation work acts through the realized spectrum or the base/range; our starting question was whether the exponents should be spaced uniformly at all.

**The experiment that tests it.** If the effect were reducible to base or range selection, pinning the range would remove it. We pinned the highest sampled frequency, the lowest sampled frequency and the log span, together with initialization, token order, optimizer, budget and all 32 evaluation anchors, and changed only the 30 interior frequencies. The endpoint-normalized Cosh interior allocation improves out-of-distribution NLL by 0.478/0.205/0.113 at 512/1K/2K, winning 32/32, 27/32 and 22/32 anchors. A separate three-seed factorial reproduces the direction under the same exact-range constraint across bases 500K/1M, training lengths 256/1024 and head dimensions 32/64/128: the preregistered 1.25× Cosh and deformation-matched exponential arms favor the non-geometric allocation in 10/12 and 9/12 structural configurations respectively. Since every quantity a scalar base can set is held fixed, the effect is not attributable to base selection.

We claim no more than that. The levels are distinguished by what is parameterized and at what stage, not by what is numerically achievable, and we do not claim to beat target-aware range methods: in the retargeted control the ordering reverses, with the evaluated uniform-FMR rule obtaining lower NLL than endpoint-normalized Cosh at these lengths, which we report.

**The question we would ask the committee to settle.** The review's inference is that a shared motivation and a shared observation about ineffective channels imply insufficient novelty. Applied consistently, that inference also excludes AdamW relative to Adam, YaRN relative to NTK-aware scaling, and — we note this only because it is the comparison at issue — FMRoPE relative to prior base-scaling work, since each shares its predecessor's motivation and differs in what is parameterized. Our difference is larger than those: we do not propose another value of b, or another rule for choosing it, but replace the geometric family by solving for the allocation map itself. We ask only that the usual standard be applied.

**Two points of record.** The review states that the tested models are too small and asks for evidence at 1B–7B; the submission includes an 8B LLaMA-3 LoRA evaluation in Appendix D, Table 23, alongside 750M, 432M MLA and 129M/382M video-DiT experiments. The review also asks for RULER; our response reports 13-family RULER at 1.485B and a matched comparison at 8B, where 16K official macro goes from 0.295% to 14.03%. We mention these only because the metareview inherited the "small models and limited downstream evaluation" framing, and both parts are now addressed.

We are not asking that any review be discounted. We are asking that the novelty determination rest on whether the training-time exponent allocation is a distinct and consequential design variable — which the exact-range control was built to test — rather than on overlap of motivation.
