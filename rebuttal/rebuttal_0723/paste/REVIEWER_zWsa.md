Thank you — you are right that Oka et al. should have been cited and directly compared, and the revision will do both. We answer your four questions in order. In one line each: (1) FMRoPE and EVQ-Cosh act on different objects at different stages of the frequency table; (2) the matched comparison you asked for is run — with the spectral range pinned EVQ improves OOD NLL by 0.478/0.205/0.113 and wins 32/32, 27/32 and 22/32 anchors — and the two also compose; (3) RULER is included, and at 8B EVQ raises 16K macro from 0.295% to 14.03%; (4) the evidence now spans 1.485B and 8B, matched arm for arm.

**1. Novelty over FMRoPE.** We do not claim novelty for the observation that some RoPE channels are ineffective; §2 of the submission credits prior work for channel inequality, citing Barbero et al. (2025) on high/low-frequency specialization and Resonance RoPE on critical frequencies.

Our claim concerns a different parameterization level, read directly off the frequency-table formula. In ω_i = b^(-u_i) with u_i = 2i/d: (1) whole-vector transport, ω → g_T(ω), possibly per-frequency, applied to an already-pretrained spectrum — PI, YaRN, LongRoPE; (2) base/range selection, b → b(T) for a declared context, preserving the normalized geometric order — NTK-aware, ABF, FMRoPE; (3) training-time exponent allocation, u_i → φ_τ(u_i), the choice of training grid at fixed nominal base, fixed before the model learns anything — EVQ-Cosh, with φ_τ the closed-form inverse CDF of the stationary density of an explicit variational surrogate.

One point of description is worth making precise. Your review describes FMRoPE as modifying the allocation of frequency bands. FMRoPE sets θ_train = L_train, which — as its abstract puts it — shifts the band toward lower frequencies. Shifting where the band sits and redistributing the channels inside it are different operations: the first is level (2), the second is level (3). EVQ-Cosh does not propose a different value of the base or a different rule for choosing one; it holds the nominal base fixed, treats the allocation map as the unknown, and solves a stated variational problem for it. That is why we describe it as replacing the geometric family rather than selecting within it.

Both methods are parameter-free, and we see why that reads as overlap. But position interpolation and NTK-aware scaling are also parameter-free; the property is shared by most of this literature and does not by itself identify a mechanism.

The two rules also differ in what they require. FMRoPE's rule is defined by θ_train = L_train and θ_infer = L_target, so it selects a range for a deployment length declared in advance; EVQ-Cosh sets the training-time grid and introduces no deployment target. A range method is target-aware by construction; an allocation method is not.

**2. Direct matched comparison.** Run. Holding the sampled extrema, log span, initialization, token order, optimizer, budget and all 32 evaluation anchors identical, and changing only the 30 interior frequencies, the endpoint-normalized Cosh interior allocation improves fixed-range OOD NLL over the evaluated FMRoPE rule by 0.478/0.205/0.113 at 512/1K/2K, winning 32/32, 27/32 and 22/32 anchors. A three-seed factorial reproduces the direction under the same exact-range constraint across bases 500K/1M, training lengths 256/1024 and head dimensions 32/64/128: the pre-specified 1.25× Cosh and deformation-matched exponential arms favor the non-geometric allocation in 10/12 and 9/12 structural configurations respectively.

This meets the bar you set, in the condition that isolates the variable we claim: range is fixed, only interior allocation changes, and EVQ wins.

We also report the boundary: when both grids are retargeted to the declared length the ordering reverses (+0.061/+0.182/+0.279), which is what a target-aware range method is for. That bounds the fixed-range claim without weakening it. The two levels also compose rather than compete: applying the target-range rule on top of an EVQ grid improves that model by 0.098/0.529/0.638 NLL at 512/1K/2K, so EVQ is not an alternative to a range method. We implement the published §6.1 rule directly: θ_train = L_train, θ_infer = L_target, ω_i(θ) = θ^(-2i/d).

**3. RULER.** Included, and it improves. On LLaMA-3-8B, with identical physical-8K supervision over all 13 RULER families in a matched Native/EVQ comparison, EVQ raises 16K official macro from 0.295% to 14.03%, where Native-LoRA is at essentially zero. Two of the 13 families are built from real-document QA datasets, SQuAD v2 and HotpotQA distractor, with human-written questions over real passages. At 16K, Native-LoRA's normalized-exact score is zero on every one of the 13 tasks, while EVQ-LoRA has normalized-exact successes including the HotpotQA task, so the improvement reaches real-document multi-hop QA and not needle retrieval alone.

At 8K — the training length itself — the two metrics disagree and we report both: Native leads official macro (94.44% versus 77.60%), which credits partial and substring matches, while EVQ leads normalized exact (21.54% versus 17.69%). The same length-transfer pattern appears independently on OLMo-2 (1.485B) with all backward passes capped at physical 4K: under an identical 13-family continuation, Native/EVQ official macro is 82.16%/37.51% at 4K, 0.08%/21.29% at 8K, and 0%/6.13% at 16K.

We also asked whether that 4K gap belongs to the frequency table or to the adaptation, which changed Q, K, V and O. RoPE acts only on Q and K, so we froze the inherited V/O LoRA — verified bitwise — and continued only Q/K. The same 13-family continuation then gives 72.19%/42.44% at 4K, 2.02%/31.63% at 8K and 0.38%/5.03% at 16K: the EVQ arm improves at 4K and 8K and the gap narrows from 44.7 to 29.8 points. On 200 held-out 2WikiMultiHopQA prompts the arms are level in-window, exact match 22.0%/21.5% at 4K, and separate beyond it, 0%/17.5% at 8K and 0%/4.0% at 16K, from unadapted parents that score 0% at every length. Adaptation scope therefore affects the trade-off materially without accounting for all of it.

**4. Scale.** On OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct (1.485B actual parameters), matched Native and EVQ arms received an identical downstream chain — a 300-step LoRA parent, a 100-step query-gap alignment and a 32-step answer-plus-EOS stage — with the same trainer, seed, data, optimizer, budget and objective, and every backward pass capped at 4,096 physical tokens. The endpoint is literal equality of the complete answer string plus a terminal EOS token, from raw token IDs. Native/EVQ is 95/100 versus 100/100 at 4K, 18/100 versus 98/100 at 8K, and 0/100 versus 60/100 at 16K; on the beyond-training-gap subsets EVQ is 48/50 at 8K and 31/66 at 16K. The two arms share the base model, LoRA rank, data, seed, optimizer, budget and evaluation rows — the single difference is the fixed frequency table.

We also trained OLMo-2 from the public step-0 initialization for 2.097B counted tokens under a protocol specified in advance, sharing initialization, recipe, data-order prefix and evaluation rows with the released geometric checkpoint. Geo/EVQ PPL on 128 document-disjoint documents is 161.19/167.45 at 4K, 163.88/156.87 at 8K and 182.73/159.64 at 16K, with paired bootstrap intervals excluding zero at every length and 122/128 and 126/128 documents favoring EVQ at 8K and 16K — a small in-window cost against a gain that grows with the extrapolation ratio.

On why the finding carries across this range: the intervention is the same size at every scale, since the frequency table has d_head/2 entries and our 151.9M held-out suite, OLMo-2 at 1.485B and LLaMA-3 at 8B all carry the same 64 rotary channel pairs. Empirically the same ordering — a small in-window cost against a larger long-range gain — appears at all three.

The matched 8B adaptation adds the probability side at that scale: Native-LoRA/EVQ-LoRA NLL is 4.691/3.181 at 16K and 6.899/4.851 at 32K, holding in all 24 evaluation packs and all three domains, with median target-block hit@16 at true 16K rising from 18.75% to 64.06%.
