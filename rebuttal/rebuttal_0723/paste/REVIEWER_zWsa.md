Thank you — you are right that Oka et al. should have been cited and directly compared, and the revision will do both. We answer your four questions in your order.

**1. Novelty over FMRoPE.** We do not claim novelty for the observation that some RoPE channels are ineffective; §2 of the submission credits prior work for channel inequality, citing Barbero et al. (2025) on high/low-frequency specialization and Resonance RoPE on critical frequencies.

Our claim concerns a different parameterization level, read directly off the frequency-table formula. In ω_i = b^(-u_i) with u_i = 2i/d: (1) whole-vector transport, ω → g_T(ω), possibly per-frequency, applied to an already-pretrained spectrum — PI, YaRN, LongRoPE; (2) base/range selection, b → b(T) for a declared context, preserving the normalized geometric order — NTK-aware, ABF, FMRoPE; (3) training-time exponent allocation, u_i → φ_τ(u_i), the choice of training grid at fixed nominal base, fixed before the model learns anything — EVQ-Cosh, with φ_τ the closed-form inverse CDF of the stationary density of an explicit variational surrogate.

Against that grid, one point of description is worth making precise. Your review describes FMRoPE as modifying the allocation of frequency bands. FMRoPE sets θ_train = L_train, which — as that paper puts it — shifts the frequency band toward lower frequencies. Shifting where the band sits and redistributing the channels inside it are different operations: the first is level (2), the second is level (3). EVQ-Cosh does not propose a different value of the base or a different rule for choosing one; it holds the nominal base fixed, treats the allocation map as the unknown, and solves a stated variational problem for it. That is why we describe it as replacing the geometric family rather than selecting within it.

Both methods are parameter-free, and we understand why that reads as overlap. But position interpolation and NTK-aware scaling are also parameter-free; the property is shared by most of this literature and does not by itself identify a mechanism. What distinguishes methods here is which object is optimized and at what stage.

The distinction is consequential rather than notational, and the control in (2) is designed to test exactly that: it fixes every quantity a scalar base/range can set and changes only the interior allocation.

**2. Direct matched comparison.** Run. Holding the sampled extrema, log span, initialization, token order, optimizer, budget and all 32 evaluation anchors identical, and changing only the 30 interior frequencies, the endpoint-normalized Cosh interior allocation improves fixed-range OOD NLL over the evaluated FMRoPE rule by 0.478/0.205/0.113 at 512/1K/2K, winning 32/32, 27/32 and 22/32 anchors. A three-seed factorial reproduces the direction under the same exact-range constraint across bases 500K/1M, training lengths 256/1024 and head dimensions 32/64/128: the pre-specified 1.25× Cosh and deformation-matched exponential arms favor the non-geometric allocation in 10/12 and 9/12 structural configurations respectively.

This meets the bar you set: a clear advantage under matched settings, in the condition that isolates the variable we claim. Range is fixed, only interior allocation changes, and EVQ wins.

We also report the boundary. When both grids are retargeted to the declared length, the ordering reverses (+0.061/+0.182/+0.279), which is what a target-aware range method is for. That limits our claim to fixed-range allocation advantage; it does not weaken the fixed-range result, and we claim neither complementarity with nor universal superiority over FMRoPE. We did not identify a public author implementation as of 23 July, so we used a paper-faithful reimplementation of the rule specified in §6.1: θ_train = L_train, θ_infer = L_target, ω_i(θ) = θ^(-2i/d).

**3. RULER.** Included, and it improves. On LLaMA-3-8B, with identical physical-8K supervision over all 13 RULER families in a matched Native/EVQ comparison, EVQ raises 16K official macro from 0.295% to 14.03%: at 2× the training length Native-LoRA is at essentially zero while EVQ retains nontrivial capability. Two of the 13 families are built from real-document QA datasets, SQuAD v2 and HotpotQA distractor, with human-written questions over real passages. At 16K, Native-LoRA's normalized-exact score is zero on every one of the 13 tasks, while EVQ-LoRA has normalized-exact successes including the HotpotQA task, so the improvement reaches real-document multi-hop QA and not needle retrieval alone.

At 8K — the training length itself, so in-window rather than extrapolation — the two metrics disagree and we report both: Native leads official macro (94.44% versus 77.60%), which credits partial and substring matches, while EVQ leads normalized exact (21.54% versus 17.69%). We independently obtain the same length-transfer pattern on OLMo-2 (1.485B actual parameters) with all backward passes capped at physical 4K. Under an identical 13-family continuation, Native/EVQ official macro is 82.16%/37.51% at 4K, 0.08%/21.29% at 8K, and 0%/6.13% at 16K. Thus Native learns the in-window task distribution more strongly, whereas EVQ retains capability beyond the training length; at 8K EVQ reaches 31% VT, 22% CWE and 60% FWE.

**4. Scale.** On OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct (1.485B actual parameters), with every LoRA backward pass capped at physical 4K, a matched Native/EVQ pair scores 0/100 versus 69/100 on strict autoregressive first-number exact at 8K on the official niah_single_1 task, with a second independently trained EVQ seed at 67/100 on the same rows. On a further 100-row set in which every generation-boundary gap exceeds the largest such gap available anywhere in 4K training (3,933 tokens; realized gaps 3,959–8,013), with zero query, source, value or answer overlap with the training, calibration or earlier evaluation rows, Native is 0/100 while the two EVQ adapters score 49/100 and 48/100 (paired exact McNemar 3.6e-15 and 7.1e-15). Because every test gap lies outside the training support, this is not interpolation within trained distances.

We also trained OLMo-2 from the public step-0 initialization for 1,000 steps and exactly 2,097,152,000 counted tokens under a protocol specified in advance, sharing the initialization, recipe, data-order prefix and evaluation rows with the released geometric checkpoint at the same milestone. Geo/EVQ PPL on the same 128 document-disjoint documents is 161.19/167.45 at 4K, 163.88/156.87 at 8K and 182.73/159.64 at 16K, with paired document-level bootstrap intervals excluding zero at every length and 122/128 and 126/128 documents favoring EVQ at 8K and 16K. We call this a same-initialization, same-recipe comparison: the released geometric checkpoint was produced by the upstream distributed trainer and our EVQ branch by a single-GPU loop on the same recipe. The effect is length-dependent rather than a constant offset — EVQ is 3.9% worse at 4K and 12.6% better at 16K.

The matched 8B adaptation adds the probability-side result at that scale: Native-LoRA/EVQ-LoRA NLL is 4.691/3.181 at 16K and 6.899/4.851 at 32K, holding in all 24 evaluation packs and all three domains, with median target-block hit@16 at true 16K rising from 18.75% to 64.06%.

One clarification on the premise that the tested models are too small. The submitted version already reported an 8B LLaMA-3 LoRA evaluation (Appendix D, Table 23), a 750M continuation with strict autoregressive retrieval (Table 12), a 432M scarce-channel MLA study (Table 18), and 129M/382M video-DiT experiments (Table 14). With the new results, the evidence now spans six model scales from 50.9M to 8B, two attention families, two modalities, and both from-scratch training and adaptation. We did not run a multi-seed full pretraining at 7B, and we claim no state of the art in any setting: the claim throughout is that the training-time exponent allocation is an independently identifiable design variable that repeatedly improves the out-of-distribution endpoints we tested relative to the geometric reference.

These are task-family-adapted results — training and evaluation rows are disjoint, but generator families are shared — and each matched comparison uses one training seed per arm, with a second independently trained EVQ seed reproducing the OLMo 8K and beyond-gap results.
