Thank you for separating the exact statement from the finite-τ operating regime. You identified three confounds — allocation shape against parameterization, against optimization effort, and against the operating-point rule — and we built experiments that take them apart one at a time. We answer your five requests in order.

**1. The approximation chain and finite τ.** You are right that the small-τ analysis does not determine a finite optimum, and that the submission let the shape rationale and the operating-point rationale sit too close together. We now separate four links: (i) Cosh is the stationary density of C_app — exact given that surrogate and nothing more; (ii) the quadratic surrogate, the discrete grid and the pure-tether branch are modeling choices, none forced; (iii) the analysis yields the *scaling form* τ ∝ d_eff / √L_train; (iv) the proportionality constant is not supplied by the analysis and is set to one by convention.

Link (iii)/(iv) is where your τ ≈ 4 observation lands, and we should have been explicit about why. Writing the local stationarity condition for the deployment objective gives θ* = [−g/h]₊, where g and h are functionals of the relative-distance structure of the data, the feature usage of the model and its heads, the base, K and endpoint/span convention, the deployment lengths and their weighting, and the objective. The expansion fixes how θ* scales with d_eff and L_train; it does not evaluate those functionals, so there is no reason for the constant to be universal — and the derivation therefore predicts that the best multiplier should move with the configuration while the scaling form does not.

**2. Independently tuned τ and matched non-Cosh schedules.** That prediction is what our two τ studies actually measure, and it reframes what they show.

The main study is a 99-run sweep over nine configurations — L_train ∈ {256, 512, 1024} crossed with d_head ∈ {32, 64, 128}, five τ values per configuration at pilot, then three seeds on the retained arms. Against midpoint-Geo the formula wins 7/9 configuration means and 18/27 paired runs, a mean of −0.0133 weighted extrapolation NLL. Against the pilot-selected neighbouring τ, evaluated only on the two seeds that did not participate in that selection, the formula wins 3/9. That second number is the one worth reading carefully: **the selected neighbour lies between 0.75× and 1.5× of the formula value in all nine configurations**. The formula is not picking the winner among adjacent points; it is placing the search in the right neighbourhood every time.

The exact-range factorial you asked for extends this under a stricter control: three seeds over two bases (500K/1M), two training lengths (256/1024) and three head dimensions (32/64/128), 12 structural configurations in which every schedule shares the same highest sampled frequency, lowest sampled frequency and log span, so the spectral range is identical by construction and only interior spacing differs.

It answers both of your requested comparisons. On tuned τ: the best pre-specified multiplier is 0.75× in 2 configurations, 1.00× in 4 and 1.25× in 6 — again never outside ±25% of the formula value — and two boundary configurations swept more widely land at 1.5× and 0.75×, still inside the same band. Tuning within the band buys a further 0.011 weighted OOD NLL on average. Across both studies that is 21 configurations in which the empirically selected τ never leaves a 0.75×–1.5× window around the formula, which is the behaviour (iv) predicts: the scaling form transfers, the constant does not.

On alternative non-Cosh schedules at matched τ: the deformation-matched exponential is statistically indistinguishable from Cosh (+0.0007 NLL, exact sign-flip p = 0.836). What survives both ablations is the allocation axis — pre-specified 1.25× Cosh and the matched exponential improve weighted OOD NLL over Geo by 0.0121 and 0.0106, favouring the non-geometric allocation in 10/12 and 9/12 configurations, so the historical gains are not an implicit base/range change.

We therefore withdraw the stronger reading that c = 1 is the optimum. What the evidence supports is an allocation axis, and a τ rule that selects the right basin in every configuration tested without any search. The revision will state it that way.

**3. Base, head dimension, and lineage.** The exact-range factorial above directly crosses bases 500K/1M and head dimensions 32/64/128 under the same pinned-range construction. Its cross-configuration direction therefore shows that the allocation effect is not confined to base 500K or d_head = 64. This is a 50.9M mechanistic control; scale is answered independently in (5).

**4. Allocation shape versus parameterization and optimization effort.** This is the confound you identified most sharply, and we ran the experiment you specified: keep the positional operator unchanged and vary only fixed frequency schedules — geometric, EVQ, and several alternative analytic allocations. We did that at three levels of control, each removing one more confound.

Level 1 removes learned capacity and tuning budget: same operator, same protocol, every arm a fixed zero-parameter analytic schedule, so parameter count and optimization effort are identical by construction. Three seeds, mean tail NLL, EVQ-Cosh minus Paper-Geo: −0.256/−0.305/−0.223/−0.238 at 1K/2K/4K/8K, all three seeds agreeing. Span-matched uniform and RMS-deformation-matched power and exponential arms are included; EVQ also beats the uniform one.

Level 2 removes the reference-grid and deviation-magnitude questions: all arms use the native Std-RoPE endpoint grid rather than our midpoint convention, share the same span, and every non-Geo arm shares the same RMS deformation from native Geo (0.2557), so no arm is simply further from geometric. Native EVQ minus native Std-RoPE is −0.113/−0.149/−0.207/−0.190/−0.099 NLL at 512/1K/2K/4K/8K, with seed-level paired 95% intervals [−0.200,−0.026], [−0.183,−0.115], [−0.241,−0.174], [−0.260,−0.121], [−0.141,−0.058], and all three seeds agreeing at every length.

Level 3 removes any implicit base/range change: the exact-range factorial of (2).

Together these say what you asked whether the original comparison could say: with operator, protocol, parameter count, tuning budget, reference grid, deviation magnitude and spectral range all fixed, changing only interior allocation still changes trained NLL. Allocation is a separately identifiable design axis. Consistent with (2), it does not say Cosh is optimal — at Levels 1 and 2 the matched exponential beats it at several lengths, and an attention-derived two-band schedule beats it by 0.116 and 0.211 NLL at 4K and 8K.
