Thank you for separating the exact statement from the finite-τ operating regime. You identified three confounds — allocation shape against parameterization, against optimization effort, and against the operating-point rule — and we built experiments that take them apart one at a time. We answer your five requests in order.

**1. The approximation chain and finite τ.** You are right that the small-τ analysis does not determine a finite optimum, and that the submission let the shape rationale and the operating-point rationale sit too close together. We now separate four links: (i) Cosh is the stationary density of \(C_{\rm app}\) — exact given that surrogate and nothing more; (ii) the quadratic surrogate, the discrete grid and the pure-tether branch are modeling choices, none forced; (iii) the analysis yields the *scaling form* \(\tau \propto d_{\rm eff}/\sqrt{L_{\rm train}}\); (iv) the proportionality constant is not supplied by the analysis and is set to one as a zero-search operating convention.

Link (iii)/(iv) is where your \(\tau \approx 4\) observation lands. Writing the local stationarity condition for the deployment objective gives \(\theta^*=[-g/h]_+\), where \(g\) and \(h\) depend on the relative-distance structure of the data, model and head feature usage, the base, \(K\), the endpoint/span convention, deployment lengths and their weighting, and the objective. The expansion fixes how \(\theta^*\) scales with \(d_{\rm eff}\) and \(L_{\rm train}\); it does not evaluate those functionals, so the best multiplier may move with the configuration while the scaling form remains stable.

**2. Independently tuned \(\tau\) and matched non-Cosh schedules.** Two studies test that distinction directly.

The main study is a 99-run sweep over nine configurations — \(L_{\rm train}\in\{256,512,1024\}\) crossed with \(d_{\rm head}\in\{32,64,128\}\), five \(\tau\) values per configuration at pilot, then three seeds on the retained arms. Against midpoint-Geo the formula wins 7/9 configuration means and 18/27 paired runs, with mean weighted extrapolation NLL improvement of 0.0133. Against the pilot-selected neighboring \(\tau\), evaluated only on the two seeds not used in selection, the formula wins 3/9; importantly, the selected neighbor lies between 0.75× and 1.5× of the formula value in all nine configurations. The rule is therefore not a per-configuration point optimum, but it consistently places the operating point in the relevant basin.

The exact-range factorial you requested extends this under a stricter control: three seeds over two bases (500K/1M), two training lengths (256/1024) and three head dimensions (32/64/128), giving 12 structural configurations in which every schedule shares the same highest sampled frequency, lowest sampled frequency and log span. The spectral range is identical by construction and only interior spacing differs.

On tuned \(\tau\), the best pre-specified multiplier is 0.75× in 2 configurations, 1.00× in 4 and 1.25× in 6; the two more widely swept boundary configurations land at 1.5× and 0.75×. Across both studies, the empirically selected \(\tau\) never leaves a 0.75×–1.5× window around the formula value. On alternative schedules, a deformation-matched exponential is statistically indistinguishable from Cosh (+0.0007 NLL, exact sign-flip \(p=0.836\)). What survives both ablations is the allocation axis: pre-specified 1.25× Cosh and the matched exponential improve weighted OOD NLL over Geo by 0.0121 and 0.0106, favoring non-geometric allocation in 10/12 and 9/12 configurations.

The supported conclusion is therefore positive and narrower than point optimality: \(c=1\) is a zero-search basin default, while the allocation axis remains identifiable across independently tuned Cosh and matched non-Cosh schedules. The revision will state it that way.

**3. Base, head dimension, and lineage.** The exact-range factorial above directly crosses bases 500K/1M and head dimensions 32/64/128 under the same pinned-range construction. Its cross-configuration direction shows that the allocation effect is not confined to base 500K or \(d_{\rm head}=64\). This is a 50.9M mechanistic control; scale is answered independently in (5).

**4. Allocation shape versus parameterization and optimization effort.** This is the confound you identified most sharply, and we ran the experiment you specified: keep the positional operator unchanged and vary only fixed frequency schedules — geometric, EVQ and several alternative analytic allocations. We did this at three levels of control, each removing one more confound.

Level 1 removes learned capacity and tuning budget: same operator, same protocol, every arm a fixed zero-parameter analytic schedule, so parameter count and optimization effort are identical by construction. Across three seeds, EVQ-Cosh minus Paper-Geo mean tail NLL is −0.256/−0.305/−0.223/−0.238 at 1K/2K/4K/8K, with all three seeds agreeing. Span-matched uniform and RMS-deformation-matched power and exponential arms are included; EVQ also beats the uniform arm.

Level 2 additionally removes the reference-grid and deviation-magnitude questions: all arms use the native Std-RoPE endpoint grid, share the same span, and every non-Geo arm has the same RMS deformation from native Geo (0.2557), so no arm is simply farther from geometric. Native EVQ minus native Std-RoPE is −0.113/−0.149/−0.207/−0.190/−0.099 NLL at 512/1K/2K/4K/8K, with seed-level paired 95% intervals [−0.200,−0.026], [−0.183,−0.115], [−0.241,−0.174], [−0.260,−0.121], [−0.141,−0.058], and all three seeds agreeing at every length.

Level 3 additionally removes any implicit base/range change: the exact-range factorial of (2).

Together these provide the direct attribution test requested in the review: with operator, protocol, parameter count, tuning budget, reference grid, deviation magnitude and spectral range all fixed, changing only the interior allocation still changes trained NLL. Allocation is a separately identifiable design axis. Alternative analytic schedules can be competitive at some lengths, so we do not claim universal empirical optimality of Cosh; its role is the closed-form inverse-CDF operating point derived from the stated surrogate.

On the learned-frequency row that prompted your question, the implemented comparator is a layer-shared learnable inverse-frequency baseline with 32 trainable parameters, evaluated under the same 128→8K protocol. It received a dedicated positional learning-rate sweep at 10× and 100×, with the better result reported (455.3 versus 477.7 PPL@8K, seed 42). The revision will label the implemented comparator precisely and report its tuning budget. More importantly, our allocation-shape attribution no longer relies on this learned-frequency row; it rests entirely on the fixed zero-parameter analytic-schedule controls above.

**5. A larger pre-specified run.** For scale, we trained OLMo-2 (1,484,916,736 parameters, \(d_{\rm head}=128\), base 500K) from the public step-0 initialization for 1,000 steps at global batch 512 and exactly 2,097,152,000 counted tokens, under a protocol specified in advance and sharing the pinned recipe, data-order prefix and evaluation rows with the released geometric checkpoint at the same milestone.

Geo/EVQ PPL on the same 128 document-disjoint PG-19 documents is 161.19/167.45 at 4K, 163.88/156.87 at 8K and 182.73/159.64 at 16K. Paired document-level bootstrap 95% intervals exclude zero at every length: the EVQ−Geo NLL delta is +0.0381 [+0.0332,+0.0428] at 4K, −0.0437 [−0.0494,−0.0380] at 8K and −0.1351 [−0.1420,−0.1281] at 16K, with 122/128 and 126/128 documents favoring EVQ at 8K/16K. These intervals measure held-out document sampling, not training-seed uncertainty.

The 4K regression is the in-window cost of reallocating a finite channel budget toward long range, and the same ordering reappears in the 1.485B and 8B adaptation studies. We call this a same-initialization, same-recipe comparison: the released geometric checkpoint came from the upstream distributed trainer and our EVQ branch from a single-GPU loop on the same recipe. It is one trajectory rather than a multi-seed estimate.
